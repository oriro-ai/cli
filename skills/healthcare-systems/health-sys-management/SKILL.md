# Healthcare Management

## Hospital operations

### Hospital structure

**Governing body (board):** Fiduciary responsibility. Sets strategic direction. Quality oversight.
**Medical staff:** Physicians credentialed to practice in hospital. Medical staff bylaws govern privileging.
**Administration:** CEO, CFO, CMO (Chief Medical Officer), CNO (Chief Nursing Officer), COO.
**Service lines:** Organized around clinical programs (cardiac, oncology, orthopaedics, women's health).

### Key hospital metrics

**Occupancy rate:** Occupied beds / Licensed beds. Target 80-85% (below = under-utilized; above = constrained).
**Average length of stay (ALOS):** How long patients stay. Shorter is generally better (cost and outcomes).
**Readmission rate:** % of patients readmitted within 30 days. CMS penalizes high readmission rates.
**Patient experience (HCAHPS):** Survey scores affect CMS reimbursement.
**Mortality and complication rates:** Risk-adjusted. Compared to benchmarks.
**Operating margin:** Revenue - Expenses / Revenue. US hospital median: ~3-4% (thin margins).

### Revenue cycle

**Chargemaster:** Hospital's list price for every service (often 3-5× actual payment). Starting point for negotiations.
**Payer mix:** Proportion of revenue from different payers (commercial, Medicare, Medicaid, self-pay).
Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement often below cost. Commercial payer subsidizes.
**DRG (Diagnosis-Related Group):** Medicare pays fixed amount per admission based on diagnosis, not actual costs. Incentivizes efficiency.
**Value-based care:** Movement from fee-for-service (paid per procedure) to payments tied to outcomes (ACOs, bundled payments).

## Quality improvement

### Major quality frameworks

**IHI Triple Aim / Quadruple Aim:**
Improve population health + Enhance patient experience + Reduce per capita cost + (4th aim) Support care team wellbeing.

**PDSA cycles:** Plan-Do-Study-Act. Rapid cycle improvement.
**Lean:** Toyota Production System applied to healthcare. Eliminate waste. Value stream mapping.
**Six Sigma:** Reduce variation and defects. DMAIC methodology.
**High reliability organizations (HROs):** Preoccupation with failure. Resistance to oversimplification. Sensitivity to operations. Commitment to resilience. Deference to expertise.

### Patient safety

**Institute of Medicine "To Err is Human" (1999):** Landmark report. 44,000-98,000 deaths/year from medical errors.
**Never events:** Serious, preventable events. Wrong-site surgery, retained foreign object, patient falls with injury, pressure ulcers, healthcare-acquired infections.
**Root cause analysis (RCA):** After adverse events. Find system causes, not just individual blame.
**Just culture:** Distinguish human error, at-risk behavior, reckless behavior. Learn from near-misses. Report without fear.
**Patient safety bundles:** Evidence-based interventions packaged together. Central line bundle eliminates CLABSI.

## Regulatory environment

**Joint Commission (TJC):** Accreditation for hospitals. Standards for safety, quality, care management.
**CMS Conditions of Participation:** Federal requirements for Medicare/Medicaid participation.
**HIPAA:** Privacy and security of patient health information.
**EMTALA:** Emergency Medical Treatment and Active Labor Act. Must evaluate/stabilize emergencies regardless of ability to pay.
**Stark Law/Anti-Kickback:** Prohibit financial arrangements that could influence medical decisions.
