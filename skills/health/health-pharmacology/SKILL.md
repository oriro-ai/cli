# Medication Literacy

## How drugs work — basic mechanisms

**Agonist:** Activates a receptor (same effect as natural substance). Morphine activates opioid receptors.
**Antagonist:** Blocks a receptor. Beta-blockers block beta-adrenergic receptors.
**Enzyme inhibitor:** Blocks enzyme activity. ACE inhibitors block angiotensin-converting enzyme.
**Transport blocker:** Prevents reuptake. SSRIs block serotonin reuptake pump.

## Major drug classes

### Cardiovascular

**ACE inhibitors** (-pril: lisinopril, enalapril): Block angiotensin II production. Lower BP, protect kidneys. SE: dry cough (in 15%).
**ARBs** (-sartan: losartan, valsartan): Block angiotensin II receptor. Same effect, no cough.
**Beta-blockers** (-olol: metoprolol, carvedilol, atenolol): Block beta-adrenergic receptors. Lower HR and BP. Don't stop abruptly.
**Calcium channel blockers** (amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil): Relax blood vessels/heart muscle.
**Statins** (-statin: atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin): Inhibit HMG-CoA reductase. Lower LDL.
**Diuretics** (hydrochlorothiazide, furosemide, spironolactone): Increase urine output, lower BP. Monitor potassium.

### Pain and anti-inflammatory

**NSAIDs** (ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac): Block COX enzymes, reduce prostaglandins. Effective for pain/inflammation. GI, cardiovascular, kidney risks with long-term use.
**Acetaminophen/Paracetamol (Tylenol):** Reduces pain and fever. NOT anti-inflammatory. Safe at correct dose; liver toxic in overdose. Max 3g/day (2g if liver disease, alcohol use).
**Opioids** (morphine, oxycodone, hydrocodone, tramadol): Activate opioid receptors. Powerful pain relief. High dependence/abuse potential. Respiratory depression in overdose.
**Gabapentinoids** (gabapentin, pregabalin): Modulate calcium channels. Neuropathic pain, anxiety, seizures.
**Corticosteroids** (prednisone, methylprednisolone, dexamethasone): Potent anti-inflammatory. Short-term: effective. Long-term: significant side effects (immunosuppression, osteoporosis, diabetes, adrenal suppression — taper slowly, never stop abruptly).

### Antibiotics

**Penicillins** (amoxicillin, ampicillin): Beta-lactam. Gram-positive coverage. Allergic reactions possible.
**Cephalosporins** (cephalexin, ceftriaxone): Beta-lactam. Broader coverage. Partial cross-reactivity with penicillin.
**Macrolides** (azithromycin, clarithromycin): Atypical organisms, respiratory infections. QT prolongation risk.
**Fluoroquinolones** (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin): Broad spectrum. Tendon rupture risk. Reserve for resistant organisms.
**Tetracyclines** (doxycycline): Broad spectrum. Avoid with food/dairy/antacids. Not for children < 8 or pregnancy.
**Key rule:** Complete the full course. Stopping early contributes to antibiotic resistance.

### Mental health

**SSRIs** (fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram): First-line depression/anxiety. 4-6 weeks for effect.
**SNRIs** (venlafaxine, duloxetine): Also affect norepinephrine. Useful for pain and anxiety.
**Benzodiazepines** (alprazolam, lorazepam, diazepam): Short-term anxiety only. Dependence develops quickly. Dangerous with alcohol or opioids — respiratory depression.
**Antipsychotics** (quetiapine, olanzapine, risperidone, aripiprazole): Schizophrenia, bipolar, augmentation.

### Diabetes

**Metformin:** First-line Type 2. Reduces hepatic glucose output. GI side effects — take with food. Pause before iodinated contrast procedures.
**GLP-1 agonists** (semaglutide/Ozempic/Wegovy, liraglutide): Injectable or oral. Weight loss + glucose. GI side effects.
**SGLT-2 inhibitors** (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin): Kidney glucose excretion. CV and renal protection.
**Insulins:** Basal (glargine, detemir — 24hr), Bolus (aspart, lispro — rapid-acting with meals), Mixed.

## Drug interactions — the most dangerous combinations

**Warfarin + NSAIDs/aspirin:** Bleeding risk (double anticoagulation effect).
**MAOIs + SSRIs/SNRIs:** Serotonin syndrome — potentially fatal.
**ACE inhibitors/ARBs + potassium-sparing diuretics:** Dangerous hyperkalemia.
**Statins + clarithromycin/erythromycin:** Statin toxicity — muscle damage.
**Metformin + iodinated contrast:** Lactic acidosis risk — hold metformin 48hrs.
**Benzodiazepines + opioids:** Respiratory depression — potentially fatal.
**Fluoroquinolones + antacids:** Dramatically reduced absorption.

## Safe medication practices

**5 Rights:** Right patient, right drug, right dose, right route, right time.
**Tell every provider AND pharmacist** about ALL medications (prescriptions, OTC, supplements, herbals).
**Never share prescription medications.**
**Store properly:** Room temperature away from heat/light unless refrigerated; away from children.
**Dispose safely:** Drug take-back programs or mix with coffee grounds/dirt in sealed bag — don't flush most medications (hormones: do flush if no take-back available).
