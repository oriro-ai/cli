# Aging and Elder Care

## Normal physiological changes with aging

**Cardiovascular:** Decreased maximum heart rate, reduced cardiac reserve, increased vascular stiffness, BP tends to rise.
**Musculoskeletal:** Sarcopenia (muscle loss) — ~1-2% per year after 50. Bone density decreases. Joint changes.
**Cognitive:** Normal: slower processing speed, slight memory lag (tip of tongue), easier fatigue. NOT normal: getting lost in familiar places, forgetting recent events repeatedly, personality changes.
**Sensory:** Presbyopia (reading glasses needed), presbycusis (high-frequency hearing loss), reduced taste/smell.
**Sleep:** Earlier sleep phase, more fragmented, less deep sleep. Total hours similar but more interrupted.
**Immune:** Immunosenescence — slower immune response, reduced vaccine efficacy, higher infection risk.
**Renal/Hepatic:** Reduced kidney and liver function → drugs clear more slowly → dose adjustments needed.

## Polypharmacy

Using 5+ medications simultaneously (common in adults 65+; average is 8+).
**Problems:** Drug interactions, falls risk, adverse effects, cognitive effects, adherence challenges.
**Beers Criteria** (AGS): List of medications potentially inappropriate for older adults:

- Benzodiazepines: fall/cognitive risk
- First-generation antihistamines (diphenhydramine/Benadryl): anticholinergic, confusion, falls
- Long-acting sulfonylureas (glipizide extended release): hypoglycemia
- NSAIDs (long-term): GI bleeding, renal impairment, BP effects
- Muscle relaxants (cyclobenzaprine): sedation, falls
  **Medication review:** Ask doctor annually to review all medications for continued necessity.

## Fall prevention — most important safety intervention

Falls cause 36 million injuries/year in US older adults. 32,000 deaths annually.
**Risk factors:** Prior fall, muscle weakness, balance problems, home hazards, polypharmacy, vision problems.
**Prevention:**

- Exercise: Tai chi, balance training, strength training all proven to reduce falls
- Medication review: Reduce fall-risk medications
- Home modification: Remove throw rugs, add grab bars, improve lighting
- Vision check annually: Poor vision ↑ fall risk significantly
- Vitamin D: 800-1000 IU/day reduces falls in deficient older adults

## Cognitive health

**Normal aging vs. MCI vs. Dementia:**
Normal: Occasional word-finding, slower recall — doesn't interfere with daily function.
MCI (Mild Cognitive Impairment): Noticeable cognitive change beyond normal, but still independent.
Dementia: Cognitive decline that impairs daily functioning.

**Alzheimer's warning signs (Alzheimer's Association):**
Memory loss disrupting daily life, challenges with planning, familiar tasks become difficult, confusion with time/place, new visual problems, word-finding problems, misplacing items unable to retrace, poor judgment, withdrawal, personality/mood changes.

**Risk reduction (evidence-based):**
Physical exercise (strongest evidence), cognitively stimulating activities, social engagement, treating hearing loss (significant risk modifier), controlling vascular risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, cholesterol), sleep quality.

**Mediterranean diet / MIND diet:** Associated with lower dementia risk in observational studies.

## Caregiver guidance

Caregiver burden is real — 40% of Alzheimer's caregivers develop depression.
**Resources:** Alzheimer's Association 24/7 helpline: 800-272-3900
**Respite care:** Temporary relief for caregivers. Adult day programs, in-home respite, short-term residential care.
**Legal planning early:** Power of Attorney (financial), Healthcare Proxy, advance directive — do while person has capacity.

## Advance directives

**Living will:** Documents preferences for end-of-life care.
**Healthcare proxy / DPOA for healthcare:** Designates someone to make medical decisions if unable.
**POLST/MOLST:** (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment) Portable medical order for patients with serious illness.
**DNR/DNI:** Do Not Resuscitate / Do Not Intubate — medical orders, not just expressed wishes.

Complete these documents while healthy. Review periodically. Provide copies to family, physicians, hospital.
