# First Aid

## Call 911 first for any life-threatening situation

**Call 911 (or local emergency number) immediately for:**

- Unconsciousness or unresponsiveness
- Not breathing or gasping
- Chest pain or pressure
- Stroke symptoms (face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty)
- Severe bleeding that won't stop
- Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)
- Suspected poisoning or overdose
- Severe burns
- Head, neck, or spinal injury

---

## CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

**Adult CPR (for someone unresponsive and not breathing normally):**

1. **Check scene safety** — is it safe to approach?
2. **Check responsiveness** — tap shoulders, shout "Are you OK?"
3. **Call for help** — Call 911, send someone for AED
4. **Start compressions:**
   - Place heel of hand on center of chest (lower half of breastbone)
   - Interlock hands, arms straight
   - Compress at least 2 inches deep
   - Rate: 100-120 compressions per minute (beat of "Stayin' Alive")
   - Allow full chest recoil between compressions
5. **Rescue breaths (if trained):**
   - 30 compressions : 2 breaths
   - Head tilt-chin lift, pinch nose, seal lips, breathe for 1 second, see chest rise
   - If untrained or uncomfortable: compression-only CPR is still effective
6. **Continue until:** AED arrives, trained person takes over, person shows signs of life, or you're too exhausted

**Child CPR (1-8 years):** Same technique, 2 fingers or one hand for compressions, press down about 2 inches.
**Infant CPR (< 1 year):** 2 fingers on breastbone, just below nipple line; press down 1.5 inches; gentler breaths.

## AED (Automated External Defibrillator)

AEDs are in most public buildings. They talk you through the process.

1. Turn on AED (open lid or press button)
2. Follow voice prompts
3. Attach pads as shown on diagrams (one upper right, one lower left)
4. Let AED analyze — don't touch the person
5. If shock advised: shout "Clear!", ensure no one touches person, press shock button
6. Resume CPR immediately after shock

## Choking

**Adult/child choking (can cough but making high-pitched noise or can't cough):**

1. Encourage forceful coughing
2. If can't dislodge: give 5 back blows between shoulder blades with heel of hand
3. 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich):
   - Stand behind person
   - Make fist, thumb side against abdomen just above navel
   - Cover with other hand
   - Thrust inward and upward
4. Alternate 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts until object dislodges or person loses consciousness
5. If loses consciousness: lower to ground, call 911, start CPR

**Infant choking:**
5 back blows face-down across your forearm, then
5 chest thrusts (2 fingers on breastbone, same position as CPR)
Never do abdominal thrusts on infants.

**Self-choking:** Make fist against your own abdomen, thrust upward. Or use a chair back.

## Bleeding control

**Direct pressure:**

1. Apply firm, continuous pressure using cloth/gauze
2. Do NOT remove cloth — add more on top if soaked through
3. Press for 10-15 minutes minimum (blood takes time to clot)
4. Elevate injured area above heart level if possible and if no fracture suspected
5. For serious wounds: maintain pressure until medical help arrives

**Tourniquets (only for severe limb bleeding that won't stop):**
Apply 2-3 inches above wound (not over joint).
Tighten until bleeding stops.
Note the time of application (tell medical personnel).
Do NOT remove once applied.

**Wound cleaning (for minor wounds):**

1. Apply pressure to stop bleeding
2. Clean with clean running water for 5+ minutes
3. Apply antibiotic ointment if available
4. Cover with clean bandage
5. Watch for infection signs: increasing redness, swelling, warmth, pus, red streaking, fever

## Burns

### Severity

**First degree:** Redness, no blisters. Superficial (sunburn).
**Second degree:** Blisters, deeper damage.
**Third degree:** White/charred skin, full thickness.

### Treatment

**Minor burns (first/second degree, small area):**

1. Cool under cool (not cold/ice) running water for 10-20 minutes
2. Do NOT use butter, toothpaste, oil, or ice
3. Cover loosely with non-stick sterile dressing
4. Take OTC pain reliever

**Seek emergency care:**

- Burns covering large area
- Third-degree burns (any size)
- Burns on face, hands, feet, genitals, major joints
- Burns from chemicals or electricity
- Inhalation injury (hoarse voice, burned nose hair, soot in mouth)

Chemical burns: flush with large amounts of water for 20+ minutes. Remove contaminated clothing. Don't try to neutralize.

## Fractures and sprains

**RICE for sprains:**

- Rest: stop using the injured area
- Ice: 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off. Never directly on skin (towel between)
- Compression: elastic bandage (not too tight — check circulation)
- Elevation: above heart level to reduce swelling

**Signs that suggest fracture (go to ER):**

- Visible deformity or abnormal position
- Severe swelling and bruising
- Can't bear weight
- Bone visible through skin (open fracture = emergency)
- Numbness or tingling

**Splinting a suspected fracture (if evacuation delayed):**
Splint in the position found — don't try to straighten.
Support above and below the injury.
Check circulation, sensation, movement before and after splinting.

---

## Anaphylaxis (severe allergic reaction)

Signs: hives, throat tightening, difficulty breathing, swelling of lips/tongue/throat, dizziness, nausea/vomiting.

1. Epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) if available — inject into outer thigh; can go through clothing
2. Call 911 immediately even if symptoms improve
3. Lay person down, elevate legs (unless breathing difficulty — then sit up)
4. Second dose of epinephrine if symptoms return after 5-15 minutes and help hasn't arrived
5. Be prepared for CPR

---

## Heat emergencies

**Heat exhaustion:** Heavy sweating, weakness, cool/pale/clammy skin, weak pulse, nausea, fainting.
Treatment: Move to cool place, remove excess clothing, cool with wet cloths, give cool water if conscious.

**Heat stroke:** High body temperature (103°F+), hot/red/dry skin, rapid/strong pulse, confusion.
**This is a medical emergency — call 911 immediately.**
Cool person rapidly: ice packs to neck/armpits/groin, cool water over body, fan.

**Hypothermia:** Shivering, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness, weak pulse.
Remove wet clothing, insulate with dry blankets, warm center of body first.
Severe: call 911.
