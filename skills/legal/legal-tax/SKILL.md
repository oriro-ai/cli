# Tax Fundamentals

## Individual income tax basics

### Tax brackets (2024 — married filing jointly)

| Rate | Income range        |
| ---- | ------------------- |
| 10%  | $0 - $23,200        |
| 12%  | $23,201 - $94,300   |
| 22%  | $94,301 - $201,050  |
| 24%  | $201,051 - $383,900 |
| 32%  | $383,901 - $487,450 |
| 35%  | $487,451 - $731,200 |
| 37%  | Over $731,200       |

**Marginal vs. effective tax rate:**
If you earn $100,000 MFJ: you don't pay 22% on all $100,000.
You pay 10% on first $23,200, 12% on next $71,100, 22% on remaining.
Effective rate is much lower than marginal rate.

### Standard deduction vs. itemizing (2024)

**Standard deduction:**
Single: $14,600
Married filing jointly: $29,200
Head of household: $21,900

Itemize only if itemized deductions exceed standard deduction.
Common itemized deductions: mortgage interest, state taxes (up to $10,000 SALT cap), charitable donations, medical expenses over 7.5% AGI.

Most people (>90%) take the standard deduction.

### Above-the-line deductions (reduce AGI regardless of itemizing)

- Traditional IRA contributions (if eligible)
- HSA contributions
- Student loan interest
- Self-employment taxes (50% deductible)
- Alimony paid (pre-2019 divorce agreements)
- Self-employed health insurance premiums

## W-2 vs. 1099 (employee vs. contractor)

**W-2 employee:**
Employer withholds federal income tax, Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%).
Employer pays matching Social Security and Medicare.
You receive W-2 at year end.

**1099 contractor (self-employed):**
No withholding. You pay BOTH sides of Social Security and Medicare.
**Self-employment tax: 15.3%** (12.4% SS + 2.9% Medicare) on net self-employment income.
Plus regular income tax on top of that.

**Estimated quarterly taxes:**
Self-employed must pay estimated taxes quarterly to avoid penalties.
Due: April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15.
Use Form 1040-ES. Pay via IRS Direct Pay.
Safe harbor: pay 100% of prior year tax (110% if prior AGI > $150,000).

**Self-employed deductions:**

- Home office (dedicated space used regularly and exclusively for business)
- Vehicle (business miles at $0.67/mile in 2024, or actual expense method)
- Business equipment (Section 179 full deduction in year of purchase, or bonus depreciation)
- Health insurance premiums (above-the-line deduction)
- Half of self-employment tax
- Business expenses: software, subscriptions, professional development, supplies

## Small business taxes

### Business structures and taxes

**Sole proprietorship/Single-member LLC:** Report on Schedule C (personal return).
**Partnership/Multi-member LLC:** Form 1065, partners report on K-1.
**S-Corp:** Form 1120-S, shareholders report on K-1.
**C-Corp:** Form 1120, corporate rate 21%, then dividends taxed again.

### Sales tax

No federal sales tax.
State sales tax: 45 states plus DC (no sales tax in: Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire, Oregon).
**Economic nexus:** Must collect sales tax in states where you have significant sales, even without physical presence. Thresholds typically $100,000/year or 200 transactions.
Use TaxJar, Avalara, or similar for automated sales tax collection.

### Payroll taxes

As employer:

- Withhold employee's federal income tax, Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%)
- Pay matching employer Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%)
- Pay federal unemployment (FUTA): 6% on first $7,000 wages (usually 0.6% after state credit)
- Pay state unemployment (SUTA): varies by state
  Deposit payroll taxes on time. IRS penalties are severe for late deposits.

## Retirement account tax benefits

**Traditional IRA:** Deductible contributions (if eligible); tax-deferred growth; ordinary income tax on withdrawals.
**Roth IRA:** After-tax contributions; tax-free growth and withdrawals.
**401(k) traditional:** Pre-tax contributions; tax-deferred growth.
**401(k) Roth:** After-tax contributions; tax-free growth.
**SEP-IRA (self-employed):** Up to 25% of net self-employment income, max $69,000 (2024). Pre-tax.
**Solo 401(k) (self-employed):** Up to $69,000 total (2024). Pre-tax or Roth options.

**Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs):** Must start withdrawals at age 73 from traditional accounts.
Roth IRAs: no RMDs during owner's lifetime.

## Tax planning basics

**Tax year:** January 1 - December 31 for individuals.
**Filing deadline:** April 15 (extension to October 15 if needed — but taxes owed due April 15).

**Before year-end moves:**

- Max retirement contributions if possible
- Harvest tax losses (sell investments at a loss to offset gains)
- Bunch charitable deductions to exceed standard deduction in alternating years
- Accelerate deductible expenses before year-end if in high bracket this year
- Defer income to January if expecting lower bracket next year

**Capital gains:**
Short-term (assets held < 1 year): taxed as ordinary income.
Long-term (assets held > 1 year): preferential rates: 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income.

**Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction (Section 199A):**
Pass-through businesses can deduct up to 20% of qualified business income.
Significant limitations for service businesses above income thresholds.
Consult a CPA to optimize.
