# Human Resources Management

## Hiring process

**Job description:** Responsibilities, required qualifications, preferred qualifications. Be specific — vague JDs attract wrong candidates and invite discrimination claims.
**Sourcing:** Employee referrals (highest quality, fastest), LinkedIn, job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn, Glassdoor), specialized boards (Stack Overflow for devs, AngelList for startup roles).
**Structured interviews:** Same questions for all candidates. Reduces bias, improves prediction of performance.
**Behavioral questions (STAR format):** Situation, Task, Action, Result. Predicts past behavior → future behavior.
Example: "Tell me about a time you had to make a decision with incomplete information."
**Reference checks:** Call references — don't just email. Ask: "Would you rehire this person?" Most revealing question.
**Offer:** Verbal offer first. Confirm enthusiasm. Then written offer with full details.

## Onboarding

First 90 days critical for retention and time-to-productivity.
**Week 1 goals:** Access to all tools/systems, meet team, understand role, culture immersion, quick win opportunity.
**30-60-90 day plan:** Clear expectations for what success looks like at each milestone.
**Buddy system:** Assign experienced peer to answer informal questions.
Cost of poor onboarding: 20% of new hires leave within 45 days. Replacement cost: 50-200% of annual salary.

## Performance management

**OKRs:** Objectives (qualitative, aspirational) + Key Results (quantitative, measurable). Set quarterly. 60-70% achievement = well-calibrated (if hitting 100%, goals weren't ambitious enough).
**Regular 1:1s:** Weekly or biweekly. Manager's agenda: listen. Employee's agenda: share updates, blockers, growth. Most important management ritual.
**Annual review:** Formal documentation of performance. Should contain zero surprises — ongoing feedback should have covered everything.
**Performance improvement plan (PIP):** Specific, measurable, time-bound goals for underperforming employees. Serious intervention — not just documentation for firing. Give genuine opportunity to improve.

## Compensation

**Market benchmarking:** Use Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Radford, Mercer, Carta for comp data. Update annually.
**Pay bands:** Salary ranges for each role level. Provides structure and equity.
**Equity compensation:** See legal-startup skill for mechanics. For employees: vesting schedule, strike price, tax implications matter.
**Total rewards:** Base + bonus + equity + benefits + flexibility + growth opportunities. Not just salary.
**Pay equity:** Audit annually for gender/race pay gaps. Legal exposure + ethical imperative + retention risk.
**Compensation philosophy:** Decide: target 50th percentile (market rate) or 75th (top of market to attract best talent)? Consistent policy prevents individual negotiation chaos.

## Retention

Gallup: Cost to replace employee = 50% (entry level) to 200% (executive) of annual salary.
**Top retention drivers:** Meaningful work, quality manager, growth opportunities, recognition, compensation fairness, flexibility.
**Stay interviews:** Ask current employees: "What would make you leave?" before they decide to go.
**Exit interviews:** Ask every departing employee why. Aggregate themes. Act on what's within your control.

## Culture

Culture = what people do when no one is watching.
**Defined culture:** Written values, behavioral norms, decision-making principles. Not a poster — observable in everyday decisions.
**Culture fit vs. culture add:** Beware over-indexing on fit — creates homogeneity. Seek people who share core values but bring diverse perspectives.
**Psychological safety:** See organizational psychology skill.
**Recognition:** Regular, specific, public recognition for behaviors that exemplify values. Powerful reinforcement.
