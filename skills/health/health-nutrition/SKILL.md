# Nutrition Science

## The foundation: macronutrients

### Protein

**Function:** Builds and repairs tissue, enzymes, hormones, immune function
**Requirements:**

- Sedentary adults: 0.8g per kg body weight (0.36g/lb)
- Active adults: 1.2-1.6g/kg
- Athletes/muscle building: 1.6-2.2g/kg
- Older adults (65+): 1.0-1.2g/kg (higher to prevent muscle loss)

**Quality sources:**
Complete proteins (all essential amino acids): meat, fish, eggs, dairy, soy, quinoa
Incomplete proteins: most plant foods — combine over the day, not necessarily at every meal

**Signs of inadequate protein:** Muscle loss, slow healing, weakened immunity, hair loss

### Carbohydrates

**Function:** Primary energy source, brain fuel, gut health (fiber)
**Requirements:** No strict minimum, but 45-65% of calories is typical recommendation

**Quality matters:**

- **Complex carbs (prefer):** Vegetables, legumes, whole grains — fiber, slower digestion
- **Simple refined carbs (limit):** White bread, white rice, sugar, processed foods
- **Simple natural carbs (moderate):** Fruit, dairy — packaged with nutrients

**Glycemic index:** How quickly foods raise blood sugar
High GI foods cause blood sugar spikes and crashes.
Lower GI foods provide steady energy.
Eating carbs with protein, fat, or fiber slows absorption.

**Fiber:**

- Soluble fiber: oats, beans, fruits — lowers LDL, feeds gut bacteria
- Insoluble fiber: whole grains, vegetables — digestive health
- Recommended: 25g/day (women), 38g/day (men)

### Fats

**Function:** Cell membranes, hormone production, fat-soluble vitamins (A,D,E,K), brain

**Types:**

- **Saturated fat:** Animal products, coconut oil — limit to <10% calories; raises LDL
- **Monounsaturated:** Olive oil, avocado, nuts — beneficial for heart health
- **Polyunsaturated:** Fish, walnuts, flaxseed
  - Omega-3 (EPA, DHA, ALA): anti-inflammatory, brain health, heart protective
  - Omega-6 (linoleic): seed oils — essential but most people get too much
- **Trans fat:** Partially hydrogenated oils — AVOID completely; raises LDL, lowers HDL

**Cholesterol and diet:** Dietary cholesterol has less impact on blood cholesterol
than saturated and trans fats for most people. Eggs are no longer restricted
in most guidelines.

## Micronutrients — most commonly deficient

### Vitamin D

**Function:** Bone health, immune function, mood, cancer prevention
**Deficiency signs:** Fatigue, bone pain, frequent illness, depression
**RDA:** 600-800 IU (most people need more — up to 2,000 IU common in northern latitudes)
**Sources:** Sun exposure, fatty fish, egg yolks, fortified foods
**Supplement note:** D3 (cholecalciferol) is more effective than D2

### Iron

**Function:** Red blood cell production, oxygen transport, energy
**RDA:** 8mg/day (men), 18mg/day (premenopausal women), 27mg (pregnancy)
**Deficiency:** Most common nutritional deficiency globally. Fatigue, pale skin, shortness of breath
**Sources:** Red meat (heme iron, best absorbed), legumes, dark leafy greens (non-heme)
**Absorption tip:** Eat vitamin C with plant iron sources; avoid coffee/tea with meals

### Vitamin B12

**Function:** Nerve function, DNA synthesis, red blood cells
**RDA:** 2.4 mcg/day (adequate in most omnivores)
**At-risk groups:** Vegans/vegetarians (no animal products), adults 50+ (absorption declines), metformin users
**Sources:** Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, fortified foods
**Supplement:** Vegans must supplement. Methylcobalamin preferred.

### Magnesium

**Function:** 300+ enzymatic reactions, muscle/nerve function, blood sugar, sleep
**RDA:** 310-420 mg/day
**Deficiency signs:** Muscle cramps, poor sleep, anxiety, constipation, headaches
**Sources:** Dark chocolate, avocado, nuts, legumes, whole grains, leafy greens
**Common depletion causes:** Stress, alcohol, processed food diets, certain medications

### Calcium

**Function:** Bone/teeth strength, muscle contraction, nerve signaling
**RDA:** 1,000 mg/day (adults), 1,200 mg (women 51+, men 71+)
**Sources:** Dairy, fortified plant milks, sardines with bones, tofu, leafy greens
**Absorption note:** Take calcium supplements in 500mg doses max; vitamin D enhances absorption

### Zinc

**Function:** Immune function, wound healing, taste/smell, DNA synthesis
**RDA:** 8mg (women), 11mg (men)
**Deficiency signs:** Frequent illness, poor wound healing, loss of taste/smell
**Sources:** Oysters, beef, pumpkin seeds, legumes
**Note:** Zinc and iron compete for absorption — don't supplement both simultaneously

### Omega-3 Fatty Acids

**RDA:** 1.1-1.6g ALA/day; EPA+DHA 250-500mg/day for heart health
**Sources:** Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel), walnuts, flaxseed, chia seeds
**Supplement:** Fish oil or algae oil if intake is low (algae = vegan source)

## Reading nutrition labels

**Serving size:** Everything else on the label is per serving, not per package.
If you eat 2 servings, double all the numbers.

**% Daily Value:** Based on 2,000 calorie diet.

- 5% DV or less = low
- 20% DV or more = high

**Ingredients list:** Listed in order by weight (most to least).
If sugar appears in the first 3 ingredients, the product is high in sugar.
Watch for sugar aliases: corn syrup, fructose, dextrose, maltose, evaporated cane juice.

**Added sugars:** Different from natural sugars.
Limit to < 25g/day (women), < 36g/day (men) per AHA guidelines.

## Evidence-based dietary patterns

### Mediterranean diet

Consistently strongest evidence for:

- Cardiovascular disease reduction
- Cognitive decline prevention
- Longevity
- Diabetes prevention

Core: vegetables, legumes, whole grains, olive oil, fish, moderate wine.
Limit: red meat, processed foods, added sugars.

### DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension)

Proven to reduce blood pressure 8-14 mmHg.
Core: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, low-fat dairy, lean proteins.
Limit: sodium (<2,300mg/day), saturated fat, sweets.

### Plant-forward diets

Strong evidence for reduced:

- Cardiovascular disease
- Type 2 diabetes
- Colorectal cancer
- BMI and obesity rates

Does not have to be fully vegan — reducing meat frequency, not eliminating.

### What the evidence says about popular diets

**Keto:** Effective short-term weight loss. Difficult to maintain. No long-term superior outcomes vs. other calorie-restricted diets.

**Intermittent fasting:** Works primarily by reducing calorie intake. No metabolic magic. Effective for people who find it easier to skip meals than portion control.

**Low-fat diets:** 1990s approach largely discredited for weight loss. Fat quality matters more than quantity.

**Gluten-free:** Medically necessary for celiac disease (~1% of population). No proven benefit for others; often higher calorie and lower fiber than whole grain alternatives.

## Weight management — what science actually shows

**The only proven mechanism:** Calorie deficit for weight loss; calorie surplus for gain.
1 pound of fat ≈ 3,500 calories.
500 calorie/day deficit ≈ 1 pound/week loss.

**Sustainable deficit:** 500-750 calories/day below maintenance.
Larger deficits cause muscle loss and metabolic adaptation.

**TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure):**
Rough estimate: Body weight (lbs) × 15 = maintenance calories (moderately active).
Use an online TDEE calculator for better precision.

**What makes diets work:**

- Calorie deficit (necessary)
- High protein (preserves muscle, increases satiety)
- High fiber (satiety, gut health)
- Adequate sleep (poor sleep increases hunger hormones)
- Reduced ultra-processed food (very easy to overconsume)
- Consistency over weeks-months, not days

**What the scale doesn't tell you:**
Weight fluctuates 2-5 lbs daily based on water, sodium, glycogen, food weight.
Track 7-day averages, not daily. Take monthly progress photos.

## Hydration

**Water needs:** ~3.7L/day (men), ~2.7L/day (women) total from all sources.
Roughly 8 cups of water for sedentary adults; more with exercise or heat.

**Signs of dehydration:** Dark urine (aim for pale yellow), headache, fatigue, poor concentration.

**Electrolytes during exercise:** For exercise > 60 minutes in heat, replace sodium, potassium.
Sports drinks appropriate; or salt + fruit + water.

**Coffee and tea:** Mild diuretic but net hydrating — count toward fluid intake.

## Food safety

**Temperature danger zone:** 40-140°F (4-60°C) — bacteria multiply rapidly.
Refrigerator: ≤40°F / Freezer: 0°F

**Safe internal temperatures:**

- Poultry: 165°F
- Ground beef: 160°F
- Whole beef/pork/lamb: 145°F (with 3-minute rest)
- Fish: 145°F
- Eggs: 160°F (or until yolk and white are firm)

**FIFO rule:** First In, First Out — use older food before newer.

**Thawing safely:** Refrigerator, cold water (change every 30 min), or microwave (cook immediately). Never on the counter at room temperature.

**Cross-contamination:** Use separate cutting boards for raw meat vs. produce.
Wash hands 20 seconds with soap before and after handling raw proteins.
