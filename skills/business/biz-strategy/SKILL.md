# Business Strategy

## The fundamental strategic question

**Strategy is choosing what NOT to do.**

A strategy that tries to appeal to everyone succeeds for no one.
The core of strategy: make trade-offs. Be the best option for a specific customer
doing a specific job, at the expense of not being the best option for everyone else.

**Three generic strategies (Porter):**

1. **Cost leadership:** Be the lowest cost producer in the industry.
2. **Differentiation:** Offer something customers value that competitors can't match.
3. **Focus:** Serve a narrow market better than generalists can.

Stuck in the middle (trying to be all three) = above-average risk, below-average returns.

## Porter's Five Forces

Measures the competitive intensity and profitability of an industry.

### 1. Threat of New Entrants

High barriers to entry = better for incumbents.
Barriers: economies of scale, brand loyalty, regulatory requirements, high capital requirements, network effects, switching costs.

**Questions:** How easy is it for a new competitor to enter? What would it cost? How long would it take?

### 2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers

Few suppliers with unique inputs = strong supplier power = bad for you.
**Reduce supplier power:** Multiple suppliers, backward integration, commodity inputs.

### 3. Bargaining Power of Buyers

Many choices + low switching costs = high buyer power = price pressure.
**Reduce buyer power:** Differentiation, switching costs, fragmented buyer base, emotional connection.

### 4. Threat of Substitutes

Alternative ways customers can meet the same need (outside your industry).
Uber is a substitute for car ownership. Zoom is a substitute for business travel.

### 5. Industry Rivalry

Number of competitors, growth rate, exit barriers, differentiation.
Intense rivalry → price wars → reduced margins.

**Most attractive industry:** High barriers to entry, low supplier power, low buyer power, few substitutes, moderate rivalry.

## Competitive advantage

### Types of sustainable advantages

**Cost advantage:** You produce at lower cost than competitors.
Sources: scale, superior processes, proprietary technology, location, access to cheaper inputs.
Risk: Someone always finds a cheaper way.

**Differentiation advantage:** Customers prefer your product and pay more for it.
Sources: brand, quality, features, service, relationships, technology.
Risk: Differentiation erodes as competitors copy.

**Network effects:** Value increases as more people use it (Facebook, Visa, WhatsApp).
Sources: direct (users connect to users), indirect (more users attract developers/partners).
Most durable advantage: very hard to replicate.

**Switching costs:** Customers stay because leaving is expensive or painful.
Sources: data lock-in, training costs, integration costs, habit.
Examples: Salesforce CRM, enterprise software, banks.

**Economies of scale:** Unit cost falls as volume increases.
Fixed costs spread over more units; bulk purchasing power; specialization.

### Testing your competitive advantage

Ask: If a well-funded competitor decided to attack your market, what would stop them?
If the answer is "not much," you lack a durable advantage.
If the answer is "5+ years and $100M to replicate our network/brand/technology," you have a moat.

## Business Model Canvas (Osterwalder)

Nine building blocks of a business model:

| Block                      | Questions                                                                                |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Customer Segments**      | Who are you creating value for? Which customers are most important?                      |
| **Value Propositions**     | What problem do you solve? What need do you fulfill? What's better than the alternative? |
| **Channels**               | How do you reach your customers? Through what touchpoints?                               |
| **Customer Relationships** | What type of relationship? Personal? Automated? Community?                               |
| **Revenue Streams**        | What value are customers paying for? How much? How?                                      |
| **Key Resources**          | What assets are required (physical, intellectual, human, financial)?                     |
| **Key Activities**         | What must you do well to deliver your value proposition?                                 |
| **Key Partnerships**       | Who are your key partners and suppliers? What do you get from them?                      |
| **Cost Structure**         | What are the most important costs? Which resources/activities are most expensive?        |

**Use this:** Before building anything, map all nine blocks. Gaps become visible.

## SWOT Analysis

|              | Helpful       | Harmful    |
| ------------ | ------------- | ---------- |
| **Internal** | Strengths     | Weaknesses |
| **External** | Opportunities | Threats    |

**Strengths:** What do you do well? What advantages do you have?
**Weaknesses:** What do you do poorly? What should you avoid?
**Opportunities:** What market trends help you? What gaps exist?
**Threats:** What are competitors doing? What trends hurt you?

**SWOT → TOWS (strategic options):**

- SO (Strengths + Opportunities): Use strengths to exploit opportunities
- WO (Weaknesses + Opportunities): Overcome weaknesses to exploit opportunities
- ST (Strengths + Threats): Use strengths to reduce threat impact
- WT (Weaknesses + Threats): Minimize weaknesses to avoid threats

## Market sizing

### TAM, SAM, SOM

**TAM (Total Addressable Market):** Everyone who could ever buy from you.
**SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** Portion you can realistically reach.
**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** Portion you can realistically capture in 3-5 years.

**Top-down:** Start with industry data, carve out your segment.
**Bottom-up:** Start with number of customers × average revenue per customer.

Bottom-up is more credible with investors.

Example (bottom-up for a local restaurant):

- Local population: 100,000
- % who eat out 2x/week: 30% = 30,000 people
- Within delivery range: 20% = 6,000 people
- Market share target: 5% = 300 customers
- Average order: $25, 2x/month = $50/month per customer
- Monthly revenue: $15,000
- Annual: $180,000

## Pricing strategy

### Cost-plus pricing

Add desired profit margin to cost of production.
Simple. But ignores what customers are willing to pay.
Often leaves money on the table.

### Value-based pricing

Price = value delivered to customer.
Best approach when value is clear and quantifiable.
A product that saves a customer $100,000/year can be priced at $20,000+.

### Competitive pricing

Price relative to competitors.
Necessary when customers can easily compare.
Leads to margin compression unless differentiated.

### Penetration pricing

Start low to gain market share; raise prices later.
Works when switching costs are high and network effects matter.
Risk: customers balk at price increases; attracts price-sensitive customers.

### Premium pricing

Price high to signal quality and status.
Works with genuine differentiation, strong brand, or status signaling.
Apple, Rolex, luxury brands.

### Freemium

Give basic product free; charge for advanced features.
Works when free version drives viral adoption; conversion rate typically 2-5%.
Requires large free user base to be sustainable.

## Strategic planning process

**Annual strategy cycle:**

1. **Environmental scan:** What has changed? New competitors, technology, regulations, customer behavior?
2. **Internal assessment:** What are we good at? Where do we fall short? (SWOT)
3. **Strategic options:** What could we do? What should we focus on?
4. **Strategic choice:** What will we do? What will we NOT do?
5. **OKRs/Goals:** What does success look like? How will we measure it?
6. **Resource allocation:** What gets funded? Who is responsible?
7. **Execution:** Quarterly reviews. Adjust based on results.

**OKRs (Objectives and Key Results):**
Objective: Qualitative, inspiring, directional.
Key Results: 3-5 measurable outcomes that indicate the objective is achieved.

Example:
Objective: "Become the go-to platform for freelance designers."
KR1: 10,000 designer sign-ups by Q4.
KR2: Net Promoter Score > 60.
KR3: 40% of designers return within 30 days of first project.
