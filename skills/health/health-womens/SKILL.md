# Women's Health

## Menstrual health

**Normal cycle:** 21-35 days. Duration 2-7 days. Blood loss 20-80 mL. Some cramping normal.
**Dysmenorrhea (painful periods):** Primary (no underlying cause) or secondary (endometriosis, fibroids).
Treatment: NSAIDs (start 1-2 days before onset), hormonal contraception, heat therapy.

**When to see a doctor:**

- Cycles < 21 or > 35 days consistently
- Bleeding > 7 days or soaking a pad/tampon every 1-2 hours
- Severe cramping disrupting daily life
- Spotting between periods (especially post-menopause)
- Significant recent changes in cycle

**PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome):** Most common endocrine disorder in reproductive-age women. Irregular cycles, excess androgens (acne, hair loss, facial hair), ovarian cysts. Management: Lifestyle (exercise, nutrition), hormonal contraception, metformin for metabolic features, spironolactone for androgen effects.

**Endometriosis:** Uterine tissue growing outside uterus. Severe painful periods, pain with sex, infertility. Often delayed diagnosis (7-10 years average). Treatment: Pain management, hormonal therapy, surgery.

## Contraception options

**Hormonal:**
Combination pill (estrogen + progestin): 91% effective (typical use), 99.7% (perfect). Daily pill. Also regulates periods, reduces cramps, improves acne.
Progestin-only pill (mini-pill): For those who can't tolerate estrogen. Must take at same time daily.
Patch (Xulane): Weekly. Same hormones as combined pill.
Ring (NuvaRing): Monthly vaginal ring. Convenient.
Injectable (Depo-Provera): Every 3 months. May cause irregular bleeding or amenorrhea.
Implant (Nexplanon): 3-year arm implant. 99.9% effective. Most effective reversible option. Can cause irregular bleeding.
Hormonal IUD (Mirena, Liletta, Kyleena, Skyla): 3-8 years. 99.9% effective. Reduces bleeding significantly.

**Non-hormonal:**
Copper IUD (Paragard): 10+ years. 99.9% effective. May increase cramping/bleeding. Also emergency contraception within 5 days.
Condoms: 85% effective (typical use), 98% (perfect). Only method preventing STIs.
Diaphragm/cap with spermicide: Requires fitting, insertion before sex. Less effective (88% typical use).

**Emergency contraception:**
Plan B/levonorgestrel: Within 72 hours (less effective 72-120 hours). 89% effective.
Ella (ulipristal acetate): Within 120 hours. More effective than levonorgestrel, especially near ovulation.
Copper IUD: Within 120 hours. Most effective (> 99%).

## Breast health

**Self-exam:** Know your normal. Report changes (lumps, skin changes, nipple discharge, pain).
**Mammogram guidelines (USPSTF 2024):** Begin at age 40 for average-risk women. Every 2 years.
Higher-risk women (BRCA mutation, strong family history): Earlier and more frequent screening + MRI. Discuss with doctor.

**Dense breast tissue:** Common (40% of women). More mammograms needed. Supplemental ultrasound or MRI often recommended.

## Menopause

**Average age:** 51 (range 45-55). Defined as 12 months without period.
**Perimenopause:** Transition period (2-10 years). Irregular cycles, hot flashes may begin.
**Symptoms:** Hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness, sleep disruption, mood changes, cognitive symptoms, joint pain, decreased libido.
**Menopause Hormone Therapy (MHT/HRT):** Most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms.
Current evidence: Initiated before age 60 or within 10 years of menopause = favorable risk/benefit. Benefits: Symptom relief, bone protection, may reduce cardiovascular risk when started early. Risks: Slight increase in breast cancer risk with combined estrogen+progestin (not estrogen alone for hysterectomy patients).
**Non-hormonal options:** SSRIs/SNRIs (off-label, reduce hot flashes), gabapentin, clonidine, cognitive behavioral therapy.
