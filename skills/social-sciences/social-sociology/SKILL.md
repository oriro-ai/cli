# Sociology

## Core sociological concepts

**Social structure:** Patterned social arrangements that influence human behavior. Institutions, norms, statuses, roles.
**Social stratification:** Hierarchical arrangement of individuals and groups based on socioeconomic status, power, prestige.
**Social mobility:** Movement between positions in stratification hierarchy.
Vertical: Up or down the hierarchy.
Horizontal: Lateral movement without status change.
Intergenerational: Children vs. parents.

## Major theoretical perspectives

### Functionalism (Durkheim, Parsons)

Society as an organism. Each part serves a function for the whole.
Social institutions (family, religion, education, economy) maintain social order.
Crime serves a function: clarifies norms, promotes solidarity.
**Critique:** Conservative bias; ignores conflict and change.

### Conflict theory (Marx, Weber)

Society marked by conflict over scarce resources. Groups compete for power.
Institutions serve the interests of dominant groups.
**Weber:** Multiple dimensions of stratification: class (economic), status (prestige), party (political).
**Critique:** Overemphasizes conflict; neglects consensus and stability.

### Symbolic interactionism (Mead, Goffman)

Focus on micro-level interactions. People create meaning through symbols and shared definitions.
Goffman's dramaturgical model: Social life as theatrical performance. Front stage (public) vs. back stage (private).
**Critique:** Ignores macro structures; how symbols become shared.

## Socialization

Process by which individuals learn the norms, values, and behaviors of their society.
**Agents:** Family (primary), peers, schools, media, religion.
**Primary:** Childhood. Core values, basic social skills, language.
**Secondary:** Adulthood. New roles (college student, worker, parent).
**Resocialization:** Replacing old behaviors with new ones. Total institutions: prisons, military, monasteries.

## Social institutions

**Family:** Organizes reproduction, child-rearing, economic cooperation. Forms vary widely across cultures.
**Education:** Socialization, credentialing, social mobility pathway, reproduces inequality.
**Religion:** Provides meaning, social solidarity (Durkheim), social control, can motivate both liberation and oppression.
**Economy:** Production and distribution of goods. Capitalism, socialism, mixed.
**Government/State:** Monopoly on legitimate use of force within territory (Weber). Law, order, public goods.
**Medicine/Healthcare:** Defines and treats illness. Medicalization: applying medical frameworks to non-medical problems.

## Social inequality

**Race and ethnicity:** Socially constructed categories with real consequences.
Racial disparities in wealth, health, education, criminal justice are documented and persistent.
Structural racism: Policies and institutions that produce racial inequality without racist intent.

**Gender:** Socially constructed. Gender gap in wages, leadership, unpaid labor.
Intersectionality (Crenshaw): Multiple identities intersect and compound disadvantage.

**Class:** Not just income — also wealth, cultural capital, social capital, lifestyle.
Bourdieu: Cultural capital (knowledge, behavior, education) reproduces class across generations.
