# Mindfulness and Meditation

## What mindfulness is

**Definition (Kabat-Zinn):** Paying attention in a particular way — on purpose, in the present moment, and non-judgmentally.

Not about relaxation (though that often results). Not about clearing the mind.
About observing experience without being controlled by it.

## Evidence base

**MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction):** 8-week clinical program. 1,000+ published studies.
Strong evidence for: Anxiety and depression (comparable to antidepressants for recurrence prevention).
Chronic pain: Reduces pain catastrophizing. Moderate evidence.
Cardiovascular: Reduces cortisol, blood pressure. Moderate evidence.

## Formal practices

### Breath awareness (foundation practice)

Sit comfortably. Close eyes. Notice breath — sensations at nostrils, chest, or belly.
When mind wanders (it will — hundreds of times), gently return to breath.
The returning is the practice. Duration: Start 5 minutes, build to 20+.

### Body scan

Lie down. Systematically move attention through body parts from feet to head.
Notice sensations without judgment. For sleep or relaxation.

### Loving-kindness (Metta)

Silently repeat phrases: "May I be happy. May I be healthy. May I be safe. May I be at ease."
Then extend to others: loved ones → neutral people → difficult people → all beings.

### Walking meditation

Walk slowly. Notice sensation of feet on ground, movement, breath.

## Informal mindfulness

**Single-tasking:** Full attention to one activity.
**Mindful eating:** Notice flavors, textures, sensations without distraction.
**STOP practice:** Stop, Take a breath, Observe, Proceed.

## Neuroscience

**Default Mode Network (DMN):** Active during mind-wandering, self-referential thought.
Mindfulness practice reduces DMN activity = less rumination.
**Amygdala:** Shrinks with sustained mindfulness practice (reduced reactivity to stress).
**Prefrontal cortex:** Strengthened — better attention regulation.
