# Consumer Rights

## Refunds and returns

No federal law requires retailers to accept returns.
Return policies are legally binding contracts — read them before purchasing.
Most retailers have 30-90 day return windows.

**Exceptions where you have rights regardless of store policy:**

- **Defective products:** UCC Article 2 implied warranty of merchantability.
  Goods must work for their ordinary purpose. Defective product = right to refund or replacement.
- **Not as described:** If product differs materially from what was advertised or described.
- **Online purchases (FTC Mail Order Rule):** Order must be shipped when promised.
  If delayed: merchant must notify you and give option to cancel.

## Warranties

**Express warranty:** Written or verbal promise about product performance.
**Implied warranty of merchantability:** Product works for its ordinary purpose. Automatic with product sale.
**Implied warranty of fitness for particular purpose:** Seller knows specific use; product must work for that use.

**Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (US federal):**
If written warranty offered on consumer product over $15:

- Must be "Full" or "Limited" (must state which)
- Must be available before purchase
- Full warranty: free repair or replacement within reasonable time; refund if can't be fixed after reasonable attempts

**Extended warranties / service contracts:**
Optional purchase, not the same as manufacturer warranty.
Often poor value — consider the source, exclusions, and deductibles.

## Credit cards and disputes

### Chargeback rights (Fair Credit Billing Act — FCBA)

For credit card purchases, you can dispute:

- Charges you didn't authorize
- Incorrect amounts
- Charges for goods not received
- Charges for goods that don't match description
- Charges for defective goods (seller won't resolve)

**Dispute process:**

1. Contact merchant first — many resolve quickly to avoid chargeback fees.
2. If merchant won't resolve: contact credit card company within 60 days of statement.
3. Dispute in writing (keep proof).
4. Credit card company investigates. Charge reversed while under investigation.

**NOT eligible for chargeback:**

- You changed your mind after receiving goods
- Subscription you forgot to cancel
- Third-party dispute (you paid someone who didn't deliver to you, but charge was authorized)

### Debit card disputes (Electronic Fund Transfer Act — EFTA)

Fewer protections than credit cards. Time is critical.

- Report within 2 days: max liability $50
- Report within 60 days: max liability $500
- After 60 days: potentially unlimited liability

**This is why financial advisors say use credit cards for purchases, not debit.**

## Debt collection (Fair Debt Collection Practices Act — FDCPA)

**Who it covers:** Third-party debt collectors (collection agencies), not the original creditor.

**What collectors CANNOT do:**

- Call before 8am or after 9pm local time
- Call you at work if you've told them you can't receive calls there
- Use abusive, obscene, or threatening language
- Misrepresent the amount owed
- Threaten legal action they don't intend to take or can't legally take
- Contact you after written request to stop (they can still sue)
- Discuss your debt with third parties (except spouse, attorney, credit bureaus)

**Your rights:**

- Request written verification of the debt (within 30 days of first contact, collector must stop collection until verified)
- Demand they stop contacting you (after this, collector can only confirm receipt of demand or notify you of legal action)
- Sue collector for FDCPA violations — statutory damages up to $1,000 + actual damages + attorney fees

**Statute of limitations on debt:** Varies by state (3-10 years for most consumer debt). After SOL, debt cannot be collected through courts. Making a payment can restart the clock.

## Small claims court

For disputes typically $2,500-$25,000 (limits vary by state).
No attorney required (or allowed in some states). You represent yourself.

**Well-suited for:**

- Security deposit disputes
- Property damage
- Unpaid wages (small amounts)
- Contract disputes
- Contractor didn't complete work

**Process:**

1. Gather evidence: contracts, receipts, photos, communications
2. File complaint with small claims court ($30-100 filing fee)
3. Serve defendant (court usually handles this)
4. Attend hearing (typically 30-60 days after filing)
5. Present case to judge; judge decides

**Judgment vs. collection:** Winning a judgment doesn't guarantee payment.
If other party doesn't pay, you must pursue collection (wage garnishment, bank levy, property liens).

## Identity theft and fraud

**Steps if identity stolen:**

1. Place fraud alert with one of the three credit bureaus (auto-notifies others): Equifax, Experian, TransUnion.
2. Get free credit reports; identify fraudulent accounts.
3. Freeze credit at all three bureaus (most effective prevention).
4. File FTC identity theft report at identitytheft.gov (creates official record, provides recovery plan).
5. File police report (needed for some creditors).
6. Dispute fraudulent accounts with creditors and credit bureaus in writing.

**Credit freeze:** Free to place and lift. Prevents new credit in your name.
Lift temporarily when applying for legitimate credit.
