# Positive Psychology

## The PERMA model (Seligman)

Evidence-based framework for wellbeing — five pillars:

**P — Positive Emotions:** Happiness, joy, love, gratitude, hope, pride, serenity.
Build: Savor positive experiences (slow down and notice them). Three good things exercise.
Upward spiral: positive emotions broaden attention and build resources (Fredrickson's broaden-and-build).

**E — Engagement (Flow):** Complete absorption in an activity; loss of self-consciousness, time distortion.
Csikszentmihalyi's flow: occurs when challenge and skill are both high and matched.
Build: Identify activities that produce flow. Structure more time for them.

**R — Relationships:** Quality relationships are the strongest predictor of happiness and longevity.
Harvard Study of Adult Development (80+ years): the quality of relationships at 50 predicted health at 80.
Build: Prioritize deep connections over breadth. Active constructive responding when others share good news.

**M — Meaning:** Belonging to and serving something larger than yourself.
Can come from: work, family, community, spirituality, cause.
Build: Identify values. Connect daily activities to larger purpose. Volunteer.

**A — Achievement/Accomplishment:** Pursuit of mastery, competence, achievement — for its own sake.
Build: Set mastery goals (improving your own performance) rather than only performance goals (beating others).

## Signature strengths

VIA Classification of 24 character strengths (Values in Action — free assessment at viacharacter.org):
Creativity, Curiosity, Judgment, Love of Learning, Perspective, Bravery, Perseverance, Honesty, Zest, Love, Kindness, Social Intelligence, Teamwork, Fairness, Leadership, Forgiveness, Humility, Prudence, Self-Regulation, Appreciation of Beauty, Gratitude, Hope, Humor, Spirituality.

**Using strengths:** People using signature strengths in new ways report greater happiness and less depression.
Exercise: Take VIA. Identify top 5. Find new ways to use them daily.

## Gratitude — the most consistently supported intervention

Three good things: Each night, write three things that went well and why they happened.
Gratitude letter: Write and deliver a letter to someone who has never been properly thanked.
Both proven in RCTs to increase happiness and reduce depression, with effects lasting weeks to months.

## Resilience

The ability to recover from adversity; not the absence of struggle.
**Key factors:**

- Social support (most important)
- Sense of meaning and purpose
- Self-efficacy (belief in own ability to cope)
- Emotional regulation skills
- Growth mindset (adversity as learning)

**Post-traumatic growth:** After trauma, many people report positive changes — improved relationships, new possibilities, personal strength, spiritual deepening, appreciation for life. Neither suppresses the pain nor dismisses it.

## Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan)

Three basic psychological needs whose satisfaction predicts wellbeing:
**Autonomy:** Feeling that actions are self-chosen and aligned with values.
**Competence:** Feeling effective; experiencing growth and mastery.
**Relatedness:** Feeling connected to others; belonging.
Environments (work, relationships) that support these needs promote wellbeing, motivation, and mental health.

## Evidence-based wellbeing practices

**Exercise:** Most consistently proven intervention for mood. 30 min moderate aerobic 3× week = significant depression reduction.
**Meditation/mindfulness:** Proven for stress reduction, anxiety, depression maintenance. Even 10 min daily has benefit.
**Acts of kindness:** Performing acts of kindness (even for strangers) increases happiness.
**Spending on experiences > things:** Experiential purchases produce more lasting happiness.
**Spending on others:** Spending money on others produces more happiness than spending on self.
**Time affluence:** Having time (not just money) predicts wellbeing. Value time.
