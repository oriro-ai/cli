# Cross-Cultural Communication

## Hofstede's cultural dimensions

Most widely used framework for understanding cultural differences.

**Power Distance:** Extent to which less powerful accept unequal power distribution.
High PD (Philippines, Malaysia, Arab countries): Hierarchical, authority respected, formality expected.
Low PD (Denmark, Austria, Israel): Flat structures, direct access to bosses, informality.

**Individualism vs. Collectivism:**
Individualist (US, Australia, UK): Personal goals over group, direct communication, "I" identity.
Collectivist (China, Latin America, Middle East): Group harmony, indirect communication, "We" identity, relationship before business.

**Uncertainty Avoidance:** Discomfort with ambiguity.
High UA (Japan, Greece, Portugal): Need for rules, structure, planning.
Low UA (Singapore, Jamaica, Denmark): Comfort with ambiguity, flexible, entrepreneurial.

**Masculinity vs. Femininity:**
Masculine (Japan, Germany, US): Assertiveness, competition, material success, achievement.
Feminine (Sweden, Norway, Netherlands): Caring, quality of life, relationships, cooperation.

**Long-term vs. Short-term Orientation:**
Long-term (China, Japan): Persistence, saving, adapting traditions to modern context.
Short-term (US, UK, many Western): Quick results, respect for tradition, fulfilling obligations.

## Communication styles

### High-context vs. Low-context (Hall)

**High-context:** Meaning in the context — relationships, position, non-verbal. What's not said is important. Japan, China, Arab world, Latin America, Mediterranean.
Communication: Implicit, indirect, relationship-dependent.
"No" may be expressed as "that would be difficult" or silence.

**Low-context:** Meaning in the explicit message. Direct, clear, literal. Germany, Scandinavia, US, Australia.
Communication: Explicit, direct, task-focused.
"Yes" means yes. "No" means no.

**Common misunderstandings:**
High-context person sees low-context as rude, aggressive.
Low-context person sees high-context as evasive, unclear, untrustworthy.

## Business etiquette essentials

### Meeting and greeting

**Japan/Korea:** Bow. Business cards presented with both hands and a slight bow. Receive with both hands. Never write on a card. Study it before putting it away.
**Middle East/South Asia:** Handshake for same gender. Wait for women to initiate.
**France/Southern Europe:** Cheek kisses among acquaintances; handshake in formal/new business.
**Business cards:** In most of Asia, they are a serious matter. Western countries increasingly casual.

### Time orientation

**Monochronic cultures (Germany, Switzerland, US, Scandinavia):** Time is linear. Punctuality is respect. One thing at a time.
**Polychronic cultures (Latin America, Middle East, South Asia, Mediterranean):** Multiple activities simultaneously. Relationships take priority over schedules. "The meeting starts when everyone is there."

### Decision-making across cultures

**US:** Decentralized. Person in the room may be empowered to decide.
**Japan:** Nemawashi — consensus-building before the meeting. The meeting is not where decisions are made.
**France:** Hierarchical. Senior person decides.
**China:** Top-down. Meeting with junior staff first may be insulting.

## Navigating cultural differences

**Suspend judgment.** Different is not wrong.
**Observe before reacting.** Behavior that seems rude may have cultural logic.
**Ask genuinely curious questions.** "How does this typically work here?"
**Adapt your communication style,** not your values.
**Take the other culture's perspective.** What is the intent behind this behavior?
**Never mock cultural differences** — even in private. Word travels.
