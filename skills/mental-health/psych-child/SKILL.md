# Child and Developmental Psychology

## Cognitive development (Piaget)

**Sensorimotor (0-2 years):** Learn through senses and actions. Develop object permanence (things exist when out of sight) at ~8 months. End: beginning of symbolic thought.

**Preoperational (2-7 years):** Language development, symbolic thinking, imaginative play. Egocentric thinking (can't yet take another's perspective). Conservation not yet understood (same amount of water in tall vs. wide glass looks like "more" in tall glass).

**Concrete operational (7-11 years):** Logical thinking about concrete objects. Conservation mastered. Can classify, seriate. Less egocentric.

**Formal operational (12+ years):** Abstract thinking, hypothetical reasoning, systematic logic.

## Social-emotional development (Erikson's stages relevant to childhood)

**Trust vs. Mistrust (0-1):** Is the world safe and reliable? Consistent caregiving builds trust.
**Autonomy vs. Shame (1-3):** Can I do things independently? Support exploration and tolerate messes.
**Initiative vs. Guilt (3-6):** Can I act on my ideas? Encourage imagination and play leadership.
**Industry vs. Inferiority (6-12):** Am I competent? Recognize effort and skill-building.

## Zone of Proximal Development (Vygotsky)

ZPD: The space between what a child can do independently and what they can do with guidance.
**Scaffolding:** Temporary support that allows a child to achieve more than they could alone, then gradually removed as competence develops.
**Implications:** Challenge just beyond current ability (not too easy, not overwhelming). Support during challenge. Fade support as mastery develops.

## Attachment-informed parenting

Secure base: Children need to know they can explore and return to a safe haven.
**Sensitive responsiveness:** Read the child's signals and respond appropriately and promptly.
**Repair:** When disruptions occur (misattunement, conflict), repair is more important than perfection. "Good enough" parenting is sufficient.

## Effective discipline approaches

**Positive reinforcement:** Reward desired behavior (attention, praise, stickers). More effective than punishment long-term.
**Natural consequences:** Allow logical outcomes of behavior (don't want to wear coat → gets cold). Not for dangerous situations.
**Logical consequences:** Related, respectful, reasonable. Didn't put toys away → toys put away for a day.
**Time-out (properly used):** Brief, immediate, calm. Not extended. 1 minute per year of age maximum. Return to connection afterward. For age 2-8.
**Time-in (alternative):** Stay with the child through upset; co-regulate, then problem-solve when calm.

**What doesn't work long-term:** Physical punishment (increases aggression, damages relationship, teaches fear not values), yelling (models dysregulation, creates shame), inconsistency.

## Emotional coaching (Gottman)

Five steps:

1. Become aware of the child's emotion
2. Recognize emotional expression as opportunity for connection
3. Listen with empathy
4. Help label emotions with words
5. Set limits on behavior (not the emotion) while exploring solutions

"It's okay to be angry. It's not okay to hit. Let's figure out what to do when you're angry."

## Screen time guidance (AAP)

Under 18 months: No screen time except video chatting
18-24 months: High-quality programming only, with adult participation
2-5 years: Max 1 hour/day high-quality content; watch together
6+: Consistent limits; ensure screens don't displace sleep, exercise, and in-person connection

**What matters more than time:** Content quality and whether viewing is passive or interactive/co-viewed.

## Child mental health warning signs

**Persistent** (weeks, not days) changes in: sleep, appetite, mood, behavior, school performance, social withdrawal, regression to younger behaviors, physical complaints without medical cause.

**Always take seriously:** Talk of death or wanting to be dead, self-harm, hallucinations, extreme behavior change, school refusal lasting >1 week.
