# Real Estate Law

## Buying a home — the legal process

### Purchase Agreement

Binding contract once signed by both parties.
Key terms to negotiate:

- **Purchase price**
- **Earnest money deposit:** Typically 1-3% of purchase price. Shows good faith. Returned if contingencies not met.
- **Contingencies:** Conditions that must be met; otherwise buyer can exit and recover earnest money.
  - Inspection contingency: right to inspect and negotiate repairs or exit
  - Financing contingency: exit if loan falls through
  - Appraisal contingency: exit if home appraises below purchase price
  - Sale contingency: exit if buyer's current home doesn't sell

**Never waive inspection contingency** unless you can afford to buy as-is regardless.

### Title

Title = legal ownership of property.
**Title search:** Examines public records for claims against the property (liens, judgments, unpaid taxes).
**Title insurance:**

- Owner's policy: protects you from pre-existing title defects
- Lender's policy: required by most lenders
  One-time premium at closing. Worth it.

### Closing

Transfer of ownership.
**Closing disclosure (CD):** Sent 3 business days before closing. Review every line.
**Common closing costs:** 2-5% of loan amount.

- Origination fees
- Appraisal
- Title insurance
- Escrow fees
- Prepaid interest
- Property tax escrow setup

**What you sign at closing:**

- Deed: transfers title to you
- Mortgage/Deed of trust: your promise to repay + security interest in property
- Promissory note: your personal promise to repay the loan

## Selling a home

**Disclosure requirements:** Sellers must disclose known material defects.
Examples: water damage, foundation issues, past flooding, hazardous materials (lead, asbestos), neighbor disputes.

Failure to disclose known defects = fraud. Liability survives closing in most states.

**Capital gains exemption:**
Single: exclude up to $250,000 of gain from taxes.
Married filing jointly: exclude up to $500,000 of gain.
Requirements: owned and lived in as primary residence for 2 of last 5 years.

## Rental — landlord obligations

### What landlords must provide

**Habitability (implied warranty of habitability):** In all states.

- Weatherproof structure
- Working plumbing, heating, electrical
- Freedom from pests
- Safe and sanitary conditions
  Failure = tenant may repair and deduct, withhold rent, or terminate lease (varies by state).

### Security deposits

**Limits:** Many states cap at 1-2 months rent.
**Permitted deductions:** Unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear.
**Normal wear and tear:** Not deductible. Includes: small nail holes, normal carpet wear, paint fading.
**Return deadlines:** Varies by state (14-30 days is common). Must provide itemized statement.
**Penalties for violations:** Many states impose 2-3× the deposit amount for improper withholding.

### Entry notice

Most states require 24-48 hours notice before landlord entry.
Emergency exceptions (fire, flooding, burst pipe).

## Tenant rights

### Anti-retaliation protections

Landlord cannot retaliate by increasing rent, decreasing services, or evicting because tenant:

- Complained about habitability
- Reported to housing authority
- Organized with other tenants
- Exercised legal rights

### Eviction process (required steps — landlord cannot skip)

1. Written notice to tenant (type depends on reason):
   - Pay or Quit: pay overdue rent within X days or vacate
   - Cure or Quit: fix a lease violation or vacate
   - Unconditional Quit: vacate with no option to fix (for serious violations or repeated issues)
2. If tenant doesn't comply: file unlawful detainer (eviction) with court
3. Court hearing: both sides present their case
4. If landlord wins: sheriff serves writ of possession
5. If tenant doesn't vacate: sheriff physically removes tenant

**Illegal eviction:** Landlord CANNOT change locks, remove doors/windows, shut off utilities, or physically remove tenant without a court order. Illegal eviction = significant liability.

### Lease termination

**Month-to-month:** Either party can terminate with proper notice (usually 30-60 days).
**Fixed-term lease:** Cannot typically terminate early without penalty unless:

- Habitability violations
- Active military duty (SCRA protection)
- Domestic violence situations (many states)
- Mutual agreement
