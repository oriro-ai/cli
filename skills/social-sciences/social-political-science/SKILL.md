# Political Science

## Political systems

### Democracy

**Procedural democracy:** Free and fair elections, majority rule, rule of law.
**Liberal democracy:** Adds protection of individual rights, minority rights, independent judiciary.
**Direct democracy:** Citizens vote directly on issues. Ancient Athens. Referenda.
**Representative/indirect democracy:** Elect representatives who make decisions. Most modern democracies.

**Presidential systems (US):** Separate executive and legislative branches. Fixed terms. Divided government possible.
**Parliamentary systems (UK, most of Europe):** Executive drawn from legislature. Government falls if loses majority vote. More disciplined parties.
**Semi-presidential (France):** President + Prime Minister. Both have significant executive power.

### Authoritarianism

**Authoritarian:** Some pluralism tolerated but restricted political competition. Limited accountability.
**Totalitarian:** State seeks to control all aspects of life. Nazi Germany, Stalinist USSR.
**Hybrid regimes:** Electoral authoritarianism — elections occur but not free/fair. Hungary, Turkey.

## Political institutions

### Separation of powers

Legislative: Makes laws. Executive: Implements laws. Judicial: Interprets laws.
**Checks and balances:** Each branch limits the other two.
**Judicial review:** Courts can strike down legislation as unconstitutional. US since Marbury v. Madison (1803).

### Electoral systems

**First-past-the-post (FPTP):** Plurality wins. Simple. Tends toward two-party system (Duverger's Law). US, UK.
**Proportional representation:** Seats allocated by vote share. Multi-party systems. Most of Europe.
**Mixed systems:** Elements of both. Germany (mixed-member proportional).
**Consequences:** FPTP = stable governments, less representation. PR = more representation, sometimes coalition instability.

### Federalism

Division of power between central and subnational governments.
**Federal states:** Power explicitly divided. US, Germany, India, Canada, Australia.
**Unitary states:** Central government supreme; subnational units can be created/changed. UK, France, Japan.
**Advantages of federalism:** Accommodates diversity, policy experimentation, checks central power.
**Disadvantages:** Coordination problems, inequality between regions.

## International relations

**Realism:** States are primary actors, pursue self-interest in anarchic system. Power balancing.
**Liberalism:** Institutions, interdependence, democracy reduce conflict. Liberal peace theory.
**Constructivism:** Interests and identities are socially constructed, not fixed.

**International institutions:** UN, WTO, IMF, NATO — constrain behavior, facilitate cooperation, but states retain sovereignty.
**Collective security:** States agree to treat aggression against one as aggression against all (NATO Article 5).
**Nuclear deterrence:** Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD) — rational actors won't use nuclear weapons if retaliation certain.
