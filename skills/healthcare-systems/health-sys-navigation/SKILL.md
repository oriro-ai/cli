# Healthcare Navigation

## Understanding your health insurance

**Premium:** Monthly cost you pay whether you use healthcare or not.
**Deductible:** Amount you pay out-of-pocket before insurance starts paying.
**Copayment:** Fixed amount for a service ($25 for primary care visit).
**Coinsurance:** Your share (%) after deductible is met (80/20 means insurer pays 80%, you pay 20%).
**Out-of-pocket maximum:** The most you'll ever pay in a plan year. After this, insurance pays 100%.
**In-network vs. out-of-network:** Providers who have contracted rates with your insurer vs. those who haven't. Out-of-network costs significantly more (sometimes all billed charges).

**Calculating your real exposure:** Add deductible + coinsurance until out-of-pocket max. That's your maximum annual risk.

## Prior authorization

Many services require insurer approval before receiving care.
**Common requirements:** Specialty drugs, elective surgeries, MRI/CT scans, specialist referrals (HMO), mental health inpatient.
**Process:** Provider submits request with clinical justification. Insurer reviews.
**Denial:** You can appeal. Request peer-to-peer review between your doctor and insurer's medical director. External review if internal appeal fails.
**Emergency care:** Prior auth not required for true emergencies. Insurer must cover stabilization regardless of network.

## Medical billing

**EOB (Explanation of Benefits):** Not a bill. Summary of what insurer paid and what you owe.
**Billed vs. allowed amount:** Provider bills $500. Insurance's contracted rate: $200. Insurance pays $160 (80% of $200 after deductible met). You owe $40.
**Surprise billing:** Federal No Surprises Act (2022) largely prohibits surprise out-of-network bills for emergency care and certain non-emergency services at in-network facilities.
**Medical debt:** Negotiate! Medical debt is often negotiable. Many hospitals have charity care programs. Ask for itemized bill and dispute errors (30%+ of medical bills contain errors).

## Patient rights

**HIPAA:** Your health information is protected. Providers cannot share without your consent (with exceptions for treatment, payment, operations, public health, law enforcement).
**Right to access your records:** Get your medical records within 30 days of request (HIPAA). Free or nominal charge.
**Informed consent:** Right to understand and consent to treatment. Right to refuse treatment.
**Non-discrimination:** Cannot be discriminated against in healthcare based on race, sex, disability, etc. (ACA, Section 1557).
**Second opinion:** Right to get a second opinion. Insurance often covers it.

## How to get the most from healthcare

**Be prepared for appointments:** Bring list of all medications (including OTC/supplements), bring your questions written down, ask for written instructions.
**The most important question:** "What happens if I do nothing?" Know your options.
**Ask about costs:** "Is this test/procedure necessary? What does it cost? Is there a less expensive alternative?"
**Follow up on abnormal results:** Don't assume "no call = everything fine."
**Preventive care:** Fully covered under ACA (no deductible) — annual exam, recommended screenings, immunizations.
