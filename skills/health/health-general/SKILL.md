# Health Literacy

## How to read a diagnosis

When a doctor gives you a diagnosis, you need to understand:

1. What is the condition?
2. What caused it?
3. How serious is it?
4. What are the treatment options?
5. What happens if left untreated?
6. What lifestyle changes help?

**Questions to always ask your doctor:**

- "Can you explain this in plain language?"
- "What are my options?"
- "What would you recommend for someone in my situation?"
- "What are the risks of each option?"
- "What happens if I choose watchful waiting?"
- "How will we know if the treatment is working?"
- "When should I come back?"
- "Is there anything I should read?"

## Understanding common lab tests

### Complete Blood Count (CBC)

**Red Blood Cells (RBC):** Normal 4.5-5.5 million/μL (men), 4.0-5.0 (women)

- Low = anemia (fatigue, weakness)
- High = polycythemia (blood too thick)

**Hemoglobin:** Normal 13.5-17.5 g/dL (men), 12.0-15.5 (women)

- Low = anemia

**White Blood Cells (WBC):** Normal 4,500-11,000/μL

- High = infection, inflammation, or rarely cancer
- Low = bone marrow problems, autoimmune, some medications

**Platelets:** Normal 150,000-400,000/μL

- Low = bleeding risk
- High = clotting risk

### Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP)

**Glucose:** Normal fasting: 70-99 mg/dL

- 100-125 = prediabetes
- ≥126 = diabetes (if confirmed)

**Creatinine:** Normal 0.7-1.2 mg/dL (men), 0.5-1.0 (women)

- High = kidneys not filtering well

**Sodium:** Normal 136-145 mEq/L
**Potassium:** Normal 3.5-5.0 mEq/L (heart rhythm critical)
**Calcium:** Normal 8.5-10.5 mg/dL

### Lipid Panel (Cholesterol)

**Total cholesterol:** Desirable < 200 mg/dL
**LDL ("bad"):** Optimal < 100 mg/dL; < 70 for high-risk
**HDL ("good"):** Optimal > 60 mg/dL; < 40 is risk factor
**Triglycerides:** Normal < 150 mg/dL; < 100 optimal

### Thyroid (TSH)

**TSH:** Normal 0.4-4.0 mIU/L

- High TSH = underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism: fatigue, weight gain, cold)
- Low TSH = overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism: weight loss, anxiety, heat)

### Liver Function (LFTs)

**ALT/AST:** Normal < 40 U/L

- High = liver inflammation (alcohol, fatty liver, hepatitis, medications)

**Bilirubin:** Normal 0.2-1.2 mg/dL

- High = jaundice, liver disease, or blood cell breakdown

### A1C (Hemoglobin A1C)

Measures average blood sugar over 3 months:

- < 5.7% = normal
- 5.7-6.4% = prediabetes
- ≥ 6.5% = diabetes

## Understanding vital signs

**Blood pressure:**

- Normal: < 120/80 mmHg
- Elevated: 120-129 / < 80
- Stage 1 hypertension: 130-139 / 80-89
- Stage 2 hypertension: ≥ 140 / ≥ 90
- Crisis: > 180 / > 120 (seek emergency care)

**Heart rate:** Normal at rest: 60-100 bpm

- Athletes may have 40-60 bpm (normal for them)
- > 100 at rest repeatedly = tachycardia (evaluate)

**Temperature:**

- Normal: 97-99°F (36.1-37.2°C)
- Fever: ≥ 100.4°F (38°C)
- High fever: ≥ 103°F (39.4°C) — contact doctor
- Dangerous: ≥ 105°F (40.6°C) — emergency

**Oxygen saturation (SpO2):**

- Normal: 95-100%
- Concerning: < 92% — seek medical attention
- Emergency: < 90%

**BMI (Body Mass Index):**
Weight (kg) / Height² (m²)

- Underweight: < 18.5
- Normal: 18.5-24.9
- Overweight: 25-29.9
- Obese: ≥ 30

Note: BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnostic. It does not account for muscle mass, bone density, or fat distribution.

## When to seek emergency care (go to ER or call 911)

**Chest pain or pressure** — especially with shortness of breath, sweating, jaw/arm pain
**Difficulty breathing** — severe shortness of breath, can't speak in full sentences
**Stroke symptoms (BE-FAST):**

- Balance loss suddenly
- Eyes (sudden vision loss or double vision)
- Face drooping
- Arm weakness
- Speech difficulty
- Time to call 911

**Severe bleeding** — not controlled by 10 minutes of direct pressure
**Loss of consciousness** or altered mental status
**Severe allergic reaction** — throat swelling, hives, difficulty breathing
**Seizure** — first time, prolonged (> 5 min), or not returning to normal after
**Severe abdominal pain** — especially sudden onset, worst of life
**Severe headache** — worst headache of your life (possible aneurysm)
**High fever with stiff neck and light sensitivity** (possible meningitis)
**Poisoning or overdose**
**Serious injury** — head trauma, suspected fractures, deep wounds

## Understanding medications

### How to read a prescription label

- **Drug name:** Generic (e.g., "metformin") or brand (e.g., "Glucophage")
- **Dose:** Amount per pill/dose
- **Frequency:** How often (QD=once/day, BID=twice, TID=three times, QID=four times)
- **Route:** How taken (PO=by mouth, SL=under tongue, TOP=topical)
- **Refills:** Number of refills allowed

### Drug interactions — what to tell your doctor

Always tell your doctor and pharmacist:

- All prescription medications
- All OTC medications (including aspirin, ibuprofen, antacids)
- All supplements and vitamins
- Herbal remedies
- Recreational substances
- Allergies to medications

**Common dangerous interactions:**

- Blood thinners (warfarin) + NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) = bleeding risk
- SSRIs + triptans = serotonin syndrome risk
- ACE inhibitors + potassium supplements = dangerous potassium levels
- Statins + certain antibiotics (clarithromycin) = muscle damage risk
- MAOIs + many other drugs = hypertensive crisis

### Medication adherence

- Take medications at the same time daily
- Do not stop prescription medications without telling your doctor
- Finishing antibiotics is critical — stopping early causes resistance
- Some medications require gradual tapering (steroids, antidepressants, beta-blockers)

## Preventive care — recommended screenings by age

### For all adults

- Blood pressure: every 2 years if normal; annually if elevated
- Cholesterol: every 5 years starting at 35 (men) or 45 (women); earlier if risk factors
- Diabetes screening: every 3 years starting at 35

### Women

- Pap smear: every 3 years age 21-65 (or every 5 years with HPV co-test age 30-65)
- Mammogram: annually or every 2 years starting at 40-50 (discuss with doctor)
- Bone density (DEXA): at 65; earlier if risk factors

### Men

- Prostate (PSA): discuss with doctor at 50 (45 if high risk, 40 if very high risk)
- AAA screening (abdominal aortic aneurysm): once at 65-75 if ever smoked

### Colorectal cancer screening (all adults)

- Start at 45 (earlier if family history)
- Options: colonoscopy every 10 years, or annual stool tests

### Vaccinations (adults)

- Flu: annually
- Tdap: once, then Td booster every 10 years
- Shingles (Shingrix): 2 doses at 50+
- Pneumococcal: 65+ or younger with certain conditions
- COVID-19: per current CDC guidance
- RSV: 60+ (discuss with doctor)

## Mental health basics

See mental health skills for detailed guidance.

**Depression screening (PHQ-2):**
Over the past 2 weeks:

1. Little interest or pleasure in doing things?
2. Feeling down, hopeless?

If yes to either: discuss with your doctor.
PHQ-9 is the full 9-question screening tool used clinically.

**Anxiety screening (GAD-2):**

1. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge?
2. Not being able to stop or control worrying?

If yes to either: discuss with your doctor.
