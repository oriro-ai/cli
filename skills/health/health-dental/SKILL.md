# Dental Health

## Oral hygiene fundamentals

**Brushing:** 2 minutes, twice daily. Soft bristle. 45° angle at gumline. Gentle circular strokes. Electric toothbrush consistently outperforms manual in clinical studies.
**Flossing:** Once daily. Under gumline. Between every tooth. Gum disease starts where brushing can't reach. Water flosser (Waterpik) is an effective alternative.
**Fluoride:** Essential for enamel remineralization. Use fluoride toothpaste (1000+ ppm for adults). Most municipal water is fluoridated.

## Common conditions

**Cavities:** Bacteria + sugar/starch → acid → enamel erosion. Early cavities: reversible with fluoride. Advanced: drilling and filling required.
**Gingivitis:** Early gum disease. Red, swollen gums that bleed easily. Reversible with professional cleaning + better home care.
**Periodontitis:** Advanced gum disease with bone loss. Irreversible but controllable. Associated with cardiovascular disease and diabetes.
**Sensitivity:** Exposed dentin from recession, erosion, or cracking. Sensitive toothpaste (stannous fluoride) provides relief.

## Preventive care schedule

**Routine exams and cleaning:** Every 6 months for most adults.
**Active gum disease:** Every 3-4 months.
**X-rays:** Every 1-2 years for low-risk adults.
**Sealants:** Highly effective for children's molars — prevents cavities in grooves.

## Emergency situations

**Knocked-out tooth:** Handle crown only (not root). Rinse gently. Reinsert immediately if possible, or store in milk. Dentist within 30 minutes is critical for re-implantation.
**Dental abscess:** Infection. Severe throbbing pain, possible swelling. Can become life-threatening if untreated. Same-day dental care required. Take ibuprofen for pain while waiting.
**Lost filling/crown:** Temporary dental cement (Dentemp, pharmacy) holds until appointment. Don't chew on that side.
