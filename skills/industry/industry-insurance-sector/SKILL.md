# Insurance Industry

## Insurance fundamentals

### Risk pooling principle

Many policyholders pay premiums to create a pool. Claims paid from pool.
Law of large numbers: Larger pool → more predictable losses → lower risk for insurer.

### Key concepts

**Premium:** Payment for coverage. Calculated: Pure premium (expected losses) + Expense loading + Profit.
**Deductible:** Amount policyholder pays before insurance kicks in.
**Policy limit:** Maximum insurer pays per claim or per period.
**Subrogation:** Insurer recovers costs from at-fault third party after paying claim.

## Insurance lines

### Property and casualty (P&C)

**Auto insurance:** Liability (required), collision (vehicle), comprehensive (theft, weather).
**Homeowners:** Structure + contents + liability. NOT flood or earthquake — separate policies.
**Commercial property/liability:** Covers businesses.
**Workers compensation:** Employer-paid. Covers employee workplace injuries. Mandatory in most US states.

### Life and health

**Term life:** Death benefit for fixed period. Lower cost. Pure insurance.
**Whole life:** Permanent + cash value accumulation. Higher cost. Investment component.
**Health insurance:** ACA categories (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum) by actuarial value.
**Long-term care:** Covers nursing home and home care. Growing importance as population ages.

## Actuarial science

Actuaries use statistics to price insurance. FSA or FCAS designation.
**Loss ratio:** Losses paid / Premiums earned. 60-70% healthy for P&C.
**Combined ratio:** Loss ratio + expense ratio. >100% = underwriting loss.

## InsurTech

Usage-based insurance (UBI): Telematics in cars. Good driver discounts.
AI underwriting: Real-time risk assessment.
Parametric insurance: Pays on trigger event (earthquake of specified magnitude) — no claims adjustment needed.
Embedded insurance: Sold at point of sale of product.
