# Entrepreneurship

## The lean startup methodology

### Build-Measure-Learn loop

**Build:** Create a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) — smallest thing that tests your key assumption.
**Measure:** Collect data on actual user behavior. Not surveys — real actions.
**Learn:** Validated learning. Did your hypothesis hold? Pivot or persevere.
**Objective:** Reduce the time through the Build-Measure-Learn cycle.

### Types of pivots

**Customer segment pivot:** Product works, but for different customers.
**Solution pivot:** Same problem, different solution.
**Revenue model pivot:** Same customers, different monetization.
**Platform pivot:** Application → platform.

## Customer discovery

**Before building anything — interview 50+ potential customers:**
"Tell me about the last time you [experienced the problem]."
"What do you do today to solve it?"
"How much time/money does the current solution cost you?"
DO NOT pitch your solution. Listen only.

**The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick):**
Bad question: "Would you use this if I built it?"
Good question: "Tell me about the last time you experienced this."

## Startup funding stages

**Pre-revenue/pre-product:**
Bootstrapping: Self-fund. Maximum control.
Friends and family: Quick. Keep amounts small (<$50K).
Grants: SBIR/STTR (US federal), state innovation programs.

**Early traction:**
Angel investors: $25K-$500K. Move faster than VCs.
SAFE/Convertible notes: Simple financing instruments.
Accelerators: YC ($500K for 7%), Techstars ($20K for 6%).

**Scaling:**
Series A: $2-15M. Institutional VCs.
Series B+: $20M+. Defined metrics expected.

## Building founding team

Two co-founders > solo. Skills diversity: Technical + Business.
**Equity split:** Equal splits are most stable.
**Vesting:** 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff. Always. Non-negotiable.

## First revenue

Charge from day one. $1 from a stranger validates more than $100K in commitments.
First customers = best reference customers and case studies.
Don't over-engineer pricing — charge something reasonable, optimize later.
