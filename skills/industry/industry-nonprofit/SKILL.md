# Nonprofit Management

## Structure and governance

**501(c)(3):** Tax-exempt public charity. Donations are tax-deductible. Cannot engage in political campaigning. Limited lobbying.
**Board of directors:** Fiduciary responsibility. Strategic oversight. Fundraising. Hire/fire CEO.
Three duties: Duty of care (informed decisions), Duty of loyalty (no self-dealing), Duty of obedience (mission adherence).
Best board size: 9-15 members. Mix of skills, networks, and community representation.
**Executive director/CEO:** Hired by board. Manages staff and operations. Implements strategy.
**Conflict of interest policy:** Required for 990 reporting. Board members must recuse from decisions where they have personal financial interest.

## Funding landscape

**Individual donors:** 70%+ of all US charitable giving. Major gifts, annual fund, planned giving.
**Foundations:** Private foundations (require 5% annual payout), community foundations, corporate foundations.
**Government grants:** Federal, state, local. Often program-specific with compliance requirements.
**Earned revenue:** Fee-for-service, membership fees, events, social enterprise.
**Corporate giving:** Cash grants, sponsorships, cause marketing, employee giving/matching.

**Funding mix:** Healthiest nonprofits have diverse revenue streams. Single-source dependency is fragile.

## Fundraising

### Major gifts

Cultivation (relationship building over time) → Solicitation (the ask) → Stewardship (grateful follow-up).
Prospect research: Capacity (ability to give), Affinity (connection to mission), Propensity (giving history).
**Moves management:** Track each prospect's progress through cultivation stages.
**The ask:** Match the ask amount to research. Name the amount. Ask face-to-face when possible. Then be quiet and wait for the answer.

### Grant writing

**Research:** Match your programs to funder's priorities. Read annual reports, 990s, past grants.
**LOI (Letter of Inquiry):** Often first step. 1-2 pages: organization, program, funding request.
**Full proposal components:** Executive summary, organizational background, need statement (why is this problem important?), program description (what you'll do), evaluation (how you'll measure success), budget (realistic, narrative justifying each line), organizational capacity.
**Budget:** Must show: direct program costs, overhead/indirect (typically 10-20%), and demonstrate cost-effectiveness.
**Grant calendar:** Track deadlines. Allow 4-6 weeks to write quality proposal.

## Financial management

**Restricted vs. unrestricted funds:** Restricted = donor-designated for specific purpose. Must be spent as designated.
**Form 990:** Annual IRS filing for nonprofits. Public document. Shows financials, governance, compensation.
**Audit:** Required by most funders above $750K annual revenue. Independent financial audit.
**Financial ratio watchpoints:**
Program expense ratio: Program spending / Total spending. Target: 65-75%+.
Fundraising efficiency: Fundraising cost / Funds raised. Target: < 25%.
Months of operating reserves: 3-6 months cash on hand recommended.

## Impact measurement

**Logic model:** Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Outcomes → Impact.
Outputs: What you did (# of people served, classes held). Quantitative, easy to count.
Outcomes: What changed (% who gained skills, improved health, increased income). Meaningful.
**Theory of change:** Narrative of how activities lead to outcomes. Assumptions made explicit.
**Evaluation:** Rigor spectrum: simple tracking → pre/post surveys → comparison group → RCT.
