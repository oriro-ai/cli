# Public Speaking

## Managing nerves

**Reframe anxiety as excitement.** Research shows telling yourself "I'm excited" (not "calm down") improves performance. Both are physiological arousal — different interpretation.
**Preparation is the best anxiety reducer.** Know your material so deeply that forgetting a word doesn't matter.
**Physiological tools:** Slow deep breaths (4-7-8). Power pose before (not during) presentation. Arrive early to make the space familiar.

## Structure

**The rule:** Tell them what you'll tell them → Tell them → Tell them what you told them.

**Opening:** The first 30 seconds determines attention. Options:

- Question: "How many of you have..." (engages immediately)
- Startling statistic: "Every 60 seconds, X happens"
- Short story: The most powerful opener
- What NOT to do: "Today I'm going to talk about..." (boring), thanking for the introduction (wasted time)

**Body — rule of three:** Three main points maximum. More than three and audiences lose the thread.
Each point: Claim → Evidence → Connection back to thesis.

**Closing:** Callback to opening. Summary of key takeaways. Clear call to action. Strong last line — don't say "That's it" or "Any questions?" as your final words.

## Delivery

**Eye contact:** Not scanning — genuine connection with one person for 3-5 seconds, then move.
**Pace:** Slower than you think. Silence is your most powerful pause. Nervous speakers speed up.
**Volume:** Project — not yell. Vary for emphasis. Quiet can be powerful.
**Movement:** Purposeful movement = energy and point transitions. Random movement = nervous energy. Plant feet when making key points.
**Hands:** Natural gestures reinforcing words. Avoid: fig leaf (clasped in front), hand-wringing, pockets.
**Filler words:** "Um," "uh," "like," "you know" — silence is better. Record yourself; awareness is the fix.

## Storytelling structure (narrative)

**Hero's journey in 2 minutes:**

1. Character in their normal world.
2. Problem appears. What was at stake?
3. The journey / struggle.
4. Resolution and lesson.

**Specificity:** "A 65-year-old grandmother named Dorothy in rural Ohio" vs. "an older woman." Specific is memorable.

## Q&A management

Listen to the full question before answering.
If unclear: Rephrase and confirm. "So you're asking...is that right?"
For hostile questions: "That's an important concern. Here's how I think about it..."
You can say "I don't know, but I'll find out."
Don't let one person dominate. "Let me take a couple more questions."
