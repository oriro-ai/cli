# Exercise Science and Fitness

## Physical activity guidelines (US DHHS / WHO)

Adults (18-64): 150-300 min moderate OR 75-150 min vigorous aerobic/week PLUS 2+ days muscle-strengthening.
Older adults (65+): Same plus balance training to prevent falls.
More = more benefit, up to a point. Even 10 minutes matters.

## The FITT principle (program design)

**Frequency:** How often (days/week)
**Intensity:** How hard (% max heart rate, RPE, % 1RM)
**Time:** Duration per session
**Type:** Modality (running, lifting, swimming, cycling)

## Strength training fundamentals

### Progressive overload — the non-negotiable principle

Muscles adapt to stress. You must consistently increase stress to keep adapting.
Methods: increase weight, increase reps, increase sets, decrease rest, increase range of motion.

### Rep ranges and goals

1-5 reps @ 85-100% 1RM: Maximal strength (neural adaptation, some hypertrophy)
6-12 reps @ 67-85% 1RM: Hypertrophy (muscle size growth) — most efficient range
12-20 reps @ 50-67% 1RM: Muscular endurance (some hypertrophy)
All ranges build strength; all build some muscle. Pick based on goal.

### Volume and frequency

Weekly volume: 10-20 sets per muscle group per week for hypertrophy.
Frequency: Each muscle 2-3× per week produces better results than once.
Full body 3× per week > upper/lower split > bro split for most people.

### Compound vs. isolation exercises

Compound (multiple joints): squat, deadlift, bench press, row, overhead press — prioritize these.
Isolation (single joint): curls, lateral raises, leg extensions — supplementary.

### Progressive beginner program (5×5 style)

- Squat: 5×5
- Bench press: 5×5
- Barbell row: 5×5
  Alternate with:
- Squat: 5×5
- Overhead press: 5×5
- Deadlift: 1×5
  Add 2.5-5 kg per session. Simple. Works for 3-6 months.

## Cardiovascular training

### Heart rate zones

Max heart rate estimate: 220 - age
Zone 1 (50-60% MHR): Very easy, recovery
Zone 2 (60-70% MHR): Easy, fat oxidation, aerobic base — most valuable for health
Zone 3 (70-80% MHR): Moderate, cardio fitness
Zone 4 (80-90% MHR): Hard, lactate threshold improvement
Zone 5 (90-100% MHR): Maximum, very short duration

**Zone 2 is underrated.** 80% of cardio should be Zone 2 (can hold conversation).
Builds aerobic base, mitochondrial density, metabolic health.

### HIIT (High-Intensity Interval Training)

High intensity intervals + recovery periods.
Time-efficient: 20-30 min sessions comparable in some adaptations to 60 min steady state.
Example: 20 sec maximal effort / 40 sec rest × 8 rounds.
Limit: 2-3× per week max — taxing on nervous system.

## Recovery — the undervalued half of training

**Sleep:** Muscle is built during sleep, not in the gym. 7-9 hours is non-negotiable for adaptation.
**Protein timing:** Consume protein within 2 hours post-workout. 20-40g complete protein.
**Deload weeks:** Every 4-6 weeks, reduce volume/intensity by ~40%. Allows full recovery.
**Overtraining signs:** Persistent fatigue, declining performance, elevated resting HR, mood changes.

## Protein for training

Training + high protein = maximizes muscle retention during weight loss, maximizes gain during surplus.
Target: 1.6-2.2g protein per kg bodyweight for active individuals.
Spread across 3-5 meals. Minimum 20-25g per meal to maximize muscle protein synthesis.

## Common mistakes

**Not tracking progressive overload:** If you're not adding weight or reps over months, you're not progressing.
**Too much variety:** Consistency > novelty. Stick to a program 3-4 months minimum.
**Insufficient protein:** Most people eat half what's needed for muscle growth.
**Insufficient sleep:** Training hard + sleeping 5 hours = spinning your wheels.
**Skipping compound movements:** Program built around isolation exercises is inefficient.
