# Home Electrical Systems

## Safety first — the non-negotiables

**Never work on live circuits.** Turn off the breaker AND verify with a non-contact voltage tester.
**GFCI outlets** (with TEST and RESET buttons): required near water. Test monthly.
**Arc-fault breakers (AFCI)**: protect against electrical fires from damaged wiring.
**When to call an electrician:**

- Any work inside walls
- Panel/breaker box work
- New circuits
- Anything requiring a permit (required in most jurisdictions for all but the simplest work)
- You're unsure — electrical mistakes can kill you or burn your house down

## Understanding your electrical panel

**Main breaker:** Controls all power to your home. Usually 100A, 150A, or 200A.
**Individual circuit breakers:** Protect each circuit from overload.

- Single pole: 15A or 20A, 120V — lights, outlets
- Double pole: typically 30A, 40A, or 50A, 240V — dryer, range, HVAC, EV charger

**Breaker rating:** The maximum continuous load the circuit should carry.
Rule: Never exceed 80% of breaker rating for continuous loads.
20A breaker: max continuous draw = 16A

**Reading the panel:**
Each breaker should be labeled with what it controls.
Write down your circuit map if it's missing or inaccurate.

**Why breakers trip:**

1. Overload: too many devices drawing power
2. Short circuit: hot wire touches neutral or ground (breaker trips immediately)
3. Ground fault: hot wire touches ground or wet surface
4. Defective breaker (uncommon but happens)

**Resetting a tripped breaker:**
Turn OFF then firmly back ON. If it trips again immediately: there's a fault or overload.
Do NOT repeatedly reset without identifying the cause.

## Outlet types

**Standard 15A outlet:** Works with standard plugs. Most living areas.
**20A outlet:** Has a T-shaped slot. For heavy appliances. Uses 12-gauge wire.
**GFCI outlet:** Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter. Required:

- Bathrooms
- Kitchens within 6 feet of sink
- Garages
- Outdoors
- Basements
- Pools and spas
  Has TEST and RESET buttons. Test monthly.

**AFCI outlet/breaker:** Arc Fault Circuit Interrupter. Required in modern code for:
Bedrooms, living rooms, dining rooms, most areas (2020 NEC expanded requirements).
Protects against arcing from damaged or aged wiring — biggest cause of house fires.

**240V outlets:**
NEMA 14-30: 30A dryer
NEMA 14-50: 50A range/EV charger
NEMA 6-20: 20A 240V workshop equipment

## Basic wiring concepts

**Wire colors (standard residential):**

- Black: hot (ungrounded conductor — carries current)
- White: neutral (returns current)
- Green or bare copper: ground (safety path)
- Red: second hot (in 240V circuits or switch loops)

**Wire gauges (bigger gauge number = thinner wire):**

- 14 AWG: 15A circuits (lights, standard outlets)
- 12 AWG: 20A circuits (kitchen, bathroom, garage)
- 10 AWG: 30A circuits (dryer, some AC units)
- 8 AWG: 40-50A circuits (ranges, large AC)

Using undersized wire creates fire hazard. Never put 14-gauge wire on a 20A breaker.

**Ground fault vs. short circuit:**
Ground fault: current takes unintended path through ground. GFCI protection addresses this.
Short circuit: hot touches neutral. Overcurrent. Breaker addresses this.

## Common DIY-safe electrical tasks

**Replacing an outlet:** De-energize circuit, verify dead with tester, replace like-for-like.
Same wire colors to same terminals. No changes to circuit.

**Replacing a light switch:** Same principle. Simple single-pole switch replacement is beginner-friendly.
3-way and 4-way switches are more complex — research the specific configuration before starting.

**Installing a ceiling fan where a light exists:** Usually DIY-safe if box is fan-rated.
Fan boxes must be rated to support the weight and motion of a ceiling fan.

**Adding a GFCI outlet:** Can replace a standard outlet with GFCI for added protection.

## When to call an electrician — firm list

- Adding new circuits or outlets (requires opening walls, panel work)
- Upgrading electrical panel (100A to 200A)
- Installing EV charger (usually requires new 240V circuit)
- Any work on the service entrance (the wires from utility to your panel)
- Whole-house rewiring (aluminum wiring upgrade, knob-and-tube replacement)
- Installing subpanel
- Commercial or multi-family buildings
- Anything requiring a permit in your jurisdiction

**Cost reference (US, 2024):**

- Electrician hourly: $50-100/hour (journeyman), $75-150 (master)
- GFCI outlet replacement: $150-250
- New 20A circuit: $300-600
- Panel upgrade 100A→200A: $1,500-3,500
