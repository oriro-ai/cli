# Business Analysis

## Problem definition (before any analysis)

**Five Whys:** Ask "why" five times to reach root cause vs. symptoms.
Symptom: "Sales are down 20%."
Why? "Conversion rate dropped."
Why? "Checkout abandonment increased."
Why? "New checkout flow has more steps."
Why? "Mobile experience is poor."
Root cause: Mobile checkout UX needs redesign.

**Problem statement format:** "The problem of [describe problem] affects [stakeholders] and results in [impact]. A successful solution would [describe desired state]."

## Stakeholder analysis

**RACI matrix:**
R — Responsible: Does the work.
A — Accountable: Owns the outcome. One person only.
C — Consulted: Input before decisions.
I — Informed: Told of decisions/outcomes.
Apply to every major activity/decision.

**Power/Interest grid:**
High power, high interest → Manage closely (champion or potential saboteur)
High power, low interest → Keep satisfied (don't burden, but keep informed)
Low power, high interest → Keep informed (useful for ground-level insight)
Low power, low interest → Monitor (minimal engagement)

## Requirements gathering

**Elicitation techniques:**
Interviews: One-on-one. Ask open-ended questions. Listen more than you talk.
Workshops: Group session. Faster than individual interviews. Facilitates consensus.
Observation: Watch people do their actual work. More revealing than what they describe.
Document analysis: Review existing processes, reports, systems.
Prototyping: Show them something; react to the concrete vs. imagining.

**Requirements types:**
Business requirements: Why the project. What business objective or problem.
Stakeholder requirements: What stakeholders need from the solution.
Solution requirements: What the solution must do (functional) and how it must perform (non-functional).
Transition requirements: What's needed to move from current to future state.

**Non-functional requirements:** Performance, security, scalability, usability, reliability, compatibility.
Often neglected until late — and expensive to retrofit.

## Process mapping

**Current state vs. future state maps:** Never design the future without understanding the current.
**SIPOC:** Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers. High-level process view.
**Swim lane diagram:** Shows who does what in cross-functional processes. Best for highlighting handoff points where errors occur.
**Value stream map:** Shows flow of materials and information + time at each step. Identifies waste and delays.

**Process analysis questions:**

- What is the purpose of each step?
- What could go wrong at each step?
- Where do errors/rework typically occur?
- Where does work wait?
- What would happen if we eliminated this step?

## Data analysis for business decisions

**Descriptive analysis:** What happened? Dashboards, reports, historical data.
**Diagnostic analysis:** Why did it happen? Drill down, correlation analysis.
**Predictive analysis:** What will happen? Models, forecasts.
**Prescriptive analysis:** What should we do? Optimization, simulation.

**Key metrics framework:**
Lead metrics: Predict future performance (sales calls made, leads generated).
Lag metrics: Report past performance (revenue, customer satisfaction scores).
Balance: Don't optimize only lag metrics — they can't be managed directly.

## Feasibility study

**Technical feasibility:** Can we build it? What's the capability gap?
**Operational feasibility:** Can we run it? People, processes, systems?
**Financial feasibility:** Is the ROI positive? Payback period acceptable?
**Legal/regulatory feasibility:** Are there compliance barriers?
**Schedule feasibility:** Can we deliver within the required timeframe?

**Business case structure:**
Executive summary → Problem/opportunity → Options considered → Recommended solution → Benefits → Costs → Risks → Implementation plan → ROI/NPV analysis

**ROI = (Benefits - Costs) / Costs × 100%**
Payback period = Initial investment / Annual net benefits
