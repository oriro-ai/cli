# Economics

## Microeconomics

### Supply and demand

**Demand:** Inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded. Lower price → more demand.
Demand shifters (move entire curve): Income, preferences, prices of related goods, expectations, number of buyers.
**Supply:** Direct relationship between price and quantity supplied. Higher price → more supply.
Supply shifters: Input costs, technology, expectations, number of sellers.
**Equilibrium:** Price where quantity supplied = quantity demanded. Market "clears."
**Price controls:**
Price ceiling (max price below equilibrium): Shortage (e.g., rent control).
Price floor (min price above equilibrium): Surplus (e.g., minimum wage → unemployment debate).

### Elasticity

**Price elasticity of demand:** % change in quantity / % change in price.
Elastic (|E| > 1): Quantity responds strongly to price. Luxury goods.
Inelastic (|E| < 1): Quantity responds weakly to price. Necessities, addictive goods.
**Implications:** Inelastic goods: raising price increases revenue. Elastic: raising price decreases revenue.

### Market structures

**Perfect competition:** Many firms, identical products, free entry/exit. P = MC = MR. Zero economic profit long-run.
**Monopoly:** One firm, high barriers to entry. MR < P. Deadweight loss. Regulates markets.
**Oligopoly:** Few dominant firms. Strategic interdependence (game theory). OPEC, tech giants.
**Monopolistic competition:** Many firms, differentiated products. Restaurants, clothing.

### Externalities

**Negative externality:** Cost imposed on third parties. Example: Pollution. Market overproduces.
**Positive externality:** Benefit to third parties. Example: Vaccines, education. Market underproduces.
**Solutions:** Taxes (Pigouvian) to correct negative externalities; subsidies for positive; regulation; property rights (Coase theorem).

## Macroeconomics

### GDP and growth

**GDP = C + I + G + (X-M)** (Consumption + Investment + Government + Net Exports)
Nominal GDP: Current prices. Real GDP: Inflation-adjusted (more meaningful for comparisons).
**GDP growth drivers:** Capital accumulation, labor force growth, technological progress (productivity).

### Inflation

**CPI (Consumer Price Index):** Tracks price of fixed basket of goods. Most common inflation measure.
**Causes:** Demand-pull (too much money chasing goods), Cost-push (supply shocks), Monetary (too rapid money supply growth).
**Effects:** Erodes purchasing power. Hurts savers and creditors. Helps debtors. Redistributes wealth.
**Hyperinflation:** Inflation > 50%/month. Destroys economies (Weimar Germany, Zimbabwe, Venezuela).
**Target:** Most central banks target ~2% annual inflation.

### Monetary policy

Central banks (Federal Reserve, ECB, etc.) use interest rates and money supply.
**Expansionary:** Lower rates, buy bonds (quantitative easing). Stimulates borrowing and spending.
**Contractionary:** Raise rates, sell bonds. Cools inflation.
**Zero lower bound problem:** Can't cut rates below zero (effectively). Hit in 2008, 2020.

### Fiscal policy

Government spending and taxation.
**Expansionary:** Increase spending or cut taxes. Stimulates demand (Keynesian multiplier).
**Contractionary:** Cut spending or raise taxes. Reduces demand (austerity).
**Automatic stabilizers:** Unemployment insurance, progressive taxes — automatically expand/contract without policy action.

## Development economics

**Poverty traps:** Low income → can't save → can't invest → low productivity → low income.
**Institutions matter:** Rule of law, property rights, contract enforcement, anti-corruption → economic development.
**Import substitution vs. export-led:** Historical debate. East Asian "miracle" = export-led industrialization.
**Human Development Index (HDI):** Income + Education + Health. Broader than GDP per capita.
