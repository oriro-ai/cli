# Home Plumbing

## Water system basics

**Supply lines:** Pressurized cold water enters from municipal supply or well.
Hot water supply goes through water heater first.
Typical household pressure: 40-80 PSI. Over 80 PSI: install pressure reducing valve (PRV).

**Drain-Waste-Vent (DWV) system:**

- Drain: carries waste to sewer/septic
- Waste: vertical pipes carrying waste
- Vent: allows air into drains to prevent siphoning of trap water (sewer gases)

**P-trap:** The U-shaped pipe under every fixture.
Holds water to block sewer gases from entering the home.
If a fixture isn't used for weeks, trap can dry out → sewer smell. Fix: run water.

**Main shutoff valve:** Know where it is. Can shut off all water to house.
Usually at the point water enters the home (basement, crawl space, outside near foundation, or in a utility box near the street).

## Pipe types

**Copper:** Durable, 50+ year lifespan. Suitable for hot and cold. Expensive. Requires soldering.
**PEX (cross-linked polyethylene):** Flexible, easy to install, freeze-resistant, inexpensive.
Now the dominant choice for new construction and remodels.
PEX-A (most flexible), PEX-B (common), PEX-C.
**PVC:** White plastic. Cold water supply and DWV. Not for hot water supply.
**CPVC:** Cream-colored plastic. Can be used for hot water supply unlike PVC.
**ABS:** Black plastic. DWV only. Common in older homes.
**Galvanized steel:** Gray metal. Found in pre-1960s homes. Prone to corrosion and reduced flow.
**Cast iron:** Black. Found in pre-1980s homes. Very durable but heavy. Common in drain systems.

## Common repairs

### Clogged drains

**Kitchen sink:** Usually grease buildup.

1. Try boiling water (NOT with PVC pipes — use hot, not boiling).
2. Baking soda + white vinegar (pour 1/2 cup baking soda, then 1/2 cup vinegar, wait 30 min, flush with hot water).
3. Plunger (cup plunger, not flange).
4. Hand auger/drain snake.
5. If still clogged: could be deeper in the drain line. Call plumber.

**Bathroom sink/tub:** Usually hair.
Remove and clean stopper/drain cover. Use hair removal tool (Zip-It) first. Then snake if needed.

**Toilet:** Use a toilet auger (closet auger), not a regular snake.

### Running toilet

Most common cause: **flapper not sealing properly**.

1. Remove tank lid. Flush and watch.
2. If water keeps running: flapper is worn. $5-10 to replace. 10-minute fix.
3. Check float height: water level should be 1 inch below overflow tube.
4. If water runs into bowl constantly: check flapper seal; replace if worn.
5. Check fill valve: if hissing and water level is at overflow tube, replace fill valve.

Running toilet wastes 200+ gallons/day. Fix it immediately.

### Dripping faucet

**Ball faucet (single handle, round cap):** Replace ball, seats, springs, and O-rings.
**Cartridge faucet (single or double handle):** Replace cartridge.
**Ceramic disc (wide single handle):** Replace disc cylinder.
**Compression (two handles, turn to tighten):** Replace rubber washers.

Parts are at home improvement stores. Bring the old part for matching.
Turn off supply shutoffs under the sink before any faucet work.

### Toilet running water or rocking toilet

Rocking toilet: usually two causes — wax ring failure (requires reset) or broken floor flange.
Water around base of toilet (not just condensation): wax ring has failed. Must reset toilet.

## Water heater

### Tank water heater

**Life expectancy:** 8-12 years (tank). 15-20 years (tankless).
**Temperature setting:** 120°F recommended. 140°F kills Legionella but scalds risk.

**Sediment flushing (annual):**
Sediment builds up and reduces efficiency.

1. Turn off cold supply to heater.
2. Attach hose to drain valve at bottom.
3. Open pressure relief valve slightly (or nearby hot faucet).
4. Open drain valve. Let sediment flush out.
5. Flush until water runs clear.
6. Close valve, restore cold supply, restore power/gas.

**Anode rod:** Sacrificial metal rod that prevents tank corrosion.
Replace every 5-7 years. If you have softened water, replace more frequently.
Failure to replace anode → tank corrodes → leaks → must replace.

**Pressure relief valve (T&P valve):**
Safety device that opens if pressure or temperature is too high.
Test annually by briefly lifting the lever. If it doesn't release or water continues to flow: replace.

**When to replace rather than repair:**
Age > 10-12 years. Significant rust in water. Tank is leaking (corrosion, not fitting).

### Tankless water heater

Heats water on demand. No tank standby losses.
Requires: higher gas BTU rating (for gas) or higher electrical service (for electric).
Annual descaling recommended in hard water areas.

## Shutting off water quickly

**Individual fixture shutoffs:** Under every sink, behind every toilet. Know and exercise them annually (they can seize).

**Whole house shutoff:** Know exactly where it is before you have an emergency.

**How to turn off water with no working shutoff:**
Main shutoff at the street/curb box. Requires a meter key (available at hardware stores).
In an emergency, utility company can turn off immediately.

## When to always call a plumber

- Any work on gas lines (always requires licensed plumber or HVAC contractor)
- Main drain line clogs (require sewer snake or hydrojetting)
- Any sewer line work
- Replacing the main water line
- Water main breaks
- Persistent low water pressure (may be supply line issue)
- Any work requiring permits in your jurisdiction
- Sewage backup into multiple fixtures (main line blockage)
