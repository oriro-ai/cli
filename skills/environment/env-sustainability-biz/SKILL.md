# Business Sustainability

## ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance)

Framework used by investors to assess sustainability-related risks and opportunities.
Not the same as corporate social responsibility (CSR) — ESG is material business risk, not philanthropy.

**Environmental:** Climate risks, emissions, water use, biodiversity, waste.
**Social:** Labor practices, supply chain human rights, community impact, data privacy, product safety.
**Governance:** Board composition, executive pay, anti-corruption, shareholder rights, transparency.

**ESG reporting frameworks:**
GRI (Global Reporting Initiative): Comprehensive, stakeholder-oriented. Most widely used globally.
SASB: Industry-specific, investor-focused. Materiality-based.
TCFD: Climate financial risk disclosure. Increasingly required.
CSRD (EU): Mandatory for large EU companies from 2024. Most stringent.
SEC proposed climate disclosure: US equivalent (pending as of 2024).
ISSB (IFRS S1, S2): Global baseline sustainability disclosure. Convergence direction.

## Circular economy

**Linear economy:** Take → Make → Dispose.
**Circular economy:** Eliminate waste and pollution, circulate products and materials, regenerate natural systems.

**Circular strategies (R-hierarchy):**
Refuse: Don't create the need.
Reduce: Use less.
Reuse: Use again as-is.
Repair: Extend life.
Remanufacture: Restore to original specifications.
Recycle: Convert to raw material.
Recover: Energy recovery (last resort before disposal).

**Business models:** Product-as-a-service (Michelin sells tires by the km, not per tire), take-back programs, design for disassembly, remanufacturing.

## Supply chain sustainability

**Key risks:** Forced labor, deforestation, human rights violations in supply chains can create legal, reputational, and operational risk.
**Legislation:** EU Due Diligence Directive, German Supply Chain Act, UK Modern Slavery Act, US Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act.
**Supplier assessment:** Questionnaires, audits (first-party, third-party), certifications (Fair Trade, FSC, Rainforest Alliance).
**Transparency tools:** Open Supply Hub (opensupplyhub.org — free supplier database).

## Sustainability strategy

**Materiality assessment:** Which ESG issues are most material to your business and stakeholders? Focus resources here.
**Double materiality (EU approach):** Issues material to financial performance AND issues where company has impact on the world.
**Sustainability committee:** Board-level oversight. Tie executive compensation to sustainability targets.
**Integrated thinking:** Sustainability embedded in strategy, not a separate function.
