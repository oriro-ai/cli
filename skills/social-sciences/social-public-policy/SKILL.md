# Public Policy

## The policy cycle

**Problem identification and agenda setting:** How does an issue get recognized as requiring government action? Crises, advocacy, media, election cycles.
**Policy formulation:** Develop options and proposals. Who is involved? Technocrats, interest groups, elected officials, research organizations.
**Adoption/legitimation:** Policy is officially enacted. Requires political support.
**Implementation:** Converting policy into action. Where many policies fail. Bureaucratic discretion, resources, coordination.
**Evaluation:** Did it work? What were unintended consequences? (Feeds back to problem identification.)

## Policy design

**Policy instruments (types):**
Regulations: Commands and controls. What you must or must not do.
Economic instruments: Taxes, subsidies, cap-and-trade. Change incentives.
Voluntary approaches: Industry self-regulation, labeling.
Information: Public education, disclosure requirements.
Direct provision: Government provides services directly (public schools, VA healthcare).

**Policy targeting:**
Universal: Available to everyone (Medicare in Canada, public schools).
Means-tested: For those below an income threshold (SNAP, Medicaid in US).
Universal tends to be more politically durable; means-tested is more efficient but stigmatized.

## Policy analysis frameworks

### Cost-benefit analysis (CBA)

Quantify all costs and benefits in monetary terms. Net benefit = Benefits - Costs.
Challenges: How to value human life? Environmental services? Future generations?
**Discount rate:** Future benefits worth less than present benefits. Choice of discount rate dramatically affects analysis.

### Cost-effectiveness analysis

Compare costs of achieving the same outcome across options. Useful when benefits hard to monetize.
"Which intervention produces the most QALYs (quality-adjusted life years) per dollar?"

### Regulatory impact analysis

Federal agencies (US) must estimate costs and benefits before major regulations.
Required to choose the most cost-effective alternative.

## Implementation challenges

**Street-level bureaucrats (Lipsky):** Front-line workers (teachers, police, social workers) make discretionary decisions that effectively create policy.
**Principal-agent problem:** Principals (elected officials) delegate to agents (bureaucrats) who have different information and interests.
**Coordination failures:** Multiple agencies responsible for one issue without clear authority.
**Implementation deficit:** Gap between policy intentions and actual outcomes.

## Evidence-based policy

**Randomized control trials in policy:** J-PAL (Abdul Latif Jameel Poverty Action Lab) applies RCTs to development policy. Rigorous evidence on what works.
**What Works Clearinghouses:** Government-funded databases of evaluated programs.
**Policy transfer:** Adopt policies that worked in other contexts. Must assess transferability.

**Politics vs. evidence:** Evidence informs but doesn't determine policy. Values and interests always involved. The question "what works?" requires prior answer to "for what?"
