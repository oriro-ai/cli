# Corporate Governance

## Board duties and fiduciary obligations

**Duty of Care:** Act in informed, diligent manner. Review materials. Ask questions. Get expert advice when needed. Business Judgment Rule protects directors who meet this standard.
**Duty of Loyalty:** Put company interests above personal interests. No self-dealing without disclosure and approval. Recuse from decisions where personal interest exists.
**Duty of Obedience (nonprofits):** Follow the organization's mission and governing documents.

## Essential corporate records

**Minutes:** Document every board and shareholder meeting decision. Must be contemporaneous.
**Resolutions:** Formal records of board decisions (officer appointments, equity grants, major contracts, bank accounts). Written consent resolutions allow action without meeting.
**Capitalization table:** All equity ownership, option grants, warrants, SAFEs, convertible notes.
**Stock ledger:** Record of all stock issuances and transfers.

**Failure to maintain records:** Pierces the corporate veil. Personal liability for directors.

## Annual compliance calendar

**Delaware C-Corp:** Annual franchise tax (due March 1). Authorized shares method vs. assumed par value capital method — calculate both, pay lower. Can be significant (up to $200,000+ for large authorized share counts if using wrong method).
**Annual report:** Most states require. Filing fee.
**Federal:** Corporate tax return (Form 1120) due April 15 or September 15 with extension.
**Registered agent:** Maintain registered agent in state of incorporation. Required for legal service.

## Shareholder rights

**Information rights:** Right to inspect books and records (varies by state and shareholder size).
**Voting rights:** Each share gets the votes its class provides. Common: 1 vote/share typical. Class B at tech companies: 10 votes/share (dual class structure — founder control).
**Preemptive rights:** Right to participate in future fundraises pro-rata (maintain % ownership).
**Liquidation preference:** Priority in asset distribution if company sold or wound down.
**Anti-dilution protection:** Protect investors from down rounds.

## Key governance practices for startups

**Consent in lieu of meeting:** Most decisions made by written consent — avoid scheduling board meetings for routine matters.
**Executive sessions:** Board meets without CEO periodically. Standard governance. Not a crisis signal.
**Audit committee:** For public companies required. For private companies: good practice if sophisticated investors present.
**Conflict of interest policy:** Written policy. Directors disclose annually and for specific transactions.
