# Men's Health

## Preventive screenings for men

**Blood pressure:** Every 2 years if normal; annually if elevated.
**Cholesterol:** Every 5 years starting at 35; earlier if risk factors.
**Diabetes:** Every 3 years starting at 35.
**Colorectal cancer:** Start at 45. Colonoscopy every 10 years preferred.
**Abdominal aortic aneurysm:** Once at 65-75 for men who have ever smoked.
**Prostate (PSA):** Discuss with doctor at age 50 (45 if African American or father/brother with prostate cancer before 65; 40 if strong family history). Screening decision is individual — discuss risks and benefits.
**Testicular self-exam:** Monthly. Know your normal. Report changes to doctor promptly.

## Prostate health

**BPH (Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia):** Prostate enlargement with aging. Very common after 50.
Symptoms: Frequent urination, urgency, weak stream, incomplete emptying, nighttime urination.
Treatment: Lifestyle modifications → medications (alpha-blockers, 5-alpha reductase inhibitors) → procedures.

**Prostate cancer:** Most common cancer in men (excluding skin). Usually slow-growing.
Most prostate cancers are diagnosed and treated without causing death.
PSA screening is controversial — benefits vs. overdiagnosis/overtreatment.
Active surveillance is appropriate for low-grade cancers.

## Testosterone

**Normal range:** 300-1000 ng/dL total testosterone (labs vary). Below 300 = low.
**Symptoms of low T:** Low energy, decreased libido, depression, difficulty concentrating, loss of muscle mass, increased body fat, anemia, bone loss.
**Causes:** Aging (normal decline ~1%/year after 30), hypogonadism, obesity, chronic illness, medications.
**Before treating:** Test twice (morning levels most accurate). Rule out reversible causes (obesity, poor sleep, alcohol, medication side effects).
**TRT (Testosterone Replacement Therapy):** Options include injections, gels, patches, pellets. Monitor hematocrit (increases). Reduces fertility while on treatment.
**Not a fountain of youth:** Lifestyle (exercise, sleep, weight loss) often dramatically improves T and symptoms.

## Men and mental health

Men experience depression and anxiety but less likely to seek help. Different presentation: Anger, irritability, risk-taking rather than sadness.
**Male suicide rate:** 3-4× higher than women in US. Men less likely to seek help.
**Barriers:** Stigma ("men don't show weakness"), socialized self-reliance, fewer social support networks.
**Encouraging help-seeking:** Frame as strength and practical problem-solving. Online/app-based therapy reduces stigma barrier.
