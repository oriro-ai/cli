# Marketing Strategy

## STP framework (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning)

The foundation of all marketing strategy.

### Segmentation — divide the market

**Demographic:** Age, gender, income, education, family size.
**Geographic:** Country, region, city, climate.
**Psychographic:** Values, lifestyle, personality, interests.
**Behavioral:** Usage rate, loyalty, purchase occasion, benefits sought, stage in buyer journey.

The best segmentations combine multiple bases. A "high-income 35-50 year old professional who values efficiency and uses premium products" is more actionable than just "high income."

### Targeting — choose your segments

**Criteria for attractive segments:**
Measurable: Can you quantify the size?
Substantial: Large enough to be profitable?
Accessible: Can you reach them with your marketing?
Differentiable: Responds differently from other segments?
Actionable: Can you design effective programs?

**Targeting strategies:**
Mass marketing (undifferentiated): One offering for all. Commodities. Declining.
Differentiated: Different offerings for different segments. Most consumer brands.
Concentrated (niche): One segment, deeply. Most effective for startups.
Micro-marketing: Individual or local customization.

### Positioning — own a distinct place in minds

**Positioning statement format:**
"For [target segment] who [need/want], [Brand] is the [category] that [differentiating benefit] because [reason to believe]."

Example: "For ambitious young professionals who want to advance their careers, LinkedIn is the professional network that connects you with opportunities and relevant peers because it has 950M+ professionals and industry-leading hiring tools."

**Perceptual map:** Plot competitors on two relevant axes. Find gaps. Own a defensible position.

**Category entry points:** Which situations trigger category consideration? Be present at these moments.

## Go-to-market (GTM) strategy

**Sales-led:** Sales team drives acquisition. Works for complex, high-value B2B. High CAC, high LTV.
**Marketing-led:** Content, paid ads, SEO, events drive inbound demand. B2B mid-market. Lower CAC.
**Product-led (PLG):** Product is primary acquisition vehicle. Freemium, trials, virality. Lower CAC, faster scale. Best for self-serve products.
**Partnership-led:** Channels, resellers, integrations drive acquisition. Leverage others' distribution.

**GTM checklist:**

- Who is the ideal customer profile (ICP)?
- Where do they spend time / how do they buy?
- What is the messaging that resonates?
- What is the sales motion (inbound/outbound/PLG)?
- What are the key metrics and targets?

## Brand strategy

**Brand equity components:**
Awareness: Do they know you exist?
Associations: What do they associate with you?
Perceived quality: Do they believe you deliver quality?
Loyalty: Do they choose you repeatedly?

**Brand architecture:**
Branded house (Apple): Master brand drives all sub-products. Strong when master brand is strong.
House of brands (P&G): Each product has its own brand. Allows targeting different segments. Higher marketing cost.

**Positioning is not your tagline.** Positioning is a strategic choice that guides all marketing decisions.

## Customer acquisition

**Customer acquisition funnel:**
Awareness → Interest → Consideration → Intent → Purchase → Loyalty → Advocacy

**AIDA:** Attention → Interest → Desire → Action

**Channels by funnel stage:**
Top of funnel (awareness): Brand advertising, PR, content, social media, SEO (informational).
Middle of funnel (consideration): Case studies, comparisons, reviews, email, retargeting.
Bottom of funnel (decision): Demos, trials, sales, promos, SEO (transactional).

**Marketing mix (4Ps):**
Product: What are you selling?
Price: At what price? What does it signal?
Place: Where is it sold/distributed?
Promotion: How is it communicated and sold?

## Marketing measurement

**Attribution:** Which touchpoints get credit for conversions?
Last-click: 100% credit to final touchpoint. Misleads.
First-click: 100% credit to first touchpoint. Misleads differently.
Linear: Equal credit across all touchpoints.
Data-driven: Machine learning allocates based on actual contribution.

**Key metrics:**
CAC = Total marketing + sales spend / New customers acquired
LTV = Average revenue per customer × Gross margin × Average customer lifetime
Marketing-originated revenue %, Marketing-influenced revenue %
MQL → SQL → Opportunity → Closed-won conversion rates
