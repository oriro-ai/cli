# International Business Law

## Jurisdiction in international contracts

**Which law governs?** Specify explicitly in every international contract (governing law clause).
"This agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of [jurisdiction], without regard to conflict of law principles."
**Enforcement:** Court judgment in one country may be unenforceable in another. Consider: International arbitration for enforceability via New York Convention (170+ countries).
**ICC, LCIA, AAA/ICDR:** Leading international arbitration institutions. ICC most widely used.

## CISG (Vienna Convention on International Sale of Goods)

Automatically applies to contracts between parties from member countries (95+ countries including US, Germany, China).
Different from US UCC in several ways.
**Opt out if needed:** "This contract expressly excludes the application of the CISG."

## Anti-corruption compliance

**FCPA (US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act):** Prohibits US persons and US-listed companies from bribing foreign officials to obtain or retain business.
**UK Bribery Act:** Broader than FCPA. Applies to any company with UK nexus. Prohibits facilitation payments. Strict liability for commercial organizations that fail to prevent bribery.
**Consequences:** Criminal prosecution, massive fines, debarment from government contracts, reputational damage.
**Required:** Clear written anti-bribery policy, training, due diligence on third parties (agents, distributors), accurate books and records.

## Sanctions

**OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control):** US sanctions against countries, entities, individuals.
Prohibited: Transactions with Specially Designated Nationals (SDN list), Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Russia (extensive), etc.
**Consequences of violation:** Civil penalties up to $1M+ per violation. Criminal prosecution.
**Required:** Screen counterparties before transactions. OFAC SDN list search.
**EU, UK, UN sanctions:** Separate regimes. Companies with European operations must comply with multiple.

## Trade law

**WTO:** Rules-based international trading system. Most-favored-nation principle. Dispute settlement.
**Customs classification (HS codes):** Universal product classification. Determines tariff rates globally.
**USMCA (US-Mexico-Canada Agreement):** Rules of origin, IP protections, digital trade, investor-state dispute settlement.
**Export controls:** EAR (Export Administration Regulations) for dual-use goods. ITAR (International Traffic in Arms) for defense articles. License required for restricted items to restricted destinations.
**Country of origin:** Determines tariff treatment. Rules of origin define when substantial transformation occurs.
