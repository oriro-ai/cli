# Startup Operations

## Entity setup sequence

1. Register LLC or C-Corp (state filing, ~1-7 days for most states)
2. Get EIN (irs.gov, free, immediate)
3. Open business bank account (bring EIN + articles of incorporation)
4. Set up accounting (QuickBooks, Xero, or Wave for bootstrapped)
5. Register for state taxes if applicable
6. Set up payroll (Gusto, Rippling, or ADP TotalSource)

## Business banking

**Best options for startups:**
Mercury (mercury.com): Built for startups. No fees. Good API. Virtual cards. Preferred by VC-backed companies.
Relay: Good for multiple team members. Sub-accounts. No fees.
Brex: Corporate cards + banking. Requires VC backing typically. High limits.
Traditional: Chase Business, BofA Business — more bureaucracy but established.

**Don't use personal accounts for business.** Even sole proprietors should have a separate account. Required to maintain liability protection for entities.

## Accounting foundations

**Chart of accounts:** Set up correctly from the start. Much harder to fix retroactively.
**Bookkeeping:** Reconcile monthly. Categorize every transaction. Know where your money comes from and goes.
**Revenue recognition:** For SaaS — recognize monthly as service is delivered, not when cash received.
**Cash vs. accrual:** Cash is easier; accrual required for GAAP and useful for understanding business economics.
**Monthly close:** Close the books monthly. Produce P&L and cash flow statement. Review them.

## First hire decisions

**Contractor first:** Less commitment, faster, no benefits. Good for testing fit and need.
**Employee:** More control over how work is done, more commitment expected both ways, more legal complexity.
**Biggest early hire mistake:** Hiring too early (before proven need) or too late (burning out founders before hiring).

**Who to hire first (depends on the business):**

- If technical bottleneck: engineer
- If sales bottleneck: salesperson/BDR
- If operational bottleneck: ops generalist
- If founder bandwidth bottleneck: EA or chief of staff

## Remote work infrastructure

**Communication:** Slack/Teams for async; Zoom/Meet for sync; Loom for async video.
**Documentation:** Notion, Confluence, or GitHub wiki for permanent knowledge.
**Rule:** Default async. Synchronous time is precious. Reserve video calls for what truly requires them.
**Over-document:** Write down decisions, context, and rationale. Future hires will thank you.
**Time zones:** State your working hours. Set response time expectations. Respect evenings/weekends.

## Vendor and supplier management

**Master service agreements:** Standard contract with key vendors before project work starts.
**Net terms:** As a startup, you'll often pay net 15 or net 30. As you grow, push for net 60.
**Critical dependency:** Identify vendors where disruption would be severe. Have alternatives identified.
