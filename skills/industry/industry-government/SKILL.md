# Government and Civic Engagement

## How government procurement works (US federal)

### Federal acquisition process

**FAR (Federal Acquisition Regulation):** The rule book. All federal procurement must follow it.
**SAM.gov:** System for Award Management. Must register to do business with federal government.
**NAICS codes:** Your business classification. Determines small business status and set-aside eligibility.
**Small business set-asides:** Contracts reserved for small businesses. Also: 8(a) (disadvantaged), WOSB, SDVOSB, HUBZone.

**Solicitation types:**
RFI (Request for Information): Market research. No award.
RFQ (Request for Quotation): Price quote. Usually for simpler purchases.
RFP (Request for Proposal): Full proposals for complex work.
IFB (Invitation for Bid): Sealed bids; award to lowest responsive, responsible bidder.

**Key resources:** SAM.gov, USASpending.gov (track existing contracts), beta.SAM.gov for opportunities, FedBizOpps is now part of SAM.

### State and local procurement

Generally similar structure but each state/locality has its own rules.
Often listed on state/local websites. Many use third-party portals (DemandStar, Bonfire).

## Regulatory processes

### Federal rulemaking (Administrative Procedure Act)

**Proposed rule:** Agency publishes proposed regulation in Federal Register. Public comment period opens (usually 30-90 days).
**Public comments:** Anyone can submit. Comment quality matters — substantive comments with evidence carry more weight than form letters.
**Final rule:** Agency reviews comments, publishes final rule with response to significant comments.
**Effective date:** Usually 30-60 days after publication.

**Comment strategy:** Identify the specific regulatory question → Provide factual evidence → Explain real-world impact → Propose specific alternative language → Submit before deadline.
**Regulatory docket:** All comments published on regulations.gov (federal). Completely public.

## Working with elected officials

**Purpose:** Legislative priorities, appropriations, oversight, constituent services.
**Who to contact:** For federal legislation: your two senators + your representative.
For federal agency: whichever agency has jurisdiction + relevant committee members.
**How to engage:**
Written letters: Personalized, specific, from constituents.
Meetings (in-person or virtual): Most effective. Bring one-pager. Respect their time (15-30 min max).
Constituent events: Town halls, district visits.
Coalition letters: Multiple organizations amplify message.

**What works:** Local constituent voices. Specific asks. Economic impact in their district. Real human stories. Willingness to follow up with information.
**What doesn't work:** Generic form letters. Asking for too many things at once. Not following up.

## Freedom of Information (FOIA)

Public has right to access federal government records.
Submit to the specific agency that holds the records.
Response time: 20 business days (often longer in practice).
Common exemptions: Classified information, personal privacy, law enforcement, trade secrets, attorney-client privilege.
If denied: Administrative appeal, then federal court.
State equivalents: Every state has similar open records laws (FOIA, sunshine laws, public records acts).
