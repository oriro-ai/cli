# Trauma-Informed Psychology

## Understanding trauma

Trauma is any experience that overwhelms a person's ability to cope and leaves lasting effects on their functioning and worldbeing. It is defined by the impact on the person, not the event itself.

**Types:** Single incident (accident, assault), Complex trauma (repeated, interpersonal — childhood abuse, domestic violence), Developmental trauma (early childhood adversity), Secondary/vicarious trauma (indirect exposure — first responders, therapists), Historical/collective trauma.

**The nervous system in trauma:**
Trauma leaves an imprint on the body's stress response systems.
Fight: aggression, anger, confrontation.
Flight: avoidance, escape, anxiety.
Freeze: numbness, dissociation, inability to move or speak.
Fawn: appease others to avoid threat; people-pleasing as survival.

**Hyperarousal vs. hypoarousal:**
Hyperarousal: anxiety, hypervigilance, startle, insomnia — nervous system over-activated.
Hypoarousal: numbness, dissociation, shutdown, depression — nervous system collapsed.
Window of tolerance: state between extremes where processing is possible.

## Trauma responses that look like "bad behavior"

Many trauma responses are misread as personality flaws or defiance:

- Emotional dysregulation: difficulty managing intense emotions
- Trust difficulties: seeming withdrawn, guarded, or hostile
- Avoidance: refusing activities, absenteeism
- Risk-taking: as dissociation or numbness
- Overachievement: hypervigilance through performance
- Physical symptoms: unexplained pain, fatigue, illness

The question is not "What's wrong with this person?" but "What happened to this person?"

## Grounding techniques

Used to return to present when triggered or dissociating.

**5-4-3-2-1 sensory grounding:**
Name 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell, 1 you can taste.
Anchors attention in the present sensory environment.

**Physiological sigh:**
Double inhale through nose (inhale, then sniff again), long extended exhale through mouth.
Most rapid way to downregulate the autonomic nervous system. Proven in Stanford research.

**Box breathing:**
Inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Repeat 4-8 cycles.
Used by Navy SEALs for stress regulation.

**Cold water:** Run cold water over wrists or splash on face. Activates dive reflex, slows heart rate.

**Physical grounding:** Press feet firmly into floor. Feel gravity. Describe physical sensations in detail.

## Supporting someone who has experienced trauma

**What helps:**

- Believe them without interrogating details.
- Ask what support they need — don't assume.
- "I'm here with you" rather than "I know how you feel."
- Maintain their sense of control — ask before touching, before discussing the trauma.
- Be present; don't need to fix or solve.
- Long-term availability; trauma recovery is not linear.

**What hurts:**

- "Just get over it / it was a long time ago"
- Minimizing: "It wasn't that bad"
- "Why didn't you just leave/fight back/say no" — ignores the neurobiological reality of trauma responses
- Probing for details they're not ready to share
- Making their disclosure about your feelings
- Disappearing after the initial disclosure

## Trauma therapy options

**Trauma-Focused CBT (TF-CBT):** Evidence-based for children and adolescents.
**EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing):** Processing trauma memories with bilateral stimulation. Strong evidence for PTSD.
**Prolonged Exposure (PE):** Gradual engagement with trauma memories and avoided situations.
**CPT (Cognitive Processing Therapy):** Challenges stuck points — distorted beliefs about the trauma.
**Somatic therapies:** Body-based approaches addressing trauma stored in the body (SE, EMDR, Sensorimotor).
**DBT:** For trauma with significant emotional dysregulation and self-harm.

None of these can be effectively done from a skills file — they require a trained therapist.
