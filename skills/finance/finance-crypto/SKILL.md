# Digital Assets and Blockchain

## How blockchain works

A blockchain is a distributed ledger — a database copied across thousands of computers (nodes).
Each block contains: transaction data + timestamp + cryptographic hash of previous block.
Changing any past block invalidates all subsequent blocks → tamper-evident.

**Consensus mechanisms:**
Proof of Work (PoW): Miners compete to solve computational puzzle; winner adds block. Bitcoin uses this. Energy-intensive but battle-tested.
Proof of Stake (PoS): Validators lock up (stake) tokens for right to add blocks; chosen by weighted probability. Ethereum moved to this in 2022. Much more energy-efficient.

**Public vs. Private keys:**
Private key: Secret number that proves ownership. Never share. Lose it = lose your crypto.
Public key (wallet address): Like a bank account number. Share to receive crypto.
No bank, no recovery, no customer service. Custody = responsibility.

## Major cryptocurrencies

**Bitcoin (BTC):**
Fixed supply of 21 million. No central authority. Store of value proposition.
Halving every 4 years reduces new supply. Known difficulty and emission schedule.
Most liquid, longest track record, most institutional adoption.

**Ethereum (ETH):**
Smart contract platform. Enables DeFi, NFTs, DAOs, and most of Web3.
Not just a currency — it's programmable money infrastructure.
EIP-1559 and PoS transition made ETH deflationary under high usage.

**Stablecoins:**
Pegged to USD (or other assets). Designed to not fluctuate.
USDC (Circle): Regulated, backed by cash and Treasuries. Most trusted.
USDT (Tether): Largest by volume; some reserve transparency concerns.
DAI: Algorithmic, collateral-backed. Overcollateralized.
Algorithmic stablecoins (Terra/LUNA): Collapsed in 2022 — avoid.

## DeFi (Decentralized Finance)

Financial services without intermediaries, running on smart contracts.

**DEX (Decentralized Exchange):** Trade crypto without a centralized exchange. Uniswap, Curve.
**Lending protocols:** Borrow against crypto collateral or lend to earn yield. Aave, Compound.
**Yield farming:** Provide liquidity for fees and rewards. High yields = high risk.

**DeFi risks:**
Smart contract bugs: Billions lost to hacks.
Impermanent loss: Liquidity provision can result in less value than holding.
Rug pulls: Project team abandons and takes funds.
Liquidation: Falling collateral value triggers forced liquidation.

## NFTs (Non-Fungible Tokens)

Unique tokens on a blockchain. Proof of ownership of a digital (or sometimes physical) item.
**Limitations:** Owning an NFT usually means owning a record on a blockchain, not the underlying file.
Most NFT projects have seen 90%+ value declines. Highly speculative.

## Custody

**Centralized exchanges (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken):** Easy to use. Not your keys, not your coins. Exchange failures (FTX, Mt. Gox) = user funds lost.
**Hardware wallets (Ledger, Trezor):** Private keys stored offline. Most secure for significant amounts. Requires safe backup of seed phrase.
**Seed phrase:** 12 or 24 words that can recover your wallet. Store offline in multiple secure locations. NEVER photograph, email, or store digitally.

## Responsible approach

1. Only invest what you can afford to lose completely — crypto is speculative
2. Understand what you own before buying
3. Use regulated exchanges with strong security
4. Self-custody significant amounts
5. Don't fall for "guaranteed returns" — all yield has corresponding risk
6. Tax implications: Crypto trades are taxable events in most jurisdictions

## Regulation landscape

US: SEC considers many tokens securities (subject to securities law). CFTC has jurisdiction over commodities (BTC, ETH). FinCEN regulates exchanges as money service businesses. Unclear framework ongoing.
EU: MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets) provides clearer framework from 2024.
Globally: Ranges from embrace (El Salvador, UAE) to prohibition (China).
