# Personal Finance

## The financial order of operations

Do these in order. Do not jump ahead.

```
Step 1: Emergency fund (1 month expenses)
Step 2: Get full employer 401k match (free money)
Step 3: Pay off high-interest debt (>7% interest)
Step 4: Build emergency fund (3-6 months expenses)
Step 5: Max HSA if available (triple tax advantage)
Step 6: Max Roth IRA ($7,000/year in 2024)
Step 7: Max 401k ($23,000/year in 2024)
Step 8: Invest in taxable brokerage
Step 9: Pay off moderate debt (4-7%)
Step 10: Low-interest debt (mortgage) — optional extra payments
```

## Budgeting systems

### 50/30/20 Rule

Simple starting framework:

- **50%** needs: housing, utilities, groceries, transport, minimum debt payments
- **30%** wants: dining out, entertainment, hobbies, subscriptions
- **20%** savings and extra debt payments

### Zero-Based Budget

Every dollar assigned a purpose. Income - all categories = $0.
Apps: YNAB (You Need A Budget), EveryDollar.

### Pay Yourself First

Automate savings on payday before spending.
Spend whatever remains guilt-free.
Requires knowing your minimum monthly expenses first.

### The anti-budget

Track only your savings rate.
Save X% first. Spend the rest however you want.
Works for people who find detailed budgets unsustainable.

## Emergency fund

**Target:** 3-6 months of essential living expenses

- Stable job + dual income household: 3 months
- Variable income / single income / risky industry: 6 months

**Where to keep it:**
High-yield savings account (HYSA) — not your checking account, not the stock market.
Reason: must be immediately accessible without penalty; stocks can be down 40% when you need it.

**2024 HYSA rates:** 4.5-5.5% APY at online banks (Ally, Marcus, SoFi, etc.)

**How to build it:**

- Open a separate HYSA at a different bank
- Automate a transfer after each paycheck
- Tax refunds, bonuses → directly to emergency fund until target reached
- Do NOT touch it for non-emergencies

## Debt management

### Avalanche method (mathematically optimal)

Pay minimums on all debts.
Put all extra money toward highest interest rate debt first.
Once paid off, roll that payment to next highest rate.

Example:

```
Credit card A: $3,000 @ 22% interest ← attack first
Credit card B: $5,000 @ 18% interest
Car loan:     $12,000 @ 6% interest
Student loans: $25,000 @ 5% interest
```

### Snowball method (psychologically motivating)

Pay minimums on all debts.
Put all extra money toward smallest balance first.
Momentum from quick wins helps people stick with it.

**Both work. Avalanche saves more money. Snowball has better completion rates.**
Use avalanche if you're disciplined; snowball if you need wins to stay motivated.

### When to prioritize debt vs. investing

**Always pay high-interest debt first (>7%)** — no investment reliably returns more than 22% interest
**Invest before paying low-interest debt (<4%)** — stock market historically returns ~7-10%/year
**Gray zone (4-7%):** Personal preference; generally invest if match is available, then pay debt

## Credit scores

### How FICO scores are calculated

| Factor                     | Weight |
| -------------------------- | ------ |
| Payment history            | 35%    |
| Amounts owed (utilization) | 30%    |
| Length of credit history   | 15%    |
| Credit mix                 | 10%    |
| New credit inquiries       | 10%    |

### Score ranges

- 800-850: Exceptional — best rates on everything
- 740-799: Very good — excellent rates
- 670-739: Good — most approvals, decent rates
- 580-669: Fair — higher rates, some denials
- Below 580: Poor — limited options, secured cards

### How to improve your score

**Biggest impact — payment history (35%):**

- Never miss a payment. Set autopay for minimum.
- Even one 30-day late payment can drop score 50-100 points.
- Missed payments stay on report 7 years.

**Second biggest — credit utilization (30%):**

- Keep utilization below 30% of each card limit. Below 10% is optimal.
- Utilization snapshot matters — pay down before the statement closing date.
- If card has $5,000 limit, keep balance ≤$500 for best score.

**Quick wins:**

- Become authorized user on a family member's good-history card
- Request credit limit increases (don't change spending — reduces utilization %)
- Don't close old accounts (reduces average age of history)
- Space out applications (each hard inquiry drops score ~5 points for ~1 year)

**Check your report (free):**
annualcreditreport.com — official source. Once/year free from each bureau.
Dispute errors: ~20% of credit reports have errors that affect scores.

## Banking basics

### Account types

**Checking:** Day-to-day transactions, bills, debit card. Keep 1-2 months expenses.
**Savings/HYSA:** Emergency fund, short-term savings goals.
**Money Market:** Higher yield than savings; check-writing ability.
**CDs (Certificates of Deposit):** Fixed term (3 months to 5 years), higher rate than savings, penalty for early withdrawal. Good for money you won't need for a defined period.

### FDIC insurance

Bank accounts are insured up to $250,000 per depositor per bank.
If bank fails, your money up to $250,000 is protected.
Credit unions: NCUA provides same protection.
Brokerage accounts: Not FDIC insured, but SIPC protects against broker failure (not investment losses).

### How to choose a bank

- **Online banks** (Ally, Marcus, SoFi): Higher interest rates, lower fees, no branches
- **Credit unions:** Member-owned, often lower fees, better rates, community focus
- **Big banks** (Chase, BofA): Convenient branches/ATMs, full service, usually lower rates

**Red flags to avoid:**

- Monthly maintenance fees you can't waive
- Minimum balance requirements you'll struggle to maintain
- High overdraft fees (>$35)
- No free ATM network

## Insurance — the basics

Insurance protects against catastrophic losses, not small ones.
The point is to avoid financial ruin, not to break even on premiums.

### Health insurance

- **Premium:** Monthly payment whether you use it or not
- **Deductible:** You pay first before insurance kicks in
- **Copay:** Fixed amount per visit
- **Coinsurance:** Your % share after deductible (e.g., 20%)
- **Out-of-pocket maximum:** Most you'll ever pay in a year — then 100% covered

**ACA marketplace:** shop at healthcare.gov during open enrollment.
Subsidies available for income 100-400% of federal poverty level.

### Term life insurance

**Who needs it:** Anyone with dependents (spouse, children) who rely on your income.
**Who doesn't:** Single with no dependents; financially independent.

**How much:** 10-12× your annual income is a common recommendation.
**Term:** Match your coverage period to your dependency period (20-30 years typical).
**Cost:** Healthy 30-year-old, $500K, 20-year term ≈ $25-35/month.

**Buy term, invest the difference** — whole life insurance is rarely the right choice.

### Auto insurance

Required in all states except New Hampshire.
**Liability:** Covers damage you cause to others. Required. Higher limits than minimum required.
**Collision:** Covers your car in an accident. Worth it if your car is worth >5× annual premium.
**Comprehensive:** Non-collision damage (theft, weather, animals). Similar threshold.
**Uninsured/underinsured motorist:** Strongly recommended.

**Deductible:** Higher deductible = lower premium. $500-1,000 if you have emergency fund.

### Renters/homeowners insurance

Renters: ~$15-30/month. Protects your belongings (up to $30-50K typical). Also liability.
Homeowners: Required by lenders. Protects structure, belongings, liability.

## Net worth — the most important financial number

**Net Worth = Assets - Liabilities**

Assets: Cash, investments, retirement accounts, home equity, valuable property
Liabilities: Mortgage balance, car loans, student loans, credit card balances, other debt

Track monthly. The trend matters more than the number.
Millionaire threshold: $1M net worth. 3.5% of US households.

**Wealth benchmarks by age** (rough guidelines from Fidelity):

- Age 30: 1× salary saved
- Age 40: 3× salary saved
- Age 50: 6× salary saved
- Age 60: 8× salary saved
- Age 67: 10× salary saved

Most people are behind. The answer is always the same: save more, earlier.
Time + compound interest is the only reliable path.
