# Corporate Strategy

## Portfolio strategy (BCG Growth-Share Matrix)

Framework for managing a portfolio of business units:
**Stars:** High growth market + high market share. Invest to maintain leadership.
**Cash Cows:** Low growth + high share. Generate cash, invest minimally, fund Stars.
**Question Marks:** High growth + low share. Invest selectively or divest. Most become Dogs.
**Dogs:** Low growth + low share. Consider divesting. Rarely worth saving.

**Application:** Allocate capital across portfolio based on matrix position. Harvest Cash Cows to fund Stars and selected Question Marks.

## Growth strategy options

### Ansoff matrix

**Market penetration:** Existing products, existing markets. Increase share. Lowest risk.
**Market development:** Existing products, new markets (geography, segment). Moderate risk.
**Product development:** New products, existing markets. Moderate risk.
**Diversification:** New products, new markets. Highest risk. Related (synergies) vs. Unrelated (conglomerate).

**Corporate strategy research finding:** Related diversification outperforms unrelated diversification. "Stick to the knitting."

### Organic vs. inorganic growth

**Organic:** Build internally. Slower, more controlled, lower risk, maintains culture.
**M&A:** Acquire capabilities, markets, technology. Faster. Higher risk. Most acquisitions destroy value.

**Acquisitions destroy value for acquirer when:**

- Overpaying (premium too high)
- Synergies don't materialize (most don't, at full expectations)
- Culture clash post-acquisition
- Distraction from core business
- Talent flees after acquisition

**When acquisitions create value:**

- Bolt-on acquisitions of complementary businesses
- Acqui-hires (buying talent/technology)
- Consolidation plays with clear cost synergies
- Geographic expansion into markets with proven product

## Corporate advantage

**Rationale:** Why is this combination of businesses worth more together than separately?
Sources of corporate advantage:

- Shared resources (manufacturing, distribution, procurement)
- Transferred capabilities (management practices, brand)
- Coordinated activities (complementary business units)
- Business building (incubating new businesses)
  Without corporate advantage, the sum is worth less than the parts — the conglomerate discount.

## Capital allocation

Peter Lynch: "The key to making money in stocks is not to get scared out of them."
Buffett: "Our favorite holding period is forever." But: businesses where capital can be reinvested at high returns.
**Discipline:** Only invest in businesses/projects where expected return exceeds cost of capital.
**When to return capital:** If no investment opportunities exceed WACC, return to shareholders (dividends, buybacks).
