# Food and Beverage Industry

## Restaurant economics

### The basic P&L

**Revenue:** Food sales + beverage sales + catering + merchandise.
**COGS (Cost of Goods Sold):** Food cost + beverage cost. Target: 28-35% food, 18-25% beverage.
**Labor:** Kitchen + front of house + management. Target: 30-35% of revenue.
**Occupancy:** Rent + utilities. Target: 5-10%.
**Other operating expenses:** Marketing, supplies, repairs, insurance, fees.
**Prime cost:** Food cost + beverage cost + labor = most controllable costs. Target: 55-65%.
**EBITDA margin:** 10-15% is considered healthy. Under 5% = struggling.
**Average check:** Revenue / Covers. Track and grow through upselling and menu engineering.

### Table turns and capacity

**Turn time:** Average time per table per service. Lunch: 45-60 min. Dinner: 60-90 min.
**Covers per day:** Tables × Seats per table × Turns × Occupancy %.
**Revenue potential:** Covers × Average check.

## Menu engineering (Boston matrix approach)

**Stars:** High popularity + high profit margin. Promote these.
**Plowhorses:** High popularity + low profit margin. Find ways to increase margin or menu placement to focus on stars.
**Puzzles:** Low popularity + high profit margin. Reposition, rename, or describe better.
**Dogs:** Low popularity + low profit margin. Remove or replace.

**Strategic menu design:** Limited menus outperform long menus (Paradox of choice). 4-7 items per category optimal. High-margin items placed upper right (eye tracking). Boxes, colors, photos draw attention to featured items.

## Operations

### Kitchen design and workflow

**Brigade system (Escoffier):** Executive Chef → Sous Chef → Chef de Partie → Commis.
**Stations:** Hot line, cold/garde manger, pastry, prep.
**Flow:** Minimize backtracking. Receiving → Storage → Prep → Service → Waste.

### Inventory and cost control

**Yield percentage:** Usable product after trimming / AP (as purchased) weight. A 10lb beef tenderloin with 25% yield loss = 7.5 lbs usable. True cost = AP price / Yield %.
**Recipe costing:** Cost each ingredient at portion size. Total = recipe cost. Per-plate cost = recipe cost / servings.
**Variance tracking:** Theoretical food cost (based on sales mix × recipe costs) vs. actual food cost. Variance > 2-3% triggers investigation.
**Par levels:** Minimum stock levels. Reorder before hitting par.

## Staffing challenges

**Turnover:** Restaurant industry averages 70-80% annual turnover. Extremely costly.
**Tipping culture (US):** Servers earn 40-60%+ of pay from tips. Kitchen/BOH disparity. Tip pooling increasingly common.
**Scheduling:** Variable demand. Optimal scheduling software balances labor cost with coverage.
**Training:** Consistent training programs reduce errors and increase retention.

## Food safety and regulations

See trades-cooking-pro skill for HACCP detail.
**Health inspections:** Unannounced in most jurisdictions. Scoring systems vary. Critical violations (immediate health risk) can result in closure.
**Alcohol licensing (US):** State-controlled. Liquor licenses can be extremely valuable (limited issuance) or relatively accessible (by state).
