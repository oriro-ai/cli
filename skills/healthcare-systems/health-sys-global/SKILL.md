# Global Health

## Global disease burden

**Leading causes of death globally (WHO 2019):**

1. Ischemic heart disease (16%)
2. Stroke (11%)
3. COPD (6%)
4. Lower respiratory infections (6%)
5. Neonatal conditions (5%)
6. Trachea/bronchus/lung cancers (3%)
7. Alzheimer's disease (3%)

**DALY (Disability-Adjusted Life Year):** One year of healthy life lost. Combines years lost to premature death and years lived with disability. Better measure of burden than mortality alone.

**Epidemiological transition:** As countries develop, burden shifts from communicable to non-communicable diseases. Low-income countries increasingly face double burden of both.

## Major global health challenges

### Infectious diseases still dominant

**HIV/AIDS:** ~37 million people living with HIV. 1.5 million new infections/year (2021). Sub-Saharan Africa: ~70% of global burden. ART (antiretroviral therapy) transformed from death sentence to chronic disease management. 28 million on treatment.
**Malaria:** ~247 million cases, ~619,000 deaths (2021, mostly under-5 children, Africa). RTS,S/AS01 vaccine (Mosquirix) and R21/Matrix-M vaccine: First malaria vaccines — 75-77% efficacy in Phase 3.
**Tuberculosis:** #2 infectious disease killer. Drug-resistant TB growing concern. Latent TB: ~2 billion infected.
**Neglected Tropical Diseases (NTDs):** Affect 1.7 billion people. Onchocerciasis, lymphatic filariasis, schistosomiasis, soil-transmitted helminths. Often preventable/treatable with low-cost interventions.

## Health systems frameworks

**WHO six building blocks:**

1. Health service delivery
2. Health workforce
3. Health information systems
4. Access to essential medicines
5. Health financing
6. Leadership and governance

**Universal Health Coverage (UHC):** All people have access to quality health services without financial hardship. Core SDG target (3.8). 3.3-3.6 billion people lack access to essential health services.

## Global health financing

**Total global health spending:** ~$8.5 trillion/year.
**Official Development Assistance (ODA) for health:** ~$40-50 billion/year.
**Key institutions:** WHO, GAVI (vaccines, $15B+ committed), Global Fund (HIV/TB/malaria, $50B+ to date), UNITAID, PEPFAR ($110B+ committed by US, most by any government for a single disease).

**Global health R&D:** Under-investment in diseases of the poor (neglected diseases). Less than 10% of global health R&D addresses 90% of global disease burden (10/90 gap). BARDA, CEPI for pandemic preparedness. Pooled procurement (PAHO revolving fund, Medicines Patent Pool).

## Health equity

**Social gradient in health:** Better health outcomes at every step up the socioeconomic ladder. Not just poor vs. rich binary.
**Structural racism and health:** Race-based health disparities are documented in nearly every health indicator. Produced by historically rooted social structures.
**One Health:** Human health, animal health, and environmental health are interconnected. Zoonotic diseases (COVID-19, Ebola, avian flu, HIV) emerge at human-animal interface. Antimicrobial resistance requires cross-sector response.
