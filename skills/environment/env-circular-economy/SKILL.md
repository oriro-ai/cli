# Circular Economy

## Circular vs. linear economy

**Linear:** Take (extract resources) → Make (manufacture) → Dispose (landfill/incinerate).
**Circular:** Eliminate waste and pollution; keep products and materials in use; regenerate natural systems.
The goal is not just recycling — that's the last resort in the hierarchy.

## The butterfly diagram (Ellen MacArthur Foundation)

Two circles: biological and technical materials.
**Technical cycle:** Products and materials flow at their highest value.
Maintain/repair → Reuse/redistribute → Refurbish/remanufacture → Recycle.
Higher loops = more value preserved.

**Biological cycle:** Consumable materials designed to re-enter the biosphere safely.
Composting, anaerobic digestion → soil amendment.
Biodegradable packaging example: decomposes without toxic residue.

## Circular strategies (R hierarchy, highest to lowest value)

**Refuse:** Redesign to eliminate the need for a material entirely.
**Reduce:** Use less material.
**Reuse:** Same product, same function, different user.
**Repair:** Restore to working condition.
**Remanufacture:** Restore to original specifications with warranty.
**Repurpose:** Use for different function (glass bottle → vase).
**Recycle:** Recover material (loses quality — "downcycling" is more accurate for most plastics).
**Recover:** Energy recovery (incineration — last resort before landfill).

## Circular business models

**Product as a service:** Sell access/performance, not the product. Manufacturer retains ownership → designs for durability, repair, remanufacture. Rolls-Royce "Power by the Hour" (jet engines).
**Reverse logistics:** Take back products at end of life. Patagonia Worn Wear, HP cartridge return.
**Industrial symbiosis:** One company's waste is another's input. Kalundborg, Denmark industrial ecology park.
**Sharing/rental platforms:** AirBnB, tool libraries, Rent the Runway (fashion).
**Remanufacturing:** Restore used products to original specs. Caterpillar remanufactured parts.

## Design for circularity

**Design principles:**

- Design for disassembly: Can be taken apart efficiently at end of life
- Mono-material: Avoid mixed materials that are hard to separate for recycling
- Non-toxic chemicals: Safe to cycle
- Durable: Lasts longer
- Modular: Components can be upgraded/replaced individually
- Standardized: Interchangeable parts

## Circular economy metrics

Material circularity indicator (MCI): Ellen MacArthur Foundation tool for products and companies.
Recycled content %: How much recycled material goes in.
Recyclability %: How much can be recovered at end of life.
Waste to landfill reduction.
Product lifetime extension.

Sources: Ellen MacArthur Foundation (ellenmacarthurfoundation.org — free, comprehensive), WRAP (wrap.org.uk — free reports), European Commission Circular Economy Action Plan (free)
