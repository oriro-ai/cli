# Sleep Science

## Sleep architecture

Sleep cycles last ~90 minutes. 4-6 cycles per night.
**NREM Stage 1 (5%):** Light sleep. Easy to wake.
**NREM Stage 2 (45%):** Temperature drops, heart rate slows. Memory consolidation begins. Sleep spindles.
**NREM Stage 3/SWS (25%):** Deep sleep. Growth hormone release. Physical restoration. Hardest to wake. Most restorative for body.
**REM (25%):** Rapid Eye Movement. Dreaming. Emotional processing. Memory consolidation. Brain activity near-waking levels.

**Sleep pressure and circadian rhythm:** Two processes regulate sleep timing.
Adenosine builds during waking (sleep pressure — caffeine blocks adenosine).
Circadian clock driven by light exposure controls alertness cycle.
Both must align for optimal sleep.

## How much sleep you need

Adults: 7-9 hours (most need 8; few genuinely function on 6).
Teens: 8-10 hours. Children 6-12: 9-12 hours.
**You cannot sleep train yourself to need less.** People who claim to function on 5-6 hours are typically impaired and unaware of it (studies show self-reported vs. objective performance diverge dramatically).

## Sleep hygiene — evidence-based practices

### The environment

**Cool room:** 65-68°F (18-20°C). Body must drop core temperature to initiate sleep.
**Dark:** Blackout curtains or sleep mask. Light at 10 lux (dim room light) delays melatonin by 1.5 hours.
**Quiet:** White noise if needed.

### Timing and habits

**Fixed wake time:** Wake the same time every day (including weekends). Most important sleep hygiene factor. Anchors your circadian rhythm.
**No caffeine after 2pm:** Caffeine has ~5-7 hour half-life. Afternoon coffee stays in your system.
**Limit alcohol:** Alcohol causes sedation but disrupts REM sleep. Net sleep quality loss.
**No screens 30-60 min before bed:** Blue light delays melatonin. Even if device has "night mode" — the stimulation itself matters.
**Wind-down routine:** Same pre-sleep sequence. Signals brain that sleep is coming.
**Don't lie in bed awake:** If awake >20 minutes, get up, do something quiet in dim light, return when sleepy. Prevents bed becoming associated with wakefulness.

## Insomnia

**Defined:** Difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep ≥3 nights/week for ≥3 months, causing daytime impairment.
**Prevalence:** ~10% chronic insomnia; 30-35% experience occasional insomnia.

**CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia):** First-line treatment. More effective than sleeping pills long-term.
Core components: Sleep restriction (initially limit time in bed), stimulus control (bed only for sleep/sex), relaxation training, cognitive restructuring.
**Sleeping pills:** Short-term use only. Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs cause rebound insomnia. Don't address root causes.

## Sleep apnea

**Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA):** Upper airway collapses repeatedly during sleep.
**Signs:** Loud snoring, gasping/choking, witnessed breathing pauses, excessive daytime sleepiness, morning headaches, partner complaints.
**Risk factors:** Obesity, male sex, age, neck size, craniofacial anatomy.
**Diagnosis:** Sleep study (polysomnography — in lab or home sleep test).
**Treatment:** CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure) — highly effective. Oral appliances for mild-moderate. Weight loss. Positional therapy.
Untreated OSA: Hypertension, cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cognitive impairment.

## Shift work and jet lag

**Jet lag:** Cross 2+ time zones. Body clock lags behind local time.
Eastward travel (losing time) harder than westward.
Recovery: ~1 day per time zone.
Melatonin: 0.5-3mg at local bedtime helps reset. Timing matters more than dose.

**Shift work:** Chronic misalignment between circadian clock and work schedule.
Associated with: Metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, cancer, depression.
Strategies: Strategic light exposure, blackout sleep environment, melatonin, consistency in schedule.
