# African Education Systems

## West Africa — WAEC/NECO (Nigeria, Ghana, Sierra Leone, Gambia, Liberia)

### WAEC (West African Examinations Council)

May/June WASSCE: End of secondary school exam. Most widely recognized.
**Subjects:** English Language, Mathematics (core). Plus electives — typically 7-9 subjects.
**Grades:** A1 (75-100), B2, B3, C4, C5, C6 (pass), D7, E8 (fail), F9 (fail).
Minimum for university: 5 credits including English and Mathematics.

### NECO (National Examinations Council) — Nigeria only

Similar to WAEC. Accepted alongside WAEC by Nigerian universities.

### JAMB (Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board) — Nigeria

Additional university entrance exam. 0-400 score. Minimum 180 for most universities.
**Cut-off marks:** University of Lagos, Ibadan, OAU: 200+.

## East Africa — Kenya (KCSE)

### KCSE (Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education)

12 subjects. Grades: A (12 points) to E (1 point). Maximum 84 points.
**University admission:** Minimum C+. Competitive courses: A-A-.
Top universities: University of Nairobi, Kenyatta University, Strathmore.

## Southern Africa — South Africa (NSC)

### NSC (National Senior Certificate / Matric)

7 subjects. Grades 1-7 (7 = >80%, 1 = <30%).
Bachelor's pass: 3 subjects at 50%+, 3 others at 40%+, 2 remaining at 30%+.
**Top universities:** UCT, Stellenbosch, Wits, UKZN.
**APS (Admission Point Score):** University minimum thresholds.

## Multilingual education challenges

54 African countries, 2,000+ languages. Official colonial languages (French, English, Portuguese, Arabic) dominate higher education.
**Mother tongue instruction research:** Shows better outcomes in early years. Policy tension with economic value of colonial languages.
African Union: "Agenda 2063" includes multilingual education promotion.
