# Leadership

## What leadership is (and is not)

Leadership = Influence toward a shared goal.
Management = Coordinate work. Leadership = Inspire work.
Both matter. At senior levels, leadership > management.

**Drucker:** Management is doing things right. Leadership is doing the right things.

## Leadership styles that work

### Situational leadership (Hersey & Blanchard)

Match your style to the follower's development level on each specific task.
D1 (enthusiastic beginner): High direction, low support. Tell them exactly what to do.
D2 (disillusioned learner): High direction + high support. Explain why + involve them.
D3 (capable but cautious): Low direction, high support. Encourage, listen, ask.
D4 (self-reliant achiever): Low direction, low support. Delegate fully.
Error: One style for all. The same person may need different styles for different tasks.

### Transformational leadership

Inspires through vision, values, and intrinsic motivation.
Four Is: Idealized influence (role model), Inspirational motivation (compelling vision), Intellectual stimulation (challenge assumptions), Individualized consideration (develop each person).
Most effective for high-complexity, high-change environments.

## Making decisions

**Levels of certainty:**
Clear problems with known solutions: Apply best practice. (Cynefin: Simple/Obvious)
Complicated problems: Expert analysis, consider options. (Cynefin: Complicated)
Complex problems: Probe-sense-respond. Run experiments. (Cynefin: Complex)
Chaos: Act first to stabilize, then sense. (Cynefin: Chaotic)

**Decision-making frameworks:**
OODA Loop: Observe → Orient → Decide → Act (military, fast-cycle decisions)
Pre-mortem: Imagine it's one year later and the decision failed. What went wrong?
Reversible vs. irreversible: Fast-cycle reversible decisions; deliberate on irreversible (Bezos: "Type 1 vs. Type 2 decisions").
10-10-10: How will I feel about this in 10 minutes / 10 months / 10 years?

## Developing others

**Coaching vs. mentoring vs. directing:**
Coaching: Ask questions to help them find their own answers. Best for capable, motivated people.
Mentoring: Share your experience and advice. Best for career development.
Directing: Tell them what to do. Best for new or struggling people on specific tasks.

**GROW coaching model:**
G — Goal: What do you want to achieve?
R — Reality: Where are you now? What's happening?
O — Options: What could you do? What else?
W — Will: What will you do? When? Who else needs to know?

**Delegation:** Assign work → Grant authority → Hold accountable. All three required.
Common failure: Assign work without authority (micromanagement follows), or assign without accountability (completion optional).

**Feedback:** See organizational psychology skill. SBI model. Radical candor.

## Leading organizational change

**Kotter's 8-step change model:**

1. Create urgency: Why must we change? What happens if we don't?
2. Build a guiding coalition: Who are the key influencers?
3. Form strategic vision: Where are we going?
4. Enlist a volunteer army: Who will carry the change forward?
5. Enable action by removing barriers
6. Generate short-term wins: Demonstrate early results
7. Sustain acceleration: Keep momentum, learn from experience
8. Institute change: Anchor in culture and systems

**Resistance to change:** Not irrational. People resist loss (of control, of competence, of relationships).
Address: What are you losing? Is that real or perceived? How will we support you?

## Communication for leaders

**The leader's communication responsibilities:**
Context: Why are we doing this? What's the bigger picture?
Clarity: What exactly are we doing? Who does what?
Consistency: Same message across all audiences.
Candor: Tell the truth, even when hard. Silence is not neutral.

**Delivering difficult messages:**
Lead with the main point. Don't bury bad news.
Separate the event from the emotion. Name both.
Focus forward: What do we do now?
Avoid euphemisms that obscure reality.
