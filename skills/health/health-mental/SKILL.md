# Mental Health Fundamentals

## Common mental health conditions

### Depression (Major Depressive Disorder)

**Diagnostic criteria (DSM-5):** 5+ of these symptoms for 2+ weeks, including #1 or #2:

1. Depressed mood most of the day
2. Loss of interest or pleasure in activities
3. Significant weight/appetite change
4. Sleep disturbance (too much or too little)
5. Psychomotor agitation or slowing (observable by others)
6. Fatigue or loss of energy
7. Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
8. Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
9. Recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal ideation

**Spectrum:** Major depression → Persistent Depressive Disorder (dysthymia, lower grade but chronic) → Adjustment disorder → Grief

**Treatment:** Psychotherapy (CBT, IPT) + medication (antidepressants) most effective combined.
Mild-moderate: therapy alone often sufficient.
Moderate-severe: medication + therapy.

### Anxiety Disorders

**Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD):** Excessive worry about multiple areas of life, difficult to control.
6+ months, with 3+ physical symptoms (restlessness, fatigue, concentration problems, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance).

**Panic Disorder:** Recurrent unexpected panic attacks + ongoing worry about attacks.
Panic attack: abrupt surge of fear with 4+ physical symptoms (racing heart, sweating, shaking, shortness of breath, chest pain, dizziness, feeling of unreality, fear of dying or losing control).

**Social Anxiety:** Marked fear of social/performance situations; feared as embarrassing or humiliating.
Most common anxiety disorder. Often mistaken for shyness.

**Treatment:** CBT is first-line. Exposure therapy for specific phobias. Medications adjunct.

### Bipolar Disorder

**Type I:** At least one manic episode (with or without depressive episodes).
Mania: elevated/irritable mood, decreased need for sleep, grandiosity, racing thoughts, increased goal-directed activity, risky behavior — 7+ days, severe enough to impair function.

**Type II:** Hypomanic episodes (less severe than mania) + major depressive episodes.
Never full mania.

**Cyclothymia:** Hypomanic symptoms + depressive symptoms, not meeting full criteria, 2+ years.

**Treatment:** Mood stabilizers (lithium, valproate, lamotrigine) + therapy.
Antidepressants alone can trigger manic episodes — never use without mood stabilizer.

### PTSD (Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder)

Follows exposure to trauma (direct, witnessed, learned about, repeated exposure).
Symptoms in four clusters:

1. Intrusion: flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories
2. Avoidance: of trauma reminders
3. Negative alterations in cognition/mood: inability to recall aspects, negative beliefs, blame, negative emotions, estrangement, restricted emotion
4. Hyperarousal: hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, aggression, sleep problems, reckless behavior

1+ month duration, causes significant impairment.

**Treatment:** Trauma-focused CBT, EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing), Prolonged Exposure, CPT (Cognitive Processing Therapy).

### OCD (Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder)

**Obsessions:** Unwanted, intrusive, distressing thoughts, images, or urges.
**Compulsions:** Repetitive behaviors or mental acts to reduce obsession-related distress.
Time-consuming (>1 hour/day) or causes significant impairment.

Common themes: contamination, harm, symmetry, forbidden thoughts.
**Key distinction:** Compulsions provide temporary relief but maintain the OCD cycle.

**Treatment:** ERP (Exposure and Response Prevention) is first-line behavioral treatment.
SSRIs at high doses often helpful.

## Psychiatric medications

### Antidepressants

**SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors):** First-line for depression and anxiety.
Examples: fluoxetine (Prozac), sertraline (Zoloft), escitalopram (Lexapro), citalopram (Celexa).
Side effects: nausea, sexual dysfunction, weight gain, insomnia. Usually improve over weeks.
Onset: 2-6 weeks for mood effects. Full effect: 6-8 weeks.

**SNRIs:** Also affect norepinephrine. Examples: venlafaxine (Effexor), duloxetine (Cymbalta).
May have better pain and energy effects.

**Bupropion (Wellbutrin):** Different mechanism. Less sexual dysfunction. No weight gain. Helpful for attention. Avoid with seizure risk or eating disorders.

**Mirtazapine:** Helps sleep and appetite. Good for underweight, sleep-disturbed depression.

**Important warnings:**

- Do not stop SSRIs abruptly — taper slowly to avoid discontinuation syndrome.
- Black box warning for suicidal thoughts in children/adolescents — monitor closely when starting.
- 2-4 weeks before any effect; don't conclude "it's not working" before then.
- If no effect at 8 weeks at therapeutic dose → try different medication.

### Mood stabilizers (for bipolar)

**Lithium:** Gold standard. Effective for mania and depression. Narrow therapeutic window — requires blood monitoring. Requires adequate hydration and consistent sodium intake.

**Valproate (Depakote):** Effective for mania. Teratogenic — extreme caution in women of childbearing age.

**Lamotrigine (Lamictal):** Excellent for bipolar depression. Must titrate slowly (risk of serious rash if increased too quickly).

**Atypical antipsychotics:** Quetiapine, olanzapine, aripiprazole — FDA approved for bipolar.

### Anti-anxiety medications

**SSRIs/SNRIs:** First-line for chronic anxiety disorders. Takes weeks to work.
**Buspirone:** Non-habit-forming, non-sedating for GAD. Takes 2-4 weeks. No abuse potential.
**Benzodiazepines (BZDs):** Rapid anxiety relief. High abuse/dependence potential. Use short-term only.
Examples: alprazolam (Xanax), lorazepam (Ativan), clonazepam (Klonopin), diazepam (Valium).
Dangerous: respiratory depression with alcohol or opioids.

### Antipsychotics

**First-generation (typical):** Haloperidol, chlorpromazine. Higher extrapyramidal side effects.
**Second-generation (atypical):** Risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, aripiprazole, clozapine.
Broader use: schizophrenia, bipolar, treatment-resistant depression, agitation.
Monitor: metabolic effects (weight gain, diabetes risk, cholesterol).

## Types of therapy

| Type               | Good for                                   | What happens                                              |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CBT**            | Depression, anxiety, OCD, PTSD             | Identify/challenge thought patterns + behavioral changes  |
| **DBT**            | Borderline PD, self-harm, eating disorders | CBT + mindfulness + distress tolerance                    |
| **EMDR**           | PTSD, trauma                               | Bilateral stimulation while processing trauma memories    |
| **IPT**            | Depression, grief, relationship issues     | Focus on interpersonal relationships and life transitions |
| **ACT**            | Anxiety, chronic pain, depression          | Accept difficult feelings, commit to values-based action  |
| **Psychodynamic**  | Personality patterns, long-standing issues | Explore unconscious patterns, childhood experiences       |
| **Family therapy** | Relationship/family conflict, youth issues | Work with family system, not just individual              |

## Recognizing a mental health crisis

### Suicidal crisis

**Warning signs:**

- Talking about wanting to die or kill themselves
- Looking for ways to kill themselves (searching online, obtaining means)
- Talking about being a burden, no reason to live
- Giving away possessions
- Saying goodbye to people
- Sudden calmness after depression (may have made a decision)
- Increased alcohol or drug use
- Social withdrawal

**If someone is in crisis:**

1. Ask directly: "Are you thinking about suicide?" (Asking does NOT increase risk)
2. Stay with them
3. Remove means if possible (medications, weapons)
4. Call 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) together
5. Take to emergency room if immediate danger

**Never promise to keep suicidal thoughts secret.** Safety > confidentiality.

### Psychiatric emergency

- Psychosis (delusions, hallucinations, disorganized behavior)
- Severe mania (not sleeping for days, extremely erratic behavior)
- Active self-harm
- Substance overdose
  → Call 911 or go to emergency room.

## Getting help

**Levels of care:**

- **Self-help / apps:** Headspace, Woebot — appropriate for mild symptoms only
- **Therapy:** Most conditions — starting point for mild-moderate
- **Medication management:** Primary care or psychiatrist
- **Intensive outpatient (IOP):** More than weekly therapy, less than 24-hour care
- **Partial hospitalization (PHP):** Day program, return home at night
- **Inpatient:** 24-hour psychiatric care for safety

**Finding a therapist:**

- Psychology Today therapist finder: psychologytoday.com/us/therapists
- Open Path Collective: lower-cost therapy
- Community mental health centers: sliding scale fees
- Employer EAP (Employee Assistance Program): often 6+ free sessions

**Cost barriers:**

- Most insurance covers mental health (Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act)
- Federally Qualified Health Centers: sliding scale
- University training clinics: lower cost
- Online therapy platforms: more affordable than in-person
