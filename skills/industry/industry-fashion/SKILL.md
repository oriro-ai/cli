# Fashion Industry

## Fashion system and calendar

**Fashion weeks:** New York, London, Milan, Paris (NYFW, LFW, MFW, PFW). Twice yearly (Spring/Summer, Fall/Winter). Collections shown 6 months before retail.
**Seasons:** Traditional 2-season (SS, FW) expanded to 4-6 micro-seasons by fast fashion. Trend cycle has accelerated dramatically.
**Fast fashion:** H&M, Zara (Inditex), Shein. Cheap, frequent, disposable. Vertically integrated or near-shored supply chains. Designed to turn trends in weeks, not months.
**Luxury fashion:** LVMH, Kering, Hermès, Chanel. Heritage, craftsmanship, exclusivity. Pricing 10-100× comparable commodity items.

## Business models

### Wholesale model

Brand designs → Manufactures → Sells to retailers (department stores, boutiques).
Retailer buys at wholesale (typically 50% of retail price), sells at retail.
**Markdown problem:** Retailer misses on forecasting → markdowns → brand's image and relationship damaged.

### Direct-to-consumer (DTC)

Brand sells directly through own stores and website.
Captures full margin. Owns customer relationship and data.
Better control over brand presentation.
Higher operational complexity.

### Fast fashion vertical integration

Design → Source/manufacture → Distribute → Retail (all controlled internally or tightly coordinated).
Zara: ~2 weeks from design decision to in-store.

## Supply chain

**Tier 1:** Cut and sew factories (assembly). Most visible in media.
**Tier 2:** Fabric mills.
**Tier 3:** Yarn spinners, chemical processors, raw material farmers.
Most attention goes to Tier 1; most environmental and labor risks in Tiers 2-3.

**Manufacturing hubs:** Bangladesh, Vietnam, Cambodia (value), China (broad capabilities), Turkey/Portugal/Eastern Europe (near-shoring for Europe), Mexico/Guatemala (near-shoring for US).
**Lead times:** Full development cycle (design → retail): 9-12 months traditional. Zara: 2-4 weeks for fast-turnaround items.

## Retail math

**Initial markup (IMU):** (Retail price - Cost) / Retail price. Brand target: 60-75% IMU.
**Sell-through rate:** % of inventory sold at full price. Target: 80%+ to minimize markdown.
**Open-to-buy:** Buying budget calculation that prevents over-inventory.
**Turns:** How many times inventory sells per year. Fashion target: 4-6× per year.

## Sizing and fit

**Standard sizing:** US, UK, EU sizing systems differ and lack standardization within regions.
Vanity sizing: US sizes have shifted — today's size 6 = yesterday's size 10.
**Size inclusivity:** Extended size ranges (XS-5X). Growing market demand.
**Fit technology:** Body scanning, 3D try-on (augmented reality), personalized fit recommendations.
**Returns:** Poor fit = #1 reason for online returns (40%+). Accurate fit descriptions reduce returns significantly.

## Sustainability crisis

**Environmental impact:** Fashion is ~10% of global carbon emissions, ~20% of global wastewater, major plastic pollution source (microfibers from synthetics).
**Fast fashion waste:** US consumers throw away 81 pounds of clothing/year. 85% goes to landfill.
**Solutions:** Circular fashion (rental, resale, repair, recycling), natural and recycled fibers, nearshoring to reduce transport, reduced overproduction (demand forecasting).
**Greenwashing:** Many brand sustainability claims are marketing, not substance. Look for third-party certifications (GOTS, Bluesign, Fair Trade, B Corp).
