# Organizational Psychology

## Motivation theories

### Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)

Most empirically validated motivation framework.
Three needs must be satisfied for optimal motivation and wellbeing at work:
**Autonomy:** Sense of volition — doing work because you choose to, not because forced.
**Competence:** Feeling effective, growing, being challenged appropriately.
**Relatedness:** Feeling connected to colleagues; belonging.

**Intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation:**
Intrinsic: doing for inherent satisfaction.
Extrinsic: doing for external reward.
Key finding: Adding external rewards to intrinsically motivated activities can undermine intrinsic motivation (overjustification effect). Design rewards carefully.

### Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory

**Hygiene factors** (must be present to prevent dissatisfaction, but don't create satisfaction):
Salary, job security, company policy, working conditions, supervision quality.
**Motivators** (create genuine satisfaction and engagement):
Achievement, recognition, the work itself, responsibility, advancement, growth.
Lesson: Paying people well prevents dissatisfaction but doesn't create motivation. Meaningful work does.

## Leadership styles

**Transformational:** Inspires through vision, values, and intrinsic motivation. High impact on discretionary effort and innovation. Best for change environments.
**Transactional:** Clear expectations, contingent rewards and punishments. Effective for routine performance. Insufficient for innovation.
**Servant:** Leader's primary role is to serve and develop the team. Builds trust and long-term engagement.
**Situational leadership (Hersey & Blanchard):** Match leadership style to follower development level.

- Low competence, high commitment: Direct (tell them what to do)
- Some competence, low commitment: Coach (explain and involve)
- High competence, variable commitment: Support (encourage, listen)
- High competence, high commitment: Delegate

## Team dynamics

**Tuckman's stages:** Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning.
Most teams skip directly from Forming to Performing expectation — storming is necessary and healthy.

**Psychological safety (Edmondson/Google Project Aristotle):**
The single most important factor in team performance.
Defined as: shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking.
High psychological safety: people speak up, share ideas, admit mistakes, ask questions.
Low psychological safety: people hide problems, don't challenge bad decisions, disengage.

**Build psychological safety:**

- Model vulnerability at the top: Leaders admit mistakes and uncertainty.
- Actively invite input, especially from quieter members.
- Respond to ideas without evaluating them immediately.
- Separate idea generation from idea evaluation.
- Reward problem-reporting, not just problem-solving.

## Burnout (Maslach)

Three dimensions:

1. **Exhaustion:** Depleted energy; emotional fatigue.
2. **Cynicism/Depersonalization:** Detachment, negative attitude toward work and colleagues.
3. **Reduced efficacy:** Feeling ineffective; doubting contributions.

**Six mismatches that cause burnout:**

1. Work overload
2. Lack of control
3. Insufficient reward (not just money — recognition, meaning)
4. Breakdown of community
5. Absence of fairness
6. Value conflicts

**Recovery:** Not fixed by vacation alone. Requires addressing the mismatches.

## Feedback and performance

**SBI feedback model (Center for Creative Leadership):**
**S**ituation: "During yesterday's team meeting..."
**B**ehavior: "...when you interrupted Sarah twice..."
**I**mpact: "...I noticed the team became less likely to share ideas afterward."

Specific and descriptive, not evaluative. Makes feedback actionable.

**Radical candor (Kim Scott):**
High caring + high challenge = Radical Candor (best)
High caring + low challenge = Ruinous Empathy (avoid)
Low caring + high challenge = Obnoxious Aggression
Low caring + low challenge = Manipulative Insincerity (worst)

## Employee engagement

Gallup research: 36% of US workers engaged; 51% not engaged; 13% actively disengaged.
Actively disengaged workers cost US $450-550B annually in lost productivity.

**Q12 Engagement indicators (Gallup):**
Do you know what's expected of you at work? Do you have materials/equipment needed? Do you have opportunity to do what you do best every day? Have you received recognition in the last 7 days? Does someone at work care about your development? Do your opinions count?
