# Professional Painting

## Paint types

### Water-based (latex/acrylic)

**Interior latex:** Fast-drying (1-2 hours). Low VOC. Easy cleanup with water.
**100% acrylic:** Best exterior durability. Flexible with temperature changes.
**Primer:** Necessary on new drywall, bare wood, dark colors, stains.

### Oil-based (alkyd)

Slow-drying (24+ hours). High VOC. Cleanup with mineral spirits.
Better for: Trim, cabinets, metal, high-moisture areas.
Low-VOC alkyds: Newer waterborne alkyds eliminate most VOC disadvantages.

### Paint finishes (sheen levels)

Flat/Matte: No sheen. Hides imperfections. Not washable. Ceilings.
Eggshell/Satin: Slight sheen. Washable. Best for walls.
Semi-gloss: Higher sheen. Very washable. Trim, doors, bathrooms, kitchens.
High-gloss: Maximum sheen. Furniture, cabinets, trim accents.

## Surface preparation (most important step)

**Drywall:** Fill holes with spackling. Sand smooth. Prime new drywall.
**Wood:** Sand with 120-grit. Fill holes. Prime knots with shellac (prevents bleed-through).
**Chalky or peeling paint:** Scrape, sand, prime. Cannot paint over flaking paint.
**Glossy surfaces:** Sand lightly (scuff) or use adhesion primer before repainting.
**Mold:** Clean with TSP or bleach solution. Allow to dry completely.

## Application techniques

### Brush application

Cut-in edges first. Feather strokes into wet field paint.
Quality brushes: Synthetic for latex. Natural bristle for oil-based.
**Load brush:** Dip 1/3 of bristle length. Tap, don't wipe.

### Roller application

9" for walls, 4" for trim. Nap thickness: Smooth surfaces 3/8", textured 3/4"+.
**W or M pattern:** Roll in W shape, fill in without lifting.
Two coats almost always necessary. Wait for first coat to fully dry.

### Spray application

**Airless sprayer:** Fastest for large areas. Practice on scrap first.
Overlap 50% on each pass. Maintain 12" distance. Keep moving.
Mask everything. Overspray travels far.

## Color selection

**LRV (Light Reflectance Value):** 100 = pure white. 0 = black. Test actual paint on wall.
**Undertones:** All "white" paints have undertones — pink, yellow, green, blue.
**60-30-10 rule:** 60% dominant (walls), 30% secondary, 10% accent.
