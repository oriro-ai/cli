# Adult Learning

## Andragogy vs. pedagogy (Knowles)

**Pedagogy:** Teaching children. Teacher decides what, when, how.
**Andragogy:** Teaching adults. Adults are self-directing, bring experience, learn for immediate application.

**Andragogy's six principles:**

1. **Self-concept:** Adults see themselves as capable self-directed learners. Don't treat them like children.
2. **Experience:** Adults have rich experience base. Build on it; connect new learning to existing knowledge.
3. **Readiness:** Adults learn when they need to. Timing and relevance matter greatly.
4. **Orientation:** Adults are problem-centered, not content-centered. "How will this help me?"
5. **Motivation:** Primarily internal (career advancement, personal satisfaction, competence).
6. **Need to know:** Adults need to understand why before learning how.

## Kolb's experiential learning cycle

**Concrete Experience:** Actually doing the thing (or simulating it closely).
**Reflective Observation:** Reviewing and reflecting on the experience.
**Abstract Conceptualization:** Drawing conclusions, developing theories.
**Active Experimentation:** Testing new concepts in future experiences.

Learning occurs by cycling through all four phases. Different learners prefer entering at different phases.
**Implication for design:** Include real or simulated experiences. Build in reflection time. Provide conceptual frameworks. Create application opportunities.

## Self-directed learning

Adults can and do learn independently. The internet has supercharged this.
**Components of self-directed learning:**

- Diagnosing learning needs (what gap do I need to close?)
- Formulating goals (what will success look like?)
- Identifying resources (books, courses, people, experiences)
- Choosing strategies (how will I learn?)
- Evaluating outcomes (did I learn what I needed?)

**Supporting self-directed learning:**
Provide learning menus, not mandated sequences.
Help learners diagnose their needs (self-assessment, feedback).
Connect learners with relevant resources, not just content.

## Professional development design

**Problem of transfer:** Most training doesn't transfer to actual job performance.
**Why:** Training teaches content; the job requires application in complex, variable, social contexts.

**For transfer, include:**

- Practice in contexts closely resembling actual work situations
- Spaced practice over time (not one-day trainings)
- Feedback from peers and coaches
- Manager support and reinforcement on the job
- Opportunity to apply immediately after learning

**Performance support:** Resources people can access in the moment of need (job aids, checklists, quick reference guides). Often more valuable than training for well-defined tasks.

## Corporate training evaluation (Kirkpatrick model)

Level 1 — Reaction: Did participants like it? (Smile sheets)
Level 2 — Learning: Did they learn? (Tests, demonstrations)
Level 3 — Behavior: Did they apply it? (Observation, manager reports, 60-90 days post)
Level 4 — Results: Did it produce business outcomes? (Productivity, sales, quality metrics)

Most training evaluation stops at Level 1. Levels 3-4 are where business value is proven.
