# Agriculture

## Soil health — the foundation of all agriculture

**Soil composition:** 45% minerals, 25% water, 25% air, 5% organic matter. Healthy soil is alive.
**Soil texture:** Sand (drains fast), Silt (moderate), Clay (drains slow, holds nutrients). Loam (equal parts) = ideal.
**Soil pH:** Affects nutrient availability.
Most crops: pH 6.0-7.0.
Blueberries: pH 4.5-5.5.
Acid-loving plants: Rhododendrons, azaleas pH 4.5-6.0.
Lower pH: Add sulfur. Raise pH: Add lime (calcium carbonate).

**Soil testing:** Do before planting. Test every 2-3 years.
Measures: pH, macronutrients (N-P-K), micronutrients, organic matter content.
Cost: $15-30 through cooperative extension services.

**NPK (Nitrogen-Phosphorus-Potassium) — the macronutrients:**
N (Nitrogen): Leaf and stem growth. Deficiency: yellow leaves starting oldest.
P (Phosphorus): Root development, flowering, fruiting. Deficiency: purple/reddish leaves.
K (Potassium): Overall plant health, disease resistance, fruit quality. Deficiency: brown leaf edges.

**Building soil:** Compost (decomposed organic matter) improves all soil types. Add 2-4 inches annually.
Cover crops: Legumes fix nitrogen (clover, vetch, beans). Plant in fall; till in spring. Reduces erosion.

## Crop selection and rotation

**Match crop to climate:** USDA Hardiness Zone map. Last frost date → planting dates.
**Companion planting:** Some plants benefit others. Three Sisters: corn, beans, squash. Beans fix nitrogen; corn provides structure; squash shades soil.
**Crop rotation (4-year cycle):**
Year 1: Legumes (fix nitrogen)
Year 2: Brassicas (use nitrogen)
Year 3: Root vegetables
Year 4: Alliums or leafy greens
Prevents soil nutrient depletion, reduces disease and pest buildup.

## Irrigation

**Water timing:** Water deeply and infrequently → encourages deep root development.
Morning watering: Less evaporation, foliage dries before night (reduces disease).
**Methods:**
Drip irrigation: Delivers water directly to root zone. 30-50% water savings vs. overhead. Reduces fungal disease.
Furrow: Water flows along channels between rows. Simple, good for row crops.
Overhead sprinkler: Efficient for field crops; promotes foliar disease in gardens.
Drip + mulch: Best combination for water conservation.

## Pest and disease management (IPM — Integrated Pest Management)

**Prevention first:** Healthy soil, resistant varieties, crop rotation, proper spacing, sanitation.
**Monitoring:** Scout regularly. Identify the pest correctly before treating.
**Economic threshold:** Is the pest causing enough damage to justify treatment cost?
**Biological controls:** Beneficial insects (ladybugs, lacewings), predatory nematodes, Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) for caterpillars.
**Chemical controls:** Last resort. Choose least toxic appropriate option. Follow label exactly. Label is law.
**Resistance management:** Rotate chemical classes.

## Sustainable practices

**No-till/minimum tillage:** Preserves soil structure, organic matter, reduces erosion.
**Mulching:** 2-4 inch layer. Conserves moisture, suppresses weeds, moderates soil temperature, adds organic matter.
**Composting:** Turns kitchen/garden waste into soil amendment. Browns (carbon: dried leaves, cardboard) + Greens (nitrogen: food scraps, fresh cuttings) + moisture + turning = 3:1 C:N ratio target.
**Water harvesting:** Rain barrels, swales, terracing.
