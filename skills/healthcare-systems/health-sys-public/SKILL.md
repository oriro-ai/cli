# Public Health

## Core public health functions (Institute of Medicine)

**Assessment:** Monitor community health. Investigate hazards and health effects.
**Policy development:** Mobilize partnerships. Develop policies and plans.
**Assurance:** Enforce laws and regulations. Link people to services. Ensure competent workforce.

**10 Essential Public Health Services (updated 2020):**
Assess and monitor health, investigate and diagnose, communicate, strengthen communities, create equitable policies, enforce laws, enable access, build a diverse workforce, improve and innovate, build and maintain infrastructure.

## Epidemiology fundamentals

### Disease measurement

**Incidence rate:** New cases per population per time period. Measures risk of developing disease.
**Prevalence:** Existing cases per population at a point in time. Measures disease burden.
**Mortality rate:** Deaths per population. Crude, age-specific, cause-specific.
**CFR (Case Fatality Rate):** Deaths / Confirmed cases. Measures severity.
**R₀ (Basic Reproduction Number):** Average number infected by one case in fully susceptible population.
R₀ > 1: Epidemic grows. R₀ < 1: Epidemic shrinks. R₀ = 1: Endemic equilibrium.
COVID-19 original: ~2-3. Measles: 12-18 (highly transmissible). Flu: ~1.3.

### Disease transmission

**Direct:** Physical contact, respiratory droplets, fomites.
**Vector-borne:** Mosquitoes (malaria, dengue, Zika), ticks (Lyme), fleas (plague).
**Foodborne/waterborne:** Contaminated food or water.
**Airborne:** Particles remain suspended (TB, measles, SARS-CoV-2 aerosols).

### Outbreak investigation steps

1. Verify diagnosis
2. Establish case definition
3. Find cases, count and characterize (person-place-time)
4. Develop hypothesis
5. Test hypothesis (analytic epidemiology)
6. Implement control measures
7. Communicate findings

## Disease prevention

**Primary prevention:** Prevent disease onset. Vaccination, health promotion, environmental controls, behavior change.
**Secondary prevention:** Early detection. Screening programs catch disease before symptoms.
**Tertiary prevention:** Reduce disease burden after diagnosis. Rehabilitation, disease management.

**Immunization:** Most cost-effective public health intervention. Herd immunity threshold: Depends on R₀. Measles: 95% vaccination rate needed. Polio: 80-85%.
**Eradicated diseases:** Smallpox (1980). Rinderpest (2011). Near-eradicated: Polio, guinea worm.

## Social determinants of health

Health is 80% determined by factors outside healthcare: income, education, housing, employment, food access, social support, safety, built environment, discrimination.
**Health inequity:** Preventable, unjust differences in health between populations.
**Health equity:** When everyone has a fair opportunity to attain full health potential.
Addressing SDH requires cross-sectoral action: housing, education, employment, transportation.
