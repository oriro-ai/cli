# Communication in Negotiations

## Verbal techniques

### Labeling (FBI tactical empathy)

Name what you observe. Creates connection without agreeing.
"It seems like you're concerned about the timeline."
"It sounds like this is really important to you."
"It looks like there's something holding you back."
Label then go silent. Let the other person respond.
Labeling defuses negative emotions. Amplifies positive ones.
Never use "I feel..." (makes it about you). Always "It seems/sounds/looks like..."

### Calibrated questions

Open-ended questions that cannot be answered "yes" or "no."
Begin with: "What," "How," "Why" (use Why carefully — can sound accusatory).
"What's most important to you here?"
"How would that work?"
"What would need to happen for this to work for you?"
"How am I supposed to do that?" (instead of "no" — puts problem back on them)
The goal: Get them talking. Understand their constraints. Find their interests behind their positions.

### Mirroring

Repeat the last 1-3 words as a question. Encourages them to continue talking.
Them: "We just don't have budget for that right now."
You: "Budget right now?"
Them: [explains the real constraint, the real timeline, the real priority]
Works because it signals you're listening and creates space without challenging.

### Minimal encouragers

"I see." / "Go on." / "Tell me more." / "That makes sense."
Keep them talking. Silence is also powerful — don't rush to fill it.

## Written negotiations (email)

### The structure

**Opening:** Connect, acknowledge their position.
"Thank you for sending over the draft. I appreciate the work that went into it."
**Name the gap:** Clearly, non-combatively state the difference.
"After reviewing, there are a few areas where we see things differently."
**Label their concern:** Acknowledge what matters to them.
"I understand protecting your margins on this engagement is important."
**Make your ask:** Specific, grounded in objective criteria.
"Based on market rates for comparable services in this region, we were expecting X."
**Invite dialogue:** Don't close it off. Leave room.
"Would you be open to a call to discuss what's possible?"

### Counter-offer phrasing

Weak: "That's too low. Can you do better?"
Strong: "I appreciate the offer. Based on [specific reason: my research / our previous work / the scope involved], I was expecting closer to [specific number]. Is there flexibility there?"

### Escalation ladder

Level 1: Direct request. "I need [X] to move forward."
Level 2: Explain impact. "Without [X], I won't be able to [do Y], which means [consequence for them]."
Level 3: Reference BATNA. "I have an alternative that I'm seriously considering."
Level 4: Walk away. (Only use if real.)

## Body language in face-to-face negotiation

### What to do

**Lean in slightly** when interested or making an important point — signals engagement.
**Open posture:** Arms uncrossed, hands visible. Signals openness, honesty.
**Eye contact:** Steady but not staring. During key moments — breaks = discomfort.
**Slower speech:** Power signals. Nervousness causes people to speed up.
**Pause before answering:** Shows you're considering. Projects confidence. Don't rush to fill silence.
**Match their energy** early; then gradually calm if they're agitated (pacing then leading).

### What to avoid

**Crossed arms:** Perceived as defensive or closed. Avoid during listening.
**Looking away when making key claims:** Perceived as deceptive (rightly or not).
**Touching face, neck, or hair:** Signals anxiety or deception.
**Pointing:** Aggressive. Use open hand gestures instead.
**Checking phone/watch:** Signals this isn't your priority.

## Handling difficult moments

### When they get angry

Don't match the energy. Don't defend. Don't apologize immediately.
Label: "It seems like you're frustrated. I want to understand why."
Wait. Let them vent completely. Often anger runs out quickly when met with calm acknowledgment.

### When they give a number far outside your range

Don't counter immediately. Label and ask.
"That's quite different from what I expected. Help me understand how you got there?"
Understanding the logic behind their number helps you find the joint problem.

### When they say "final offer"

"I understand that's where you are. I need to take some time to consider this fully."
Most final offers aren't. Give them a face-saving way to move.
If truly final: Make sure you know your BATNA before you hear the final offer.
