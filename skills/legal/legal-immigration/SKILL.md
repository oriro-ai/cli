# US Immigration Basics

## Nonimmigrant (temporary) visas

### Work visas

**H-1B:** Specialty occupation (requiring at least bachelor's degree equivalent).

- Annual cap: 65,000 (plus 20,000 for US master's degree holders)
- Lottery required due to demand exceeding supply
- Employer must sponsor; filing season in April
- Duration: 3 years, extendable to 6 years; extensions possible while green card pending
- Requires maintained employment — job loss creates status risk

**L-1:** Intracompany transferee.

- L-1A: Manager or executive → path to EB-1C green card
- L-1B: Specialized knowledge
- Must have worked for foreign affiliate for 1+ year within last 3 years

**O-1:** Extraordinary ability in arts, sciences, education, business, athletics.

- High bar: must demonstrate sustained national/international acclaim
- No cap

**TN:** Canadian and Mexican citizens under USMCA.

- Available for specific professional categories (engineers, accountants, scientists, lawyers, nurses, etc.)
- Available at port of entry for Canadians; consulate for Mexicans
- Indefinitely renewable in 3-year increments

**E-1/E-2:** Treaty trader/investor for nationals of treaty countries.

- E-1: Substantial trade between US and treaty country
- E-2: Substantial investment in US business (no minimum amount, but typically $100K+)

**F-1:** Student visa.

- OPT (Optional Practical Training): Work in field of study for 12 months after graduation (36 months for STEM)
- CPT: Work authorization during studies

## Green card (permanent residence) paths

### Employment-based categories

**EB-1A:** Extraordinary ability (self-petition, no employer needed). No priority date wait for most countries.
**EB-1B:** Outstanding researcher or professor. Employer required.
**EB-1C:** Multinational manager/executive. Employer required.
**EB-2:** Advanced degree or exceptional ability. Labor certification usually required.
**EB-2 NIW:** National Interest Waiver (self-petition, no employer needed).
**EB-3:** Skilled workers, professionals, other workers. Labor certification required.
**EB-5:** Investor ($800K in targeted employment area; $1.05M otherwise; 10 full-time jobs created).

**Priority dates and backlogs:**
Certain countries (India, China) face decades-long waits in EB-2 and EB-3 due to per-country limits.
Born in other countries: typically much shorter or no wait.

**PERM Labor Certification:**
Required for most employment-based green cards.
Employer must test US labor market, prove no qualified willing US workers.
Process: 8-18+ months through Department of Labor.

### Family-based green cards

- Immediate relatives of US citizens (spouses, children under 21, parents): no annual cap → faster processing
- Preference categories: adult children, siblings → annual caps → backlogs for some countries

### Asylum

Protection for people persecuted on account of race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or social group.
File within 1 year of entry. Affirmative (not in removal proceedings) or defensive (in removal).

## Status maintenance

### Critical rules for maintaining status

**H-1B:**

- Must work for sponsoring employer
- Job change requires new H-1B petition (can file portability if new job in same specialty and I-485 pending 180+ days)
- Layoff: typically 60-day grace period to find new employer, transfer status, or leave

**F-1:**

- Maintain full-time enrollment
- Work authorization limited to specific programs
- Must maintain visa stamp for re-entry (not the same as status — can be inside US on expired visa stamp but valid status)

**All visas:**
Overstaying (remaining past authorized stay) has serious consequences:

- 180 days to 1 year overstay: 3-year bar from re-entry
- 1+ year overstay: 10-year bar from re-entry

## Naturalization (citizenship)

**Requirements:**

- 5 years as lawful permanent resident (3 years if married to US citizen)
- Continuous residence (no single trip > 6 months without re-entry permit)
- Physical presence: 30 months of 60 months
- Good moral character
- Attachment to the Constitution
- Pass civics and English tests

**Process:**

1. File Form N-400
2. Biometrics appointment
3. Interview and test
4. Oath of Allegiance ceremony
5. Receive Certificate of Naturalization

## DACA

Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals — not a legal status, but deferred removal.
Provides work authorization for 2-year renewable periods.
Requires: arrived in US before 16th birthday; continuous presence since June 15, 2007; no serious criminal history.
Status: subject to ongoing litigation. Check USCIS for current status.
