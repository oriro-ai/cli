# Addiction Psychology

## Understanding addiction

**Modern definition:** Addiction is a chronic brain disorder, not a moral failing or lack of willpower.
**Brain changes:** Repeated substance use changes dopamine pathways. The substance becomes the primary reward. Natural rewards (food, relationships) feel less rewarding. Craving intensifies; control weakens.
**Why people start:** Pain relief, stress, social pressure, curiosity, trauma, genetic predisposition.
**Why they continue:** Physical dependence (withdrawal if stop), psychological dependence (emotional need), neurological changes.

**Substance Use Disorder (DSM-5):**
Severity determined by number of criteria met (out of 11) in past 12 months:
2-3 = Mild. 4-5 = Moderate. 6+ = Severe.
Criteria include: Taking more than intended, failed attempts to cut down, cravings, interfering with obligations, continued use despite problems, tolerance, withdrawal.

## Substance categories

### Alcohol

Most widely used and most socially accepted addictive substance.
**Physical withdrawal:** Can be life-threatening (seizures, delirium tremens). Never stop heavy alcohol use abruptly without medical supervision.
**Medical detox required for:** Daily heavy use, history of seizures or DTs, prior withdrawal complications.
**Pharmacotherapy:** Naltrexone (reduces craving), acamprosate (reduces withdrawal discomfort), disulfiram (aversion therapy).

### Opioids

Prescription opioids (oxycodone, hydrocodone) and illicit (heroin, fentanyl).
Fentanyl: 50-100× more potent than morphine. Contaminates most illicit drug supply. Major cause of overdose deaths.
**Overdose signs:** Unresponsive, slow/stopped breathing, pinpoint pupils. Call 911. Administer naloxone (Narcan) if available. Give rescue breathing if trained.
**Naloxone:** Reverses opioid overdose. Available without prescription in most states. Every household should have it if anyone uses opioids.
**MOUD (Medications for Opioid Use Disorder):** Buprenorphine (Suboxone), methadone, naltrexone (Vivitrol). Reduce craving, prevent withdrawal, dramatically reduce overdose risk and increase recovery rates.

## Recovery models

### Abstinence-based (12-Step)

AA, NA: Peer support. Sponsor relationship. 12-step framework. "One day at a time."
Evidence: Cochrane review shows 12-step programs comparable to other treatments. Social support component very valuable.
Works well for: People who respond to spiritual framing and peer accountability.

### Harm reduction

Reduce harms associated with use without requiring abstinence.
**Examples:** Needle exchange programs (prevent HIV/Hepatitis C), naloxone distribution, safe consumption sites, drug checking services.
Research consistently shows harm reduction saves lives without increasing drug use.
Not incompatible with eventual abstinence — reduces barriers to engaging with services.

### Medication-assisted treatment (MAT)

Medication + counseling. Most evidence-based approach for opioid and alcohol use disorders.
**Buprenorphine:** Partial opioid agonist. Prevents withdrawal, reduces craving. Take-home doses possible. Major barrier removed: can now prescribe in primary care.
**Methadone:** Full agonist. Daily clinic dosing. Most effective but most restricted.

## Supporting someone with addiction

**Avoid:** Enabling behaviors (giving money that funds use, calling in sick for them, covering consequences). Don't shame or moralize. Don't make ultimatums you won't keep.
**Do:** Express concern with specific, observed behaviors ("I noticed...I'm worried about you"). Offer to help find treatment. Maintain the relationship while setting limits.
**Al-Anon / Nar-Anon:** Support groups for family members of people with addiction. Reduces enabling, improves your wellbeing.

## Crisis resources

SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-HELP (4357) — 24/7 treatment referral
988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: 988 (also helps with substance crisis)
Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
