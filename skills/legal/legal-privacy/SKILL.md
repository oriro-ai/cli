# Privacy Law

## GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

Applies to ANY organization that processes data of EU/EEA residents,
regardless of where the organization is located.
Fines: up to €20M or 4% of global annual revenue, whichever is higher.

### Six lawful bases for processing personal data

You must have one to process personal data:

1. **Consent:** Freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous. Withdrawable.
2. **Contract:** Processing necessary for a contract with the person.
3. **Legal obligation:** Processing required by law.
4. **Vital interests:** To protect someone's life.
5. **Public task:** Performance of a public authority function.
6. **Legitimate interests:** Your interests balanced against individual rights.

**Consent requirements:**

- Cannot be buried in terms of service
- Cannot be pre-ticked boxes
- Must be as easy to withdraw as to give
- Must be documented

### Individual rights under GDPR

- **Right to be informed:** Know what data is collected and why.
- **Right of access:** Receive a copy of their personal data (within 30 days).
- **Right to rectification:** Correct inaccurate data.
- **Right to erasure ("right to be forgotten"):** Delete data (with limitations).
- **Right to restrict processing:** Pause processing while disputing accuracy.
- **Right to data portability:** Receive data in machine-readable format.
- **Right to object:** Object to processing based on legitimate interests.
- **Rights related to automated decision-making:** Human review of automated decisions.

### Data breach notification

Must notify supervisory authority within 72 hours if breach poses risk to individuals.
If high risk: must also notify affected individuals "without undue delay."

### Privacy by Design

Build privacy protection into systems from the start, not as an afterthought.
Default settings must be privacy-friendly.

### Data Processing Agreement (DPA)

Required when sharing personal data with processors (vendors, cloud providers).
Must specify: purpose, duration, nature of processing, rights, obligations.

## CCPA / CPRA (California Consumer Privacy Act)

Applies to for-profit businesses doing business in California AND meeting any one of:

- $25M+ annual gross revenue
- Buy/sell/receive personal info of 100,000+ consumers annually
- Derive 50%+ of annual revenue from selling personal information

### Consumer rights under CCPA

- **Know:** What personal information is collected, used, disclosed, sold.
- **Delete:** Request deletion of personal information (with exceptions).
- **Opt-out of sale:** Stop the sale of their personal information.
- **Non-discrimination:** Cannot be penalized for exercising rights.
- **Correct:** Request correction of inaccurate information (CPRA addition).
- **Limit use:** Limit use of sensitive personal information (CPRA addition).

### "Do Not Sell My Personal Information"

If you sell personal information, must have a clear link on homepage.
"Selling" broadly interpreted — includes sharing for advertising purposes.

### Privacy Policy requirements

Must disclose:

- Categories of personal information collected
- Purposes for collection
- Categories of third parties information is shared with
- Consumer rights and how to exercise them
- Contact information for privacy requests

## What every website needs

### Privacy Policy

Required by: GDPR (if any EU users), CCPA (if California users), CalOPPA (all California users), most app stores, most payment processors.

Minimum content:

1. What data you collect (name, email, payment info, cookies, usage data)
2. Why you collect it (fulfilling orders, analytics, marketing)
3. How it's stored and protected
4. Who you share it with (payment processors, analytics, advertising)
5. User rights (access, deletion, opt-out)
6. How long you retain data
7. Contact information for privacy requests
8. Date of last update

### Cookie notice

GDPR requires informed consent BEFORE placing non-essential cookies.
Non-essential = analytics cookies, advertising cookies.
Essential cookies (login session, shopping cart) don't need consent.

Requirements:

- Clear description of cookie categories
- Opt-in for non-essential (not opt-out)
- Easy to reject as to accept
- Cannot deny service for rejecting non-essential cookies
- Preference should be remembered and not reset constantly

### Data breach plan

Prepare before an incident:

1. Who is notified internally first?
2. How is breach scope assessed?
3. Who notifies authorities (72-hour GDPR window)?
4. How are affected users notified?
5. How is evidence preserved?
6. Who is the outside counsel/PR contact?

## Practical checklist for web products

**Data minimization:** Only collect what you actually use.
**Encryption:** Encrypt data at rest and in transit.
**Access controls:** Only employees who need data can access it.
**Vendor review:** Review privacy practices of every vendor you send data to.
**Retention limits:** Delete data when no longer needed.
**Privacy policy:** Plain language, accurate, current.
**Response process:** Document how to handle access/deletion requests.
**Incident response plan:** Written and tested before needed.
